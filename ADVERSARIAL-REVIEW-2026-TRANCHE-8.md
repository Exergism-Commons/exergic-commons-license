# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Tranche 8

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche reviews Tajikistan, Togo, Tunisia and Turkmenistan.

## Results

### #172 Tajikistan — **UPHOLD R, CREDIT LIMITED LEGAL REFORM**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Tajikistan decriminalized the act of merely 'liking' critical social-media posts, a concrete narrowing of one expression-related offence.
- The State continued participation in the UN Committee against Torture review process in April 2026 and submitted additional information.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- Current 2025/26 reporting describes deaths in detention, near-complete impunity for widespread torture/ill-treatment, long politically sensitive sentences after unfair trials, continued imprisonment of human-rights lawyer Buzurghmehr Yorov, prosecution of journalists and continued persecution of the Pamiri minority.
- Participation in treaty review has not yet translated into an independent domestic mechanism visibly capable of restraining security, prosecution, detention and politically sensitive judicial systems.

**Adversarial finding:** the expression-law reform is a genuine but narrow improvement. The coercive pattern remains cross-institutional and the reviewed counter-evidence does not establish effective systemic remediation. `R` survives.

### #176 Togo — **UPHOLD R, MANDATORY REMEDIATION-MILESTONE REVIEW**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Togo entered a new constitutional/governance phase, creating at least the possibility of institutional change.
- UN experts themselves noted in April 2026 that the new governing phase placed the authorities in a position to deliver justice, accountability and guarantees of non-repetition. That possibility is relevant counter-evidence and should become a governance milestone.

**Evidence against downgrade at cutoff:**

- The 30 April 2026 UN Special Procedures communication describes a prolonged deterioration of fundamental freedoms/civic space and reports **systematic** practices including enforced disappearance, arbitrary detention, torture, excessive force and use of chemical agents during demonstrations, alongside restrictive legislation and surveillance technologies.
- Amnesty's current record adds severe expression restrictions, media suspensions, internet disruption, routine bans on peaceful protest and arbitrary detention of critics.
- The constitutional change also concentrated political power in the President of the Council of Ministers rather than demonstrating effective independent restraint.

**Adversarial finding:** potential reform is not equivalent to realized remediation. `R` survives at the cutoff. A later review should be mandatory once there is evidence on investigations/prosecutions, disappeared persons, repeal/narrowing of restrictive laws, independent oversight and actual non-recurrence.

Evidence/counter-evidence:
- https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TmSearch/Mandates?m=44
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/location/africa/west-and-central-africa/togo/

### #179 Tunisia — **UPHOLD R**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Tunisian courts and lawyers remain institutional actors rather than nonexistent formalities; targeted NGOs continue to challenge restrictions in court, and the legal profession has publicly resisted declining fair-trial standards.
- Tunisia retains a substantial independent civil society capable of documenting and challenging abuse, even while under increasing pressure.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- In 2026 authorities intensified the use of court-ordered suspensions, dissolution threats, administrative harassment, asset freezes and prosecutions against NGOs working on rights, migration, anti-racism, elections, corruption, media and social justice.
- Politically motivated mass trials and long prison sentences continue; arbitrary detention is used against opposition and critics.
- Tunisia withdrew its Article 34(6) declaration under the African Court protocol, cutting off direct individual/NGO access to that regional remedy from March 2026.
- A July 2026 record describes systematic racialized exclusion, discriminatory detention, collective expulsions and ill-treatment of refugees/asylum seekers alongside repression of organizations assisting them.

**Adversarial finding:** the fact that lawyers/civil society continue resistance is important, but the strongest internal judicial counterweight has itself been weakened and legal/judicial tools are central to the repression. The conduct spans executive, prosecution, judiciary/administration, migration enforcement and civic-space systems. `R` survives.

### #181 Turkmenistan — **UPHOLD R**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Turkmenistan continues formal participation in UN treaty-body processes and in April 2026 submitted follow-up information to the Committee against Torture and replies concerning economic/social rights.
- The State formally maintains a national human-rights structure.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- Current reporting describes enforced disappearances, continued imprisonment after sentence expiry, punishment of independent voices, travel bans, pervasive censorship and widespread torture/ill-treatment.
- There is **no independent national or international monitoring of penitentiary institutions** in the current record.
- UN treaty mechanisms have repeatedly focused on the need for a genuinely independent national human-rights institution and disclosure/remedy for incommunicado/disappeared prisoners.

**Adversarial finding:** international reporting compliance is not an effective domestic counter-institution. The information, security, detention, border/travel and judicial apparatus remains tightly centralized and mutually reinforcing. `R` survives.

Evidence/counter-evidence:
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/location/europe-and-central-asia/eastern-europe-and-central-asia/turkmenistan/report-turkmenistan/
- https://tbinternet.ohchr.org/
