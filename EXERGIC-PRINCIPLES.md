# Exergic Principles

This document explains the ethical model that motivates the Exergic Commons License (ECL). It is interpretive and philosophical unless a software release expressly incorporates a specific provision into its operative license terms.

## 1. Exergy as effective human capacity

In thermodynamics, exergy describes the portion of energy capable of producing useful work relative to an environment. ECL uses *exergy* as a normative analogy: the effective capacity of persons and communities to understand reality, coordinate, choose, dissent, exit, create alternatives, and transform their conditions.

The aim is not to maximize activity for its own sake. A system can be energetic while still being coercive, opaque, or destructive. Exergic ethics asks whether technological capability remains meaningfully available to the people affected by it, rather than being captured by structures that remove their agency.

## 2. Core principles

### 2.1 Agency

Technology should preserve or expand meaningful human capacity to choose, act, contest decisions, and pursue alternatives.

A system is exergically suspect when it intentionally turns people into passive objects of decisions they cannot understand, challenge, avoid, or influence.

### 2.2 Truthful understanding

Effective agency requires access to reality. Systems should not be designed primarily to manufacture false beliefs, conceal material facts, or destroy a population's ability to distinguish evidence from manipulation.

This principle does not prohibit persuasion, disagreement, rhetoric, fiction, satire, privacy, confidential information, or ordinary strategic communication. The concern is systematic deception used to remove meaningful agency.

### 2.3 Reversibility and exit

Power becomes especially dangerous when affected people cannot leave, contest, replace, or reverse the system exercising it.

Exergic systems should, where reasonably possible, preserve meaningful exit, interoperability, contestability, and institutional alternatives. Intentional irreversible capture is treated more seriously than ordinary dependency or technical lock-in.

### 2.4 Distributed capacity

Concentration of capability is not inherently unethical, but concentration that systematically deprives others of effective capacity is exergically negative.

The relevant question is not whether an actor is large or powerful, but whether its power depends on reducing the autonomy, knowledge, bargaining capacity, or future options of others.

### 2.5 Non-domination

ECL rejects uses of technology whose material function is coercive domination: persecution, unlawful political repression, torture, collective punishment, arbitrary detention, or comparable destruction of fundamental agency.

### 2.6 Knowledge and science

Knowledge creation, reproducibility, falsifiability, scientific exchange, and the ability to inspect systems are generally exergically positive because they increase the capacity to understand and alter reality.

This principle does not override legitimate privacy, safety, or confidentiality obligations.

### 2.7 Human and collective flourishing

Exergy is not purely individual. People depend on social institutions, shared knowledge, infrastructure, ecosystems, and collective coordination. A technology may increase one actor's capacity while catastrophically reducing the capacity of everyone else.

ECL therefore considers both individual agency and durable collective capacity.

## 3. Capture

"Capture" is the conversion of broadly available or potentially plural capacity into a structure controlled by an actor in a way that materially prevents affected persons or communities from understanding, contesting, leaving, or creating alternatives.

Examples can include, depending on facts and context:

- repressive surveillance infrastructure;
- opaque systems of political persecution;
- technological systems built to facilitate unlawful coercion;
- deliberate population-scale deception intended to destroy informed agency;
- infrastructures intentionally designed to make meaningful exit impossible.

Not every centralised service, proprietary platform, security system, classifier, recommendation algorithm, government database, or commercial product constitutes capture.

## 4. Exergic assessment

When evaluating a use, actor, or project, relevant questions include:

1. Does it expand or reduce meaningful agency?
2. Can affected people understand the material role of the system?
3. Can decisions be contested or corrected?
4. Is there a meaningful possibility of exit or replacement?
5. Does the system depend on systematic deception?
6. Does it facilitate coercion or persecution?
7. Are benefits and capabilities broadly available, or are they produced primarily by disabling others?
8. Is the effect reversible?
9. Is the harm incidental, or is it a primary purpose or reasonably foreseeable material effect?
10. Are less agency-destroying alternatives reasonably available?

No single question is automatically decisive.

## 5. Political neutrality of the criteria

ECL's principles are behavioral rather than partisan. A corporation, democratic government, authoritarian government, military organization, political movement, non-profit, university, or individual can be evaluated under the same substantive criteria.

A designation should not be based merely on ideology, nationality, ethnicity, religion, political party, or geopolitical alignment.

## 6. Partial knowledge and revision

Ethical judgments are made under incomplete information. ECL governance should therefore distinguish between established facts, reasonable inferences, disputed claims, and uncertainty.

Designations should be reviewable when material evidence changes. Removal from a Restricted Parties Schedule should be possible when the factual basis for restriction no longer exists.

## 7. Anti-purity principle

The objective is not to construct an infinitely expanding graph of moral contamination. Association alone is insufficient.

A collaborator of a collaborator does not become restricted merely through social proximity. Restrictions should track material participation, control, benefit, circumvention, or specifically designated status.

This is necessary both for fairness and for a license to remain knowable in practice.

## 8. Relationship to the license

The legally operative restrictions are contained in the exact ECL text and Restricted Parties Schedule incorporated by a software release.

This document explains *why* those rules exist and how future versions should be developed. It should not be used to invent restrictions that are absent from the operative license.
