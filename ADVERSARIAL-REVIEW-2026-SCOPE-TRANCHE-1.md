# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 1

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This file begins adversarial review of the `S — Restricted with defined scope` cohort. A scoped finding is not presumed correct merely because it is narrower than a whole-State designation. Review asks whether the named system still exists, whether it has been suspended/remediated, whether oversight has become effective, and whether the scope identifies the actual coercive project rather than an entire agency.

## #46 Denmark — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO SPECIFIED UDBETALING DANMARK / ATP FRAUD-PROFILING SYSTEMS**

**Original scope:** Udbetaling Danmark / ATP automated welfare fraud-control systems.

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Danish authorities disputed parts of Amnesty International's legal/technical characterization, including whether the systems amount to prohibited social scoring under the EU AI Act.
- Denmark has ordinary judicial/administrative review structures and is subject to EU data-protection/AI law. These safeguards weigh strongly against any broader Danish State designation.

**Evidence against removal:**

- Amnesty's technical investigation documents the use of dozens of data-driven fraud-control algorithms by Udbetaling Danmark/ATP to flag welfare recipients for investigation, including models using foreign affiliation/citizenship and atypical household patterns, with mass-data processing, opacity and discrimination risks.
- The authorities declined Amnesty's request for a collaborative audit/full code and data access. This adversarial pass did not locate authoritative evidence that the relevant fraud-profiling architecture has been terminated or independently cleared of the documented ECL concerns by August 2026.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives, but it must remain **system-specific**. The Restricted scope is not ATP generally, Udbetaling Danmark generally, or Danish welfare administration generally. It is the particular automated fraud/risk-profiling models and materially connected data-processing workflows where discriminatory, opaque or disproportionate surveillance effects remain unremediated.

**Removal trigger:** independently auditable retirement/redesign of the implicated models, validated non-discrimination/proportionality assessment, adequate notice/contestability and effective remediation for affected persons.

Evidence/counter-evidence:
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/eur18/8709/2024/en/
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/eur18/9419/2025/en/

## #122 Netherlands — **NARROW S TO IDENTIFIED UNREMEDIATED PROFILING SYSTEMS**

**Original scope:** automated welfare-risk profiling and discriminatory administrative systems.

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:**

- The Dutch government updated the Human Rights and Algorithms Impact Assessment (IAMA) in February 2026 and aligned it with EU AI Act fundamental-rights impact assessment requirements.
- Government institutions maintain an Algorithm Register/algorithm-governance framework, and a July 2026 technical agreement provides methods to test profiling algorithms for discrimination.
- Independent oversight is active rather than formal: in February 2026 the Justice and Security Inspectorate found serious defects in probation risk algorithms and expressly required rapid remediation or temporary suspension of a defective algorithm.

**Evidence preserving a scoped finding:**

- In May 2026 the independent State Commission against Discrimination and Racism concluded that data-driven profiling for fraud/crime detection structurally conflicts with principles of good administration and produces discrimination or unacceptable discrimination risks, calling for government to stop that form of profiling.
- The February 2026 Inspectorate review found that one probation algorithm had swapped formulas and therefore misclassified recidivism risk in roughly a quarter of recommendations, while other variables could produce discrimination.

**Adversarial finding:** the original phrase `automated welfare-risk profiling and discriminatory administrative systems` is too generic. `S` survives only for **identified government profiling/risk systems that remain deployed despite a substantiated finding of discriminatory, materially defective or non-contestable operation**. Systems that have been withdrawn, corrected and independently validated must leave the Restricted scope.

This formulation turns ECL into a remediation incentive rather than a permanent stigma against Dutch algorithmic public administration.

- https://www.staatscommissietegendiscriminatieenracisme.nl/documenten/2026/05/07/principes-voor-profilering

## #60 France — **NARROW S; REMOVE THE NICE SYSTEM, RETAIN ACTIVE NATIONAL VSA / ABUSIVE PUBLIC-ORDER PROJECTS**

**Original scope:** public-order policing and AI/video-surveillance systems.

**Counter-evidence with binding effect:**

- On 30 January 2026 the Conseil d'État held that Nice's algorithmic processing of public-street CCTV at school entrances was not authorized under existing law. The CNIL had previously reached the same conclusion. That is a concrete counter-institutional remedy and the Nice project must **not** remain Restricted merely because it once existed.

**Evidence preserving a narrower scoped finding:**

- The national Olympic-derived algorithmic video-surveillance experiment did not simply end in March 2025: the Interior Ministry stated in January 2026 that the government proposed reconducting the experiment through 31 December 2027 for defined high-risk events/locations.
- Parliament in 2026 has considered or adopted at intermediate stages additional measures to generalize/extend algorithmic video protection, including a May proposal to generalize it and July 2026 provisions in the RIPOST legislative process.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives but the scope must identify **currently authorized or materially deployed law-enforcement algorithmic video-surveillance systems whose operation satisfies ECL surveillance criteria**, plus separately evidenced abusive public-order deployments. The judicially blocked Nice system is removed from scope. Mere legislative proposals do not themselves make a project Restricted until enacted/deployed or otherwise materially implemented.

**Removal trigger:** expiry/termination of the active VSA authority without replacement, or independently enforceable safeguards that eliminate the ECL-relevant repressive/discriminatory surveillance characteristics.

## #154 Serbia — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO BIA/POLICE DIGITAL SURVEILLANCE AND SUBSTANTIATED PROTEST-REPRESSION SYSTEMS**

**Original scope:** State surveillance, protest and civic-space apparatus.

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Cellebrite halted product use in Serbia following the surveillance reporting, demonstrating that supplier intervention can materially reduce one technical capability.
- Serbian law formally regulates police/intelligence activity, and independent journalism/civil society continues to expose abuse. These factors weigh against treating the entire Serbian State or every law-enforcement function as Restricted.

**Evidence against removal:**

- Amnesty's forensic work documented systematic use of NoviSpy and mobile-forensics tools against activists/journalists and later found two BIRN journalists targeted with Pegasus in February 2025, concluding there was a strong likelihood of Serbian State actor involvement.
- Amnesty's 2026 country/enlargement reporting says journalists, protesters and civil-society organizations continued to face intimidation, harassment, unlawful surveillance and police repression through April 2026, with a continuing deterioration in rights/rule-of-law conditions.
- This pass did not identify an independent Serbian investigation producing accountability or a demonstrated cessation of the State-linked surveillance campaign.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives. Scope should be limited to **BIA/police or State-linked projects materially using spyware/mobile-forensic access for unlawful surveillance of journalists, activists or lawful opposition**, plus specifically substantiated protest-repression operations. Cellebrite itself is not a Serbian Restricted Party merely because its technology was misused; supplier conduct is assessed separately under ECL's actor/project rules.

Evidence/counter-evidence:
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/eur70/8813/2024/en/
- https://securitylab.amnesty.org/latest/2025/03/journalists-targeted-with-pegasus-spyware/
- https://www.amnesty.eu/news/serbia-amnesty-international-submission-for-the-european-union-enlargement-package-2026/
