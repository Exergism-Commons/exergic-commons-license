# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Original `N` Cohort

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This review examines all 44 State dossiers that were classified `N — No current state-level basis` in the initial 2026 adjudication. It is a falsification pass, not a rubber stamp. The question is whether new/current evidence defeats the no-basis conclusion, whether it identifies a sufficiently attributable and materially deployed ECL §5 prohibited project, or whether `N` remains the most accurate current outcome.

The review applies the operative concepts in `LICENSE` §5 strictly. A rights violation, discriminatory law, harsh punishment, migration restriction, institutional failure, security capacity or controversial policy does **not** automatically constitute an ECL prohibited use. The prohibited-use nexus must fit repressive surveillance, coercive targeting, automated/data-driven discriminatory domination, deceptive population manipulation, irreversible coercive technological capture, or circumvention. Conversely, `N` is not an ethical endorsement; it is a current evidence conclusion under that narrower license standard.

## Cross-cohort governance conclusions

This phase exposes several consistency requirements for the next global audit:

1. **The license standard is narrower than a general human-rights blacklist.** ECL §5 requires a defined prohibited-use/project nexus. General unlawful policy or severe punishment cannot silently substitute for that nexus.
2. **Capital punishment requires a consistent ECL theory.** Japan remains `U`, not `S`, because execution itself is not automatically one of §5.1–§5.5. Existing `S` dossiers that include execution/capital-punishment projects — especially Singapore — must therefore be rechecked for a concrete ECL-covered software/targeting/capture theory or narrowed.
3. **Migration and detention findings require the same discipline.** A return, detention or asylum policy becomes an ECL matter only when the relevant project materially fits unlawful targeting, repressive surveillance, automated discrimination, coercive technological capture or another operative prohibited use. Existing `S` migration/detention scopes must be checked against that exact nexus.
4. **Capability is not deployment.** Digital-state capacity, proposed legislation and retained coercive powers are not enough without material current use.
5. **Attribution remains actor/project specific.** Nauru/Australia, Solomon Islands/security partners and South Africa/non-State xenophobic actors demonstrate why cross-actor conduct cannot be collapsed into a nominal State label.
6. **Counter-institutions are substantive evidence.** Courts, ombuds institutions, NHRIs, regulators and rights-protective reforms can defeat or narrow a restriction when they produce real contrary outcomes.
