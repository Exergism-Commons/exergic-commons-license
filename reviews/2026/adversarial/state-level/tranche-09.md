## Results

### #185 United Arab Emirates — **UPHOLD R**

**Counter-evidence considered:**

- The UAE maintains formal federal courts and appellate structures and has extensive administrative/legal modernization. Formal availability of appeal is credited where it produces meaningful review.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- In the major political mass trial, the Federal Appeal Court upheld lengthy terrorism-related convictions of 53 defendants, including rights defenders, despite serious fair-trial concerns; the federal judicial system therefore forms part of, rather than a demonstrated counterweight to, the central ECL conduct in this area.
- Political opponents/activists continue to face enforced disappearance and arbitrary detention.
- The strongest current evidence directly implicates federal security, prosecution, detention and appellate judicial processes, making a whole-governing-apparatus finding more defensible than a narrow police-only designation.

**Adversarial finding:** this pass identified no independent federal counter-institution producing remedies sufficient to rebut the political-security/judicial architecture. `R` survives. Ordinary unrelated municipal/public-service functions remain conceptually protected by the proposed accountability/remediation and material-participation limits rather than by nationality or mere geography.

### #191 Venezuela — **UPHOLD R, MANDATORY TRANSITION REVIEW**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Following the January 2026 change in national leadership, authorities adopted an amnesty framework and released a significant number of prisoners.
- International humanitarian organizations gained some detention access, and the government announced symbolic/administrative reforms including a proposed transformation of El Helicoide.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- In March 2026 the UN fact-finding mission reported that the repressive State apparatus remained operational after the leadership change and that institutional structures facilitating violations had not been dismantled; politically motivated detentions continued and officials previously linked to abuses retained power.
- Amnesty/other current evidence indicates that releases/amnesty remain incomplete and opaque, while politically restrictive judicial and security structures remain in place.

**Adversarial finding:** the reform trajectory is material and could justify rapid future reclassification, but at the 11 August cutoff it has not dismantled the cross-institutional coercive architecture. `R` survives **provisionally**.

**Mandatory transition test:** review should be triggered by transparent completion of political-prisoner releases, restoration of judicial/prosecutorial independence, dismantling/reform of implicated intelligence/security structures, accountability for torture/killings and credible competitive elections.

Evidence/counter-evidence:
- Current UN fact-finding mission reporting to the Human Rights Council, March 2026.
- https://www.hrw.org/world-report/2026/country-chapters/venezuela

### #192 Viet Nam — **UPHOLD R, CREDIT DEATH-PENALTY NARROWING**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Viet Nam abolished the death penalty for eight offences, a concrete legal reduction in irreversible State coercion.
- The government participates in UN Human Rights Committee review and remains a Human Rights Council member, creating external review channels.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- The government continues to use vague Penal Code provisions against dissent and expression, with arbitrary arrests/detention and harsh prison treatment of activists.
- Current reporting describes party-controlled courts imposing long sentences on peaceful critics.
- July 2026 evidence documents transnational repression extending into Thailand, including bilateral security cooperation used to target political dissidents.

**Adversarial finding:** death-penalty reform is meaningful partial exergic progress but does not establish an independent domestic counter-institution capable of restraining political-security, prosecution, prison and transnational-repression systems. `R` survives.

Evidence/counter-evidence:
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/location/asia-and-the-pacific/south-east-asia-and-the-pacific/viet-nam/report-viet-nam/
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/asa41/1252/2026/en/

### #195 Zimbabwe — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Counter-evidence with operative judicial effect:**

- Zimbabwean courts repeatedly acquitted or released opposition/human-rights defendants in politically sensitive cases. On 3 June 2026 a Harare magistrates' court acquitted opposition activist Godfrey Karembera after seven months' detention.
- In March 2026 the High Court quashed convictions and sentences of former opposition legislators and other activists over an allegedly unsanctioned political gathering.
- In 2025 magistrates acquitted multiple human-rights activists after politically motivated arrests, and magistrates have also set aside some police 'admission of guilt' convictions.
- Zimbabwe abolished the death penalty in law and began re-sentencing death-row prisoners, demonstrating that significant rights-protective legal change can operate through State institutions.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- Police/security and executive authorities continue arbitrary arrest, surveillance, protest/meeting restrictions and harassment of journalists, opposition supporters and defenders.
- In March 2026 constitutional lawyer/opposition leader Lovemore Madhuku and activists were violently attacked in the presence of uniformed police while opposing presidential-term-extension changes.
- The Private Voluntary Organisations framework grants the executive broad powers over civil society, and coercive action around proposed constitutional changes continues.

**Adversarial finding:** the executive/security/political-enforcement pattern remains strongly ECL-relevant, but courts have repeatedly produced genuine contrary outcomes against politically motivated cases. A whole-State `R` would therefore capture institutions that are demonstrably acting as counterweights. **DOWNGRADE to `S`.**

**Proposed scope:** police/security/intelligence and executive-administrative projects materially involved in arbitrary political detention, protest/meeting repression, surveillance/harassment of opponents, coercive civil-society control and unconstitutional/abusive term-extension enforcement. Independent courts and legal/remediation functions are excluded absent separate evidence.
