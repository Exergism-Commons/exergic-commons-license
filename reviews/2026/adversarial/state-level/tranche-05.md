This tranche tests Cambodia, El Salvador, Guinea and Kuwait against counter-institutions, current remediation and attribution precision.

## Results

### #30 Cambodia — **UPHOLD R, WITH ACCOUNTABILITY/REFORM CARVE-OUT**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- OHCHR Cambodia works directly with the judiciary, Supreme Council of Magistracy and Constitutional Council on legal/judicial reform, criminal-procedure implementation, legal aid, prevention of torture and excessive detention.
- OHCHR's prison-reform work includes independent monitoring and structured dialogue with Cambodian authorities. These are genuine reform channels and must not be treated as nonexistent.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- Amnesty International's 2025/26 assessment describes Cambodia as a one-party State backed by the military with tight control over the judiciary; journalists, civil-society leaders and government critics continue to face allegedly unfounded politically motivated criminal charges.
- The same current record documents slavery, forced labour, trafficking and torture in cyber-scam compounds flourishing with the knowledge of authorities, many of which were allowed to continue operating. A June 2026 investigation found serious shortcomings in the government's claimed crackdown and victim protection.

**Adversarial finding:** unlike India/Georgia/Kazakhstan, the reform mechanisms identified here do not establish a counter-institution demonstrably capable of invalidating or independently restraining the political coercive system itself. `R` survives. However, OHCHR-supported prison/judicial reform, victim-rescue and independent accountability projects should be protected by the general Accountability and Remediation Exception.

### #52 El Salvador — **UPHOLD R**

**Counter-evidence considered:** El Salvador retains formal courts, prosecutors and other legal institutions. The government has also provided replies to some UN Special Procedures communications. Formal participation in international review is credited but is not itself remediation.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- UN Special Procedures continue to raise concerns about detention in El Salvador incompatible with safeguards against arbitrary detention, enforced disappearance, torture and inhuman detention conditions, including 2026 follow-up regarding people transferred from the United States.
- The broader state-of-emergency record alleges mass detention without ordinary warrants/safeguards, short-term enforced disappearance, torture/ill-treatment and excessive force by police and military. These practices are not confined to a single rogue unit; they are produced by the emergency legal, police, military, prosecutorial and detention architecture.

**Adversarial finding:** this pass did not identify an internal counter-institution producing remedies at sufficient scale to rebut the cross-institutional emergency system. `R` survives provisionally. Any future mass release, judicial invalidation of emergency detention practices, prosecutions for torture/disappearance or restoration of ordinary safeguards should trigger review.

- https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TmSearch/Mandates?m=47%2C280

### #69 Guinea — **UPHOLD R, REQUIRE REMEDIATION REVIEW**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Guinea adopted a new Constitution and held presidential, legislative and local elections. The Supreme Court formally reviews electoral results/challenges.
- Prosecutors have publicly announced investigations into some abductions, including cases where authorities themselves said an arrest occurred outside lawful authority. These steps are relevant and should be tested for effectiveness rather than dismissed.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- Amnesty's 2025/26 record documents suspension/dissolution of political parties and media, a continued protest ban, imprisonment of opposition figures and enforced disappearances/abductions of critics.
- In July 2026 Amnesty and Guinean/West African rights organizations reported that abductions and enforced disappearances continue with impunity, including cases involving children; several victims remain missing despite announced investigations.
- Major opposition parties were excluded/dissolved before the June 2026 legislative elections, while the president's coalition and allies secured an overwhelming majority. Electoral form therefore does not by itself demonstrate meaningful contestability or remediation of coercive political control.

**Adversarial finding:** announced investigations and constitutional elections are meaningful counter-evidence, but their demonstrated effect is insufficient at the cutoff. The coercive pattern remains central across executive, party-regulation, media, security and accountability systems. `R` survives. A future review should focus on whether disappearance investigations yield prosecutions, victims are located, opposition parties regain legal participation and protest/media restrictions are lifted in practice.

Evidence/counter-evidence:
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/location/africa/west-and-central-africa/guinea/report-guinea/
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/07/guinea-fndc-impunity/

### #90 Kuwait — **UPHOLD R**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Kuwait retains formal courts and administrative/legal mechanisms, and official nationality review processes are publicly documented.
- The government frames many nationality revocations as responses to fraud, dual citizenship or other statutory grounds. Those explanations are part of the evidentiary record and should not be silently excluded.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- Parliament remains suspended, so legislative changes are being introduced through executive decree without parliamentary oversight.
- Amnesty's April 2026 country assessment documents repression of expression/assembly, arbitrary detention of critics, systematic discrimination against the Bidun population, tens of thousands of nationality revocations, summary deportations and executions.
- In April 2026 Kuwait amended nationality law to expand revocation grounds involving the State's 'supreme interest' or national security. On 26 April authorities announced that more than 1,200 people had been stripped of nationality through a decree that did not state reasons; Amnesty reports that these decisions created a chilling effect on expression. Further 2026 official decrees and reviews continue the denaturalization programme.
- The concentration of legislative authority in the Emir/Cabinet while Parliament is suspended materially weakens the principal political counter-institution that historically constrained the executive.

**Adversarial finding:** the strongest coercive conduct is not limited to one agency; it runs through executive law-making, nationality administration, police/cybercrime enforcement, detention and discriminatory legal status. `R` survives.
