## Results

### #79 Iran — **UPHOLD R**

**Counter-evidence considered:** Iran responds to some UN communications and maintains formal judicial/legal institutions. This is relevant procedurally but does not by itself demonstrate independent remediation.

**Evidence against downgrade:** 2026 UN Special Procedures communications describe widespread arbitrary arrest and detention in the nationwide protests, often followed by enforced disappearance, torture or cruel treatment; death-penalty cases with alleged torture-tainted confessions and deficient fair-trial guarantees; systematic denial of legal representation; detention/disappearance of lawyers and defenders; and coercion against religious minorities. These allegations cut across intelligence/security, police, prosecution, judiciary and prison functions.

**Adversarial finding:** the current evidence remains cross-institutional and directly attacks mechanisms of legal accountability themselves. No counter-institution with demonstrated capacity to restrain the relevant coercive system was identified in this pass. `R` survives.

### #127 North Korea (DPRK) — **UPHOLD R**

**Counter-evidence credited:** the DPRK government claims improvement in living standards, and the 2026 Special Rapporteur notes isolated improvements in some issues.

**Evidence against downgrade:** the UN Special Rapporteur reported to the Human Rights Council in March 2026 that the overall human-rights situation over the previous decade had shown no improvement and in many respects had deteriorated. Surveillance has expanded, including facial-recognition CCTV; newer laws provide for additional death-penalty offences; movement remains severely restricted; and State economic/control measures further limit individual agency. UN and other organizations remain unable to return to the country at scale.

**Adversarial finding:** isolated improvements do not rebut the comprehensive, centralized and persistent coercive structure. `R` survives with high confidence.

Evidence/counter-evidence:
- https://seoul.ohchr.org/en/reports
- https://seoul.ohchr.org/en/speical-rapporteur-reports

### #173 Tanzania — **UPHOLD R, REQUIRE ACCOUNTABILITY MILESTONE REVIEW**

**Counter-evidence considered:** Tanzania has formal investigative and judicial institutions and public commitments to examine election-period violence. The existence of an inquiry mechanism is relevant.

**Evidence against downgrade:** UN Special Procedures material covering October 2025 into 2026 describes alleged excessive and lethal force by security forces, widespread extrajudicial killings and injuries of protesters, arbitrary arrests/detention, enforced disappearances, internet shutdowns and intimidation/harassment of journalists, defenders and civil society across the country after the October 2025 elections. The communication concerns approximately 701 identified/unidentified alleged victims and a geographically broad security response.

**Adversarial finding:** at the current cutoff, the review did not identify public prosecutions, binding institutional findings or reparations sufficient to show that the implicated security/political apparatus has actually been constrained. `R` therefore survives provisionally. A future downgrade should become mandatory if credible independent investigation produces prosecutions, public findings, compensation, institutional reform and non-recurrence safeguards.

- https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/LatestReports/CommunicationSent?page=20

### #183 Uganda — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:**

- The Uganda Human Rights Commission is a constitutional institution with court-like powers. It may order release of detained persons, compensation, injunctions and other legal remedies; its decisions can be appealed to the High Court.
- UHRC investigates complaints, visits detention facilities, monitors treaty compliance, reports to Parliament and publishes annual human-rights reports. Its 28th Annual Report was published in June 2026.
- UHRC monitored the 2026 elections and maintains an institutional mechanism for complaints and rights adjudication.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- A January 2026 UN Special Procedures communication concerned the alleged arbitrary detention of rights defender Sarah Bireete.
- Uganda's own UHRC publicly recognizes a persistent national history/pattern of arbitrary detention, torture and abuse by security organs, and its anti-torture work expressly warns that abuse tends to intensify around political contestation.

**Adversarial finding:** serious security-sector coercion remains ECL-relevant, but a constitutionally empowered rights tribunal that can order release, compensation and injunctions makes whole-State `R` too broad. **DOWNGRADE to `S`.**

**Proposed scope:** security/intelligence and law-enforcement units involved in arbitrary/incommunicado detention, torture, disappearance or political repression; election-period coercive operations; surveillance projects targeting lawful opposition/rights activity. UHRC, ordinary independent judicial/remediation functions and unrelated public services are excluded absent separate evidence.

### #157 Singapore — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Counter-evidence with legal effect:**

- Singapore courts exercise judicial review over POFMA directions and constitutional claims. In the Court of Appeal litigation concerning The Online Citizen/SDP, the court treated statements not yet judicially determined false as protected by Article 14(1)(a), analysed the constitutional limits of ministerial directions and preserved a judicial route to set such directions aside.
- The existence of an operative appeal/review mechanism means it is inaccurate to treat every Singapore governmental/legal institution as indistinguishably part of the coercive projects identified in the original adjudication.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- POFMA nevertheless gives ministers substantial information-control powers and the courts have upheld the framework's constitutionality.
- Public-order legislation, restrictions on assembly/expression, capital punishment and other coercive systems remain independently reviewable under ECL. The adversarial review does not endorse those systems.

**Adversarial finding:** the evidence supports restriction of specific information-control, public-order, detention/execution and political-enforcement systems rather than every State function. **DOWNGRADE to `S`.**

**Proposed scope:** POFMA deployments materially used for political/informational coercion; public-order/assembly enforcement suppressing protected expression; execution/capital-punishment operational systems where ECL-covered software would materially facilitate prohibited conduct; other specifically evidenced surveillance/political-control projects.

Evidence/counter-evidence:
- https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/judgments/case-briefs-by-smu/the-online-citizen-pte-ltd-v-attorney-general-and-another-appeal-and-other-matters

### #180 Türkiye — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:**

- Türkiye's Constitutional Court continues to hear individual applications and issue rights-protective judgments. In 2026 it published decisions finding violations of the right to effective judicial review and of private life in public-service dismissal cases, among other individual-rights cases.
- The European human-rights system also continues to exercise binding external judicial scrutiny; in May 2026 the ECtHR Grand Chamber found violations of legality and the prohibition of inhuman/degrading treatment in Yasak v. Türkiye.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- 2026 UN Special Procedures communications continue to allege judicial harassment, arbitrary dissolution of associations, counterterrorism criminalization of defenders and lawyers, police violence and detention/prosecution linked to peaceful human-rights/environmental activity.
- The ECtHR continues to process major detention cases including İmamoğlu and Kavala, evidencing unresolved systemic concerns rather than their disappearance.

**Adversarial finding:** the coercive pattern remains serious and spans police, prosecution, counterterrorism, detention, media/civic-space and administrative systems. However, the Turkish Constitutional Court remains a functioning internal counter-institution capable of finding rights violations. Treating it and every unrelated public institution as part of a single Restricted Party is overbroad. **DOWNGRADE to `S`.**

**Proposed scope:** politically motivated/counterterrorism detention and prosecution; protest repression; media/information blocking; coercive action against civil society/LGBTI/environmental defenders; abusive prison/detention projects and other specifically evidenced systems. Constitutional-court and independent remediation functions are excluded absent separate evidence.
