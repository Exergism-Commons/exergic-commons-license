## #189 Uzbekistan — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Original:** State apparatus Restricted.

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:**

- Uzbekistan's Parliamentary Ombudsman operates a National Preventive Mechanism and conducts large-scale monitoring of closed institutions. The Ombudsman reports more than 3,000 monitoring visits over five years, including 954 visits in 2025, with thousands of detainees interviewed individually and collectively.
- The Ombudsman's formal response measures produced documented material changes: more than UZS 8.5 billion was allocated to improve conditions in institutions after identified deficiencies.
- The Ombudsman has a right of legislative initiative and in 2026 successfully advanced amendments expanding material/legal guarantees for people in pre-trial detention. The proposal was developed from monitoring visits and detainee/family appeals and was adopted through Parliament.
- Criminal accountability is not wholly fictitious. Human Rights Watch reports that in 2025 a Tashkent court convicted three police officers of torturing suspects; two received prison terms of five years and one month and a third received a longer sentence on combined charges.
- The Constitution formally prohibits torture, requires judicial authorization for detention beyond 48 hours and recognizes judicial remedies against unlawful State action.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- Amnesty International's 2025/26 record documents politically motivated prosecutions and travel restrictions against activists/journalists, delayed reform on torture impunity, forced evictions/demolitions, systemic abuses against LGBTI people and coercive cotton-production mandates.
- The Karakalpakstan record remains particularly serious. The UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention raised grave concern over mass detention/excessive force and torture allegations; calls for an independent international investigation remain unmet.
- The Ombudsman itself has significant limits: Amnesty reports that despite repeated complaints, the Ombudsperson did not meet Dauletmurat Tazhimuratov during a prison inspection while allegations of continued abuse persisted.
- A June 2026 case involving activist Javokhir Muminov provides further current evidence of allegedly fabricated prosecution and abuse in police custody.

**Adversarial finding:** Uzbekistan contains both coercive systems satisfying ECL and institutions that produce verifiable rights-protective intervention, legislation, prison monitoring and criminal accountability. The first-pass whole-State `R` therefore overstates institutional attribution. **DOWNGRADE to `S — Restricted with defined scope`.**

**Proposed scope:**

- politically motivated prosecution/detention of activists, journalists and Karakalpak rights/political actors;
- prison/police units and officials engaged in torture or retaliatory detention;
- coercive forced-eviction/expropriation projects;
- law-enforcement systems targeting LGBTI people;
- State agricultural quota/cotton systems where land-loss or comparable coercion is materially used;
- surveillance/travel-control projects used to suppress lawful human-rights activity.

The Parliamentary Ombudsman/NPM, genuinely independent criminal investigation/prosecution of official abuse, judicial remediation and human-rights reform projects are not part of the scoped Restricted Party merely because they are State institutions.
