# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Tranche 7

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche reviews Niger, Oman, Rwanda and Saudi Arabia, with particular attention to whether formal reform or ombuds/judicial institutions actually rebut whole-State attribution.

## Results

### #125 Niger — **UPHOLD R**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Niger's 2025 national consultations included recommendations addressing violence against women and children, and the authorities announced a repatriation programme in response to migrants expelled from Algeria. These demonstrate some capacity for protective public policy.
- Formal judicial, administrative and human-rights institutions continue to exist.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- Amnesty's submission for Niger's April-May 2026 UPR describes a serious human-rights setback since the July 2023 coup, including arbitrary detention/unlawful arrests, restrictions on defenders and press freedom.
- The 2025/26 record includes dissolution of unions, detention of journalists, defenders and opposition figures, prolonged detention of the former president and his wife, and an unresolved disappearance of a former minister.
- A July 2026 Central Sahel assessment describes authoritarian governance without political opposition and systematic repression of critical voices across Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger.

**Adversarial finding:** the protective-policy examples are meaningful but do not establish an independent counter-institution capable of restraining the military/de facto governing apparatus in political-security matters. `R` survives.

### #130 Oman — **UPHOLD R**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Oman has introduced minimum employment standards for domestic workers and maintains formal courts, including appellate review; activist Talal Al-Salmani's prison term was reduced on appeal in 2025.
- Government digital services provide structured administrative procedures in nationality matters. These mechanisms are relevant but do not establish rights-protective independence on the central ECL issues.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- A 2025 nationality law allows citizenship to be revoked for vague conduct including offending Oman/the Sultan or belonging to organizations whose principles are deemed harmful to State interests.
- Authorities continued summoning, arresting and prosecuting peaceful activists/critics, while new media regulation expanded State censorship/surveillance powers.
- Migrant workers, particularly domestic workers, remain exposed to coercive kafala-based dependence with limited legal protection, and gender discrimination remains embedded in nationality/family systems.

**Adversarial finding:** the coercive systems span nationality administration, speech/media regulation, policing/prosecution and labour-migration governance under a highly centralized State structure. The appellate reduction of one sentence and labour standards do not rebut the whole-apparatus basis. `R` survives, while genuinely independent judicial/remediation activity remains protected by the proposed general carve-out.

### #145 Rwanda — **UPHOLD R, WITH ACCOUNTABILITY/OMBUDSMAN CARVE-OUT AND MANDATORY EFFECTIVENESS REVIEW**

**Counter-evidence with real institutional function:**

- Rwanda's constitutional Office of the Ombudsman receives and decides complaints concerning injustice/corruption, follows up cases referred to other organs and may request the Supreme Court to review a final judgment it finds marred by injustice.
- The Office reports concrete case-resolution activity: in January 2026 it states it resolved 105 citizen cases during a four-day outreach exercise, and further 2026 outreach resolved additional cases.
- Rwanda is participating in current UN treaty-body review, including a March 2026 Human Rights Committee list of issues.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- Domestic political contestability remains severely constrained; opposition leader Victoire Ingabire remained detained and on trial in 2026 on charges she describes as politically motivated.
- At the external level, UN reporting has continued to identify Rwandan troop presence in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and support for M23; this conduct implicates core military/executive organs, not an isolated local agency.
- The Ombudsman evidence reviewed is strong for ordinary injustice/corruption resolution but does not yet show comparable capacity to restrain politically sensitive security or external-military conduct.

**Adversarial finding:** unlike purely nominal rights bodies, Rwanda's Ombudsman has demonstrable operative functions and must be excluded from collateral restriction when acting independently for accountability/remediation. However, the domestic/external coercive basis remains sufficiently central and cross-institutional that this pass does not justify `R → S`. `R` survives provisionally.

**Mandatory future test:** if the Ombudsman/Supreme Court or other independent institutions demonstrate binding remedies in politically sensitive detention/security cases, or if Rwanda verifiably ends military support/presence connected to M23 and provides accountability for abuses, whole-State attribution should be re-opened immediately.

### #152 Saudi Arabia — **UPHOLD R, CREDIT RELEASES AND LABOUR REFORM**

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Authorities released a number of people detained for exercising freedom of expression during 2025.
- Saudi Arabia has pursued substantial labour-governance reform, including measures intended to reduce employer control and digitize contracts/payment systems. These can materially improve worker agency and should be credited where effective.

**Evidence against downgrade:**

- Releases coexist with continuing long prison terms, unfair trials and travel bans against critics/defenders.
- Saudi Arabia executed a record number of people in 2025. By 22 June 2026 it had executed 96 more people, 61 for drug-related offences, with foreign nationals heavily represented.
- Fair-trial concerns include lack of legal representation/interpretation and allegations of torture-tainted confessions in death-penalty cases.
- Centralized security, prosecution/judicial, nationality/travel-ban and execution systems remain directly involved in the strongest ECL concerns.

**Adversarial finding:** releases and labour reforms are meaningful partial improvements, but they do not dismantle the political-coercion and execution apparatus and are not produced by an independent counter-institution capable of restraining it. `R` survives. Future review should separately assess whether labour reform warrants excluding specific labour-administration projects from the restricted scope where they demonstrably increase worker exit/agency.
