# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Full `U` Cohort

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This review resolves the second-pass adversarial examination of all 45 dossiers that entered this phase as `U — Under Review`. It applies the same falsification-first method used for the `R` and `S` cohorts, but asks a different question: whether the uncertainty can now be resolved upward to a defined `S`/`R`, downward to `N`, or remains genuinely unresolved.

A rights problem, bad law, institutional failure or severe historical abuse does **not** by itself justify `S`. Escalation requires a current, materially deployed and attributable coercive project. Conversely, `N` does not mean ethical endorsement: it means the reviewed evidence does not establish a current ECL-prohibited State/project finding at the cutoff. `U` is retained where serious concerns survive but attribution, persistence, deployment or ECL-project fit remains genuinely indeterminate.

## Attribution and reversibility conclusions

This cohort review reinforces four governance rules:

1. **Capability is not deployment.** Finland, Brunei and several proposed-law cases demonstrate that ECL cannot restrict a party merely because a coercive power exists on paper.
2. **Institutional failure is not automatically an ECL prohibited project.** Belgium, Papua New Guinea, Norway and Uruguay contain severe systemic harms, but the evidence does not yet show the material project-level participation required for `S`.
3. **Counter-institutions are evidence, not decorations.** Montenegro, Malawi, New Zealand, South Korea and Portugal move to `N` because courts, ombuds institutions, prosecutors or other safeguards produced material contrary outcomes.
4. **Once prospective risk becomes deployed coercion, `S` is justified.** Slovenia, Lithuania, Argentina, Armenia and Maldives illustrate this transition.
