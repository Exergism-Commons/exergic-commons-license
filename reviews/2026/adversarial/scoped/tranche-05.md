# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 5

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche reviews Iraq, Italy, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Kenya, Kyrgyzstan, Laos and Lebanon. It continues the rule that a scoped restriction must attach to an identifiable current coercive project or apparatus, survive meaningful counter-evidence, and remain reversible when the factual basis changes. Whole-State findings already narrowed in earlier adversarial review are not silently re-expanded.

## #80 Iraq — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO ATTRIBUTABLE ENFORCED-DISAPPEARANCE / IRREGULAR-DETENTION AND REPRISAL SYSTEMS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** The UN Committee on Enforced Disappearances completed a further review of Iraq in March 2026 and issued concluding observations (`CED/C/IRQ/OAI/2`) after finding that major implementation problems remained. The record continues to identify unresolved enforced-disappearance cases, weak investigation/search implementation and risks surrounding security and armed structures. Civil-society material submitted to the Committee also documents continuing concerns involving activists, protesters and families of disappeared persons.

**Counter-evidence:** Iraq continues to engage with the Committee, has established missing-person/search mechanisms and maintains judicial and institutional structures capable in principle of issuing warrants, registering cases and investigating disappearances. Those mechanisms are relevant counter-evidence and prevent treating every Iraqi State body or security function as Restricted.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives only for **security, detention, PMF-linked or other attributable State/State-connected units and projects materially participating in enforced disappearance, secret or irregular detention, reprisals against victims/activists/families, or material obstruction of search and investigation**. Unrelated ministries, courts exercising independent remedies, ordinary public services and non-attributable armed-group conduct are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** autonomous criminalization and effective investigation of enforced disappearance, reliable search/results for pending cases, enforceable safeguards against irregular detention, credible accountability across implicated State/PMF-linked structures and demonstrable protection of victims/families from reprisals.

## #83 Italy — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE CURRENT PUBLIC-ORDER, DETENTION AND MIGRATION-EXTERNALIZATION PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Italy's Law No. 54 of 24 April 2026 converted a public-security decree containing expanded public-order, police and immigration measures. Amnesty International's June 2026 legal analysis identifies provisions it considers inconsistent with international human-rights obligations. The broader 2025/26 record also documents punitive detention conditions, disproportionate protest restrictions and migration externalization/cooperation where people may be exposed to serious abuse.

**Counter-evidence:** Italian constitutional and ordinary courts remain meaningful restraints. Courts have repeatedly reviewed migration detention and external-processing arrangements, including ordering releases and constraining implementation. Investigations and judicial remedies concerning detention and law-enforcement abuse also exist.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **implementation of public-order/preventive-policing powers materially used to punish protected peaceful protest or civil disobedience; detention practices amounting to serious ill-treatment or arbitrary confinement; and migration-externalization/return projects materially facilitating detention or return into systems with a substantiated serious-abuse risk**. The Italian State, judiciary and police are not Restricted generally.

**Removal trigger:** repeal or rights-compliant implementation of the relevant public-security measures, durable proportionality in protest policing, effective detention safeguards/remedies and migration cooperation redesigned around individualized review, non-refoulement and meaningful judicial control.

## #86 Jordan — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO EXPRESSION, ADMINISTRATIVE-DETENTION AND MEDIA-BLOCKING SYSTEMS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Current evidence includes continued imprisonment of activist Ayman Sanduka for protected expression, broad administrative detention without charge or ordinary judicial challenge, and blocking of media websites under vague national-symbol/public-order rationales. These are attributable coercive projects rather than merely restrictive laws on paper.

**Counter-evidence:** Jordan retains ordinary courts, parliament and a National Centre for Human Rights that publicly affirms constitutional protections for privacy, expression and press freedom. The existence of political representation outside the banned Muslim Brotherhood organization and rights-protective public institutions is material evidence against a whole-State finding.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **State Security Court/prosecutorial/security projects materially punishing protected expression or peaceful assembly, governor-administered detention without meaningful judicial review, cybercrime enforcement used against protected speech, and Media Commission blocking based on vague political-expression grounds**. The NCHR, ordinary remedial judicial functions, parliament and unrelated State activity are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** release/remedy in expression-only cases, effective judicial review of administrative detention, narrowing of vague cybercrime/public-order offences and durable cessation or independent review of politically motivated media blocking.

## #87 Kazakhstan — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; DEFINE POLITICAL-PROSECUTION / MEDIA / RELIGIOUS-ENFORCEMENT SCOPE**

**Prior adversarial status:** Whole-State review already downgraded Kazakhstan from `R` to `S` because functioning constitutional review and other counter-institutions made State-wide attribution overbroad.

**Evidence preserving `S`:** UN Special Procedures in 2026 raised the arrest, detention and criminal prosecution of Atajurt movement activists in connection with peaceful assembly, association and expression, as well as judicial harassment and registration denial involving an independent religious community. Separate 2026 communications concern detention/prosecution around independent media and retaliatory professional consequences for legal representation.

**Counter-evidence:** The 2026 Constitution and Constitutional Court provide operative rights safeguards, including review of excessive detention and other rights-restricting rules. State responses to UN communications also provide a contestable record rather than an irrebuttable presumption.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **political-security and prosecutorial projects materially criminalizing protected peaceful assembly/expression; politically motivated detention; media repression or retaliatory professional sanctions; and discriminatory religious enforcement**. The Constitutional Court and unrelated public functions remain expressly excluded.

**Removal trigger:** release/remedy in peaceful-expression/assembly cases, effective protection of independent media and counsel, rights-compliant religious registration/enforcement and sustained implementation of Constitutional Court safeguards.

## #88 Kenya — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE PROTEST-DISAPPEARANCE, TORTURE AND SURVEILLANCE/PUBLIC-ORDER PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** On 29 June 2026 the Kenya National Commission on Human Rights documented seven enforced disappearances connected to the 25 June protests. Six protesters arrested outside Parliament were later released after alleged secret detention and severe torture; the Commission linked the incidents to security operations and also documented arbitrary arrests, unlawful force and media-freedom violations.

**Counter-evidence:** KNCHR itself is a constitutionally mandated independent counter-institution documenting violations and demanding accountability. Other oversight, prosecutorial and judicial mechanisms remain capable of investigation and remedy. Their existence makes a whole-State designation inappropriate.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **police, DCI or other attributable security/public-order units and operations materially involved in enforced disappearance, torture, unlawful protest force, arbitrary protest detention, intimidation or coercive protest surveillance/media suppression**. KNCHR, independent oversight, courts and unrelated police/public functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** identification and prosecution/remedy for June 2026 disappearance/torture cases, traceable custody safeguards, sustained lawful protest policing, independent surveillance controls and demonstrated protection of journalists and monitors.

## #91 Kyrgyzstan — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; DEFINE SECURITY, MEDIA, TORTURE AND DISCRIMINATORY-ENFORCEMENT SCOPE**

**Prior adversarial status:** Whole-State review already downgraded Kyrgyzstan from `R` to `S` because the Ombudsman/Akyikatchy and other oversight functions provide material institutional counterweight.

**Evidence preserving `S`:** The current record includes politically motivated prosecution of journalists and activists under broad extremism/mass-unrest provisions, arbitrary-detention findings, allegations of torture in religious-enforcement cases, mandatory/controlled media regulation and restrictive religious legislation. These concerns remain project- and institution-specific rather than grounds to recapture the whole State.

**Counter-evidence:** The Ombudsman/Akyikatchy has strengthened statutory/constitutional functions, monitors detention and law enforcement, issues findings against State bodies and performs prison inspections. Courts and review procedures remain separately assessable and are not treated as collectively Restricted.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **identified security/prosecutorial projects involving politically motivated detention or torture; coercive media/civic-control enforcement; and discriminatory or vague extremism/religious enforcement materially suppressing protected conduct**. The Ombudsman and independent remedial functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** effective remedy for arbitrary/political detention, investigation and prosecution of torture, durable restoration of independent media/civic space, rights-compliant religious enforcement and demonstrated implementation of Ombudsman recommendations.

## #92 Laos — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE POLITICAL-DISAPPEARANCE, TRANSFER AND RELIGIOUS-PERSECUTION PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** In February 2026 government critic Sisay Luangmonda (Bao Mor Khaen) disappeared after a reported arrest by soldiers; his body was later found and Human Rights Watch reported that authorities had not provided his family information or initiated an adequate investigation at that point. The wider record includes restrictions on expression/assembly, persecution of Christians, transfer of Myanmar nationals to the Myanmar military despite serious-risk concerns and unresolved attacks/disappearances of critics. UN Special Procedures have also raised possible transnational repression against an exiled Lao defender, while carefully treating State linkage as an allegation requiring proof.

**Counter-evidence:** Laos has participated in the Universal Periodic Review and accepted recommendations concerning investigation of enforced disappearances. Attribution is not sufficiently established for every attack on critics, and alleged transnational conduct must not be treated as proven merely because it is plausible.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **attributable political-security detention/disappearance projects, failure or obstruction in investigating disappearances where State custody/agents are materially implicated, coercive cross-border transfer into a substantiated serious-abuse risk, and specifically evidenced discriminatory religious-persecution enforcement**. Unproven proxy conduct and unrelated public functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** independent investigation and accountability in disappearance/death cases, protection of critics and defenders, non-refoulement-compliant transfer practice, religious-freedom remediation and credible clarification of alleged transnational-repression cases.

## #94 Lebanon — **DOWNGRADE S → U; CURRENT PERSISTENCE INSUFFICIENT FOR A STABLE ACTIVE RESTRICTION**

**Prior scope:** security, prosecution, extradition and civic-space apparatus.

**Evidence for the former `S`:** The 2025 record includes repeated summonses of activists and journalists under criminal defamation or vague offences, plus the extradition of Abdul Rahman Al-Qaradawi to the UAE followed by enforced disappearance. Those events remain serious historical and attribution evidence.

**Material change / counter-evidence:** In 2026 parliamentary committees advanced a media-law proposal containing rights-protective elements and continued work on other human-rights reforms. Judicial and administrative remedies also produced meaningful counter-evidence in the broader record. At the August 2026 cutoff, the evidence reviewed here does not establish enough fresh recurrence to treat the prior speech/extradition pattern as a stable, currently operating prohibited project.

**Adversarial finding:** ECL's reversibility rule requires **DOWNGRADE `S → U — Under Review`**. Review remains focused on **recurrence of speech-based criminal summons/prosecution, implementation of the pending media-law framework, accountability/remedy for abusive extradition, and any future extradition/security cooperation materially exposing critics to disappearance or comparable abuse**.

**Re-escalation trigger:** renewed systematic criminalization of protected journalism/activism, coercive media-law implementation or another attributable extradition/transfer into a substantiated disappearance/torture risk. **Removal to `N` trigger:** durable decriminalization/protection of expression, effective remedies/accountability and rights-compliant extradition safeguards.
