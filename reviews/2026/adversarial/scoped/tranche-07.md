# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 7

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche reviews Nigeria, North Macedonia, Pakistan, Palestine, Panama, Paraguay, Peru and the Philippines. It continues falsification-first review: current restrictions must attach to identifiable coercive projects, must survive counter-evidence, and must be narrowed when a prior scope captures institutions or programmes not currently shown to participate in ECL-prohibited conduct.

## #126 Nigeria — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO IDENTIFIED POLICE/SECURITY DETENTION, TORTURE, DISAPPEARANCE AND UNLAWFUL-FORCE PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty International's February 2026 investigation of the Imo State Police Anti-Kidnapping Unit (`Tiger Base`) documented prolonged arbitrary detention, torture, extortion, enforced disappearances, deaths in custody and degrading conditions. Amnesty's 2025/26 country record also documents unlawful force against peaceful protesters and civilian harm from security operations.

**Counter-evidence:** Nigeria's National Human Rights Commission remains an active quasi-judicial counter-institution, publishes human-rights dashboards, issues civic-space guidance to police and public authorities and, as Nigeria's National Preventive Mechanism, publicly works on torture prevention and detention oversight.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **identified police, military or other public-security units/facilities materially involved in torture, enforced disappearance, arbitrary or secret detention, unlawful lethal force, serious custody abuse or material obstruction of investigation/accountability**. The finding is not a generic restriction of the Nigeria Police Force, armed forces, federal/state governments or ordinary public services. NHRC, courts and independent remedial/oversight functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** closure or compliant reform of abusive facilities such as Tiger Base, traceable custody and access-to-counsel safeguards, credible prosecutions/remedies for torture/disappearance/unlawful killings, and sustained rights-compliant protest/security operations.

## #128 North Macedonia — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO CURRENT BORDER-PUSHBACK AND SPECIFIC DETENTION/ILL-TREATMENT PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's current 2025/26 record reports continuing unlawful pushbacks from North Macedonia to Greece. The most recent CPT baseline also found credible allegations of police ill-treatment and longstanding detention deficiencies. These are attributable projects rather than merely restrictive rules on paper.

**Counter-evidence:** North Macedonia is implementing an EU/Council of Europe penitentiary reform programme through December 2026, including external oversight, healthcare, rehabilitation, reporting rules and mandatory training on prevention of ill-treatment. A specialised prosecutorial unit investigates offences by police/prison-police personnel.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives only for **border-control operations materially carrying out unlawful summary pushbacks or associated violence, and identified police/prison-detention practices involving ill-treatment or serious accountability failures**. The wider police, prison administration, prosecutors, courts and reform/oversight projects are excluded unless independently implicated.

**Removal trigger:** independently verified cessation of unlawful pushbacks, effective access to asylum/shelter, credible investigation/remedy for police ill-treatment and demonstrated operation of the external-oversight/reform framework.

## #131 Pakistan — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; DEFINE SECURITY, DISAPPEARANCE, PREVENTIVE-DETENTION, CENSORSHIP AND COERCIVE-SURVEILLANCE SCOPE**

**Prior adversarial status:** Whole-State review downgraded Pakistan from `R` to `S` because federalism, parliamentary oversight and civilian/remedial institutions made a single whole-State designation overbroad.

**Evidence preserving `S`:** The 2025/26 record continues to document enforced disappearance, arbitrary detention, counterterrorism misuse, protest repression and censorship, particularly in Balochistan. In August 2026 the government also expanded foreign-media reporting controls to Pakistani staff/associates, requiring registration and permission for reporting outside major cities, reinforcing that current censorship/control projects remain active.

**Counter-evidence:** Pakistan remains a federal, multi-party system with parliamentary human-rights oversight, courts and civilian bodies capable of contesting executive/security action. Those institutions do not erase abuse but prevent recursive attribution to the entire State.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **military/intelligence/security and counterterrorism projects materially involved in enforced disappearance, torture, extrajudicial killing, arbitrary/preventive detention or repression of peaceful protest; censorship/media-control projects materially suppressing independent reporting; and specifically evidenced coercive-surveillance systems**. Parliamentary oversight, courts/remedial functions and unrelated civilian services remain excluded.

**Removal trigger:** effective disappearance/torture investigations and remedies, restoration of civic/media freedoms, transparent and rights-compliant counterterrorism/preventive-detention practice, repeal/non-use of coercive media controls and effective civilian/parliamentary control over implicated security structures.

## #133 Palestine — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE PA WEST-BANK SECURITY/DETENTION SCOPE AND KEEP HAMAS SEPARATE**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** On 22 July 2026 OHCHR stated that Palestinian authorities continued arbitrary/unlawful detention in the West Bank, including journalists, dissidents and political activists, with some detainees held despite court release orders. OHCHR also documented reports of torture and ill-treatment including severe beatings, stress positions, whippings and prolonged solitary confinement.

**Counter-evidence:** The Palestinian authorities continue engagement with OHCHR and the UN Committee against Torture and have undertaken legal/institutional commitments including work toward a National Preventive Mechanism. Palestinian courts have issued release orders that security bodies are obliged to respect.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **Palestinian Authority security/intelligence, detention and prosecutorial projects in the West Bank materially involved in arbitrary detention, torture/ill-treatment, detention contrary to judicial release orders or repression of protected journalism/political dissent**. Courts ordering release, torture-prevention/remedial functions and unrelated civilian services are excluded. **Hamas and other de facto/non-State armed structures are not collapsed into this State dossier; they require separate actor/project designations and attribution.**

**Removal trigger:** compliance with judicial release orders, independent torture investigations/prosecutions, operational National Preventive Mechanism, durable cessation of political/journalistic arbitrary detention and transparent detention safeguards.

## #134 Panama — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO INDIGENOUS PROTEST-POLICING / TERRITORIAL-ENFORCEMENT PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** UN Special Procedures have recorded allegations concerning serious and systematic violations against Indigenous peoples in the context of protests from April 2025 onward, including excessive force, arbitrary detention, torture, sexual violence, criminalisation of Indigenous leaders and territorial dispossession/FPIC failures. These allegations remain sufficiently current and attributable to justify continued scoped review/restriction.

**Counter-evidence / material narrowing:** In 2026 the Government introduced legislation expressly aimed at protecting peaceful protesters, the Defensoría del Pueblo actively monitored demonstrations, and Panama/OAS projects strengthened assistance and rights protection for people in mobility in Darién. Current evidence therefore does **not** justify retaining a generic migration-detention restriction by inertia.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **identified police/security operations materially involving unlawful force, arbitrary detention, torture or criminalisation of peaceful Indigenous protest, and territorial/development enforcement materially defeating Indigenous consultation/FPIC or using coercive dispossession**. The prior generic migration-detention component is removed from active scope absent new project-specific evidence. Defensoría, courts, humanitarian/protection programmes and unrelated policing are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** independent investigation/remedy for 2025 protest abuses, sustained rights-compliant protest policing, effective Indigenous consultation/FPIC and land-remedy mechanisms, and non-recurrence of coercive territorial enforcement.

## #136 Paraguay — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE INDIGENOUS-LAND, DEFENDER-CRIMINALISATION AND SPECIFIC PROTEST/CIVIC-CONTROL SCOPE**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's 2025/26 country assessment continues to report repression of social protest, criminalisation of defenders, structural discrimination and setbacks in Indigenous rights. Current 2026 reporting also documents unresolved Avá-Guaraní territorial/displacement claims and criminalisation concerns affecting Ayoreo community leaders.

**Counter-evidence:** Paraguay retains courts, public institutions, elections and civil-society organisations capable of contesting State action. The record does not justify restriction of the State or police generically, and private agribusiness conduct cannot be imputed to government without attribution.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **specific police/prosecutorial/public-administration projects materially repressing peaceful protest or criminalising protected defender activity, and Indigenous-land/eviction or development-enforcement projects materially involving coercive displacement, discriminatory denial of recognised rights or serious FPIC failures**. Unrelated public services, independent judicial/remedial functions and non-attributable private conduct are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** durable protection of peaceful assembly/defenders, dismissal/remedy of retaliatory prosecutions, effective Indigenous land recognition/restitution and FPIC safeguards, and accountability for unlawful police/public-authority coercion.

## #137 Peru — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE PUBLIC-ORDER, CIVIC-CONTROL AND IMPUNITY-ENABLING PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's April 2026 assessment reports unnecessary/disproportionate force against demonstrators, continuing lack of justice for the 2022–23 protest killings, attacks on journalists/defenders and legislation restricting civil society. On 20 July 2026 Peru enacted Law 32735 expanding military/police jurisdiction over alleged offences by security personnel, creating a fresh risk that serious human-rights cases involving civilian victims may be removed from ordinary courts.

**Counter-evidence:** Peru retains ordinary courts, prosecutors, electoral institutions and civil society that continue to litigate, investigate and contest these measures. International and domestic judicial review remain meaningful counterweights even as institutional independence is under pressure.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **police/military public-order projects materially using unlawful/disproportionate force against peaceful demonstrators; civic-control projects materially suppressing protected NGOs, journalists or defenders; and legal/jurisdictional projects materially obstructing independent investigation, ordinary-court accountability or remedy for serious security-force human-rights violations**. The Peruvian State, military/police as institutions, courts and unrelated public functions are not Restricted generally.

**Removal trigger:** credible accountability for protest killings/abuse, durable rights-compliant crowd policing, repeal/narrowing or rights-compliant interpretation of impunity-enabling jurisdictional laws, protection of civil society/journalism and restored independent remedies.

## #138 Philippines — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO CURRENT ANTI-DRUG KILLING/ARBITRARY-ARREST AND SPECIFIC SECURITY-REPRESSION PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Human Rights Watch reported in June 2026 that extrajudicial killings and arbitrary arrests continue under the anti-drug campaign and that President Marcos has not rescinded the Duterte-era policy architecture. Independent monitoring counted 1,273 drug-war killings since Marcos took office. Current evidence also documents warrantless/arbitrary drug arrests and weak domestic accountability.

**Counter-evidence:** The Philippines arrested and transferred former President Rodrigo Duterte to the ICC in 2025, and in May 2026 launched an independent civilian Truth and Reconciliation Commission led by a former ICC judge to document drug-war killings, make referrals and support accountability. These are substantial exergic/accountability institutions and weigh strongly against any whole-State designation.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **police, anti-drug and materially participating security projects involved in extrajudicial killing, arbitrary/warrantless detention, planted/fabricated evidence, torture/ill-treatment or material obstruction of accountability; and separately evidenced counterterrorism/security projects materially criminalising protected activism or causing enforced disappearance**. Generic online criticism regulation, protest policing or the Philippine State are not Restricted absent project-specific evidence. ICC cooperation, the Truth Commission, courts and independent accountability functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** formal termination of abusive drug-war policies, sustained elimination of extrajudicial killings/arbitrary arrests, effective prosecution/remedy, preservation and production of body-camera/custody evidence, and effective protection against abusive counterterrorism/red-tagging/disappearance projects.
