# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 3

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche reviews Central African Republic, Colombia, Côte d’Ivoire, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, Equatorial Guinea and Ethiopia. Particular attention is given to conflict attribution: abuses by non-State armed groups must not be used to inflate a State designation.

## #33 Central African Republic — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO GOVERNMENT / STATE-ALIGNED SECURITY OPERATIONS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Current 2025/26 reporting documents unlawful attacks and killings by government forces and allies, alongside armed-group abuses. Freedom-of-expression restrictions, prison overcrowding and failures to execute accountability warrants remain material concerns.

**Strong counter-evidence:** The hybrid Special Criminal Court (SCC) remains a genuine accountability institution with ongoing investigations/trials. In June 2026 it opened the trial of former president François Bozizé and co-defendants for crimes against humanity. Its work is materially exergic even though funding and warrant-enforcement failures limit effectiveness.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives only for **identified government forces, State-aligned/contracted armed actors and security operations materially connected to unlawful attacks, killings, disappearance, torture or coercive repression**, and State projects materially enabling such conduct. The SCC, independent investigators/prosecutors and victim-remediation projects are expressly excluded.

**Review trigger:** effective SCC/national prosecutions, execution of warrants, termination of abusive allied-force relationships and verified security-sector reform.

## #37 Colombia — **NARROW S TO MILITARY-JUSTICE IMPUNITY MECHANISMS AND SPECIFIC SECURITY-FORCE ABUSE CASES**

**Prior scope:** police, military-justice and conflict-security apparatus.

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's November 2025 research analyzed 398 jurisdictional conflicts and concluded that Military Criminal Justice intervention continues to obstruct ordinary investigation of alleged human-rights violations/crimes by National Police and Armed Forces, including protest-force cases. In May 2026 the UN Committee against Torture assessed prior recommendations on conflict violence and detention as only partially implemented.

**Counter-evidence and exergic institutions:** Colombia ratified OPCAT through Act 2371 of 2024, a step the CAT expressly treated as direct implementation of its detention-monitoring recommendation. The State maintains public policies to dismantle criminal organizations and protect defenders, judicial institutions, ordinary prosecutors and transitional-justice mechanisms. Much current violence is caused by non-State armed groups and cannot be attributed to the State.

**Adversarial finding:** generic `conflict-security` attribution is overbroad. `S` is narrowed to **Military Criminal Justice jurisdictional interventions materially obstructing independent accountability for alleged police/armed-forces abuses, and specifically substantiated security-force units/operations involving unlawful force, torture, disappearance or related impunity**.

**Review trigger:** consistent ordinary-jurisdiction handling of human-rights cases, implementation of OPCAT monitoring, effective prosecutions/remedies and demonstrable end to military-jurisdiction obstruction.

## #40 Côte d’Ivoire — **NARROW S TO THE 2025 ELECTION-PROTEST CRACKDOWN AND CONTINUING DETENTION/PROSECUTION**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Hundreds were arrested during banned opposition protests before the October 2025 presidential election. As of March 2026, people including a pregnant woman remained in pretrial detention despite alleged non-involvement in protests, while others had been convicted after proceedings in which access to counsel was reportedly denied.

**Counter-evidence:** Some large opposition demonstrations in 2025 proceeded peacefully, showing that repression was not mechanically applied to every political assembly. Ordinary courts remain available.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` should not attach to Ivorian police/judiciary generically. It is limited to **the October 2025 election/protest crackdown, units and detention/prosecution processes materially responsible for arbitrary arrest, excessive force or unfair prosecution, and any renewed materially comparable political-repression operation**.

**Removal trigger:** release/remedy or fair independent review of remaining detainees/convictions, investigation of abuse and demonstrated non-recurrence in later elections/protests.

Sources:
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/03/cote-divoire-women-detained-without-cause/

## #45 Democratic Republic of the Congo — **NARROW S TO FARDC AND STATE-BACKED PROXY PROJECTS; EXCLUDE M23/ADF ABUSES FROM STATE ATTRIBUTION**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** 2025 research documented abuses involving FARDC and Wazalendo alongside M23. In June 2026 Amnesty documented killings, torture, looting and sexual slavery by CMC-FDP, a Wazalendo armed group backed by FARDC, and called on the Congolese government to cease support.

**Critical attribution correction:** Major current atrocities by M23 and ADF are attributable to those armed groups (and, where evidenced, their external sponsors), **not to the DRC State**. FARDC's failure to prevent ADF attacks is not equivalent to perpetrating the ADF crimes.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` is narrowed to **FARDC units and State-backed/armed Wazalendo or other proxy projects where material support, command, collaboration or knowledge connects the State to substantiated killings, torture, sexual violence, looting, child recruitment or other ECL-prohibited conduct**. M23/ADF conduct is separately attributed.

**Removal trigger:** end to support for abusive proxies, effective command/accountability mechanisms, prosecutions/remediation and verifiable civilian-protection safeguards.

Sources:
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/06/drc-authorities-must-end-support-for-armed-group-suspected-of-war-crimes/
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/afr62/0145/2025/en/
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/05/drc-rampant-adf-abuses-against-civilians-war-crimes-which-the-world-must-not-continue-to-ignore-new-report/

## #49 Dominican Republic — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE DGM / BORDER-MIGRATION ENFORCEMENT PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** During 2025 at least 265,215 Haitians were expelled, including women and children, amid reported racial profiling, arbitrary detention, excessive force, cruel/inhuman treatment, collective expulsions and lack of effective asylum access. The scale increased further in 2026: official DGM figures record 99,268 deportations through March and 126,790 through April, overwhelmingly Haitian nationals.

**Counter-evidence:** DGM states that operations are conducted under Law 285-04 and strict legal/human-rights protocols. The existence of formal processing centres and rules is credited but does not by itself rebut documented discriminatory/coercive practices.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **DGM, Rapid Reaction Agents, CESFRONT, military/police and detention/border projects materially participating in collective or discriminatory deportation, racial profiling, arbitrary detention, abusive force or denial of protection procedures**. The Dominican State and unrelated public functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** effective individualized screening/asylum access, independent detention oversight, end to racial profiling/collective expulsion and credible accountability for abuse.

## #50 Ecuador — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE MILITARY / INTELLIGENCE / EMERGENCY-SECURITY PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** A May 2026 UN communication concerns alleged excessive force, arbitrary detention, torture, intimidation, enforced disappearance, property destruction and displacement risk during the military operation `Exterminio Total` in San Martín in March 2026. A March 2026 communication raises concerns about intelligence legislation, terrorism offences and repeated states of exception, including privacy, independent oversight, life/liberty, assembly, fair-trial and torture risks. A 2025 communication answered in 2026 documented alleged civic-space repression and abusive force/detention.

**Counter-evidence:** Ecuador replied substantively to the UN communications in May and July 2026, and constitutional/electoral judicial institutions continue to operate. These are relevant review mechanisms but do not yet establish remediation of the specified projects.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **identified military public-security operations (including `Exterminio Total` where allegations are substantiated), intelligence/surveillance systems lacking adequate independent safeguards, and emergency/counterterrorism enforcement projects materially producing arbitrary detention, torture, disappearance or repression of protected civic activity**.

**Review trigger:** independent investigation of San Martín, judicial review/remediation of intelligence/emergency powers, accountability and demonstrated safeguards against recurrence.

## #53 Equatorial Guinea — **UPHOLD S; CREDIT MASS PARDONS AND ADD SPECIFIC THIRD-COUNTRY DETENTION/REFOULEMENT PROJECT**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Human-rights defender Joaquín Elo Ayeto remained unaccounted for after transfer to Oveng Azem prison, while Anacleto Micha Ndong Nlang remained incommunicado despite a UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention opinion calling for release. In April 2026 UN experts also raised urgent concerns about nine persons transferred from the United States and detained in Malabo facing potential refoulement to places where they risk severe harm.

**Counter-evidence with material effect:** In June 2025 the President pardoned 476 prisoners, including 37 Annobón protesters whose detention the WGAD had found arbitrary. This is large-scale concrete remediation and must materially narrow the ECL assessment.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **identified security/detention/political-repression projects involving arbitrary/incommunicado detention, disappearance or torture, and the specific third-country migrant detention/transfer/refoulement project where non-refoulement and due-process safeguards are not met**. Pardoned/released persons leave the active detention scope.

**Removal trigger:** implementation of WGAD opinions, disclosure/release/remedy for disappeared/incommunicado defenders, independent detention oversight and lawful individualized protection screening for transferred migrants.

Sources:
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/location/africa/west-and-central-africa/equatorial-guinea/report-equatorial-guinea/
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/afr28/1119/2026/en/

## #57 Ethiopia — **NARROW S TO UNREMEDIATED FORCED-EVICTION AND MEDIA/CIVIC-REPRESSION PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Authorities carried out mass forced evictions under the Corridor Development Project across many cities with inadequate notice/consultation and harassment of people challenging the project. Journalists faced arbitrary/incommunicado detention, surveillance, accreditation revocation and media licence closures; Amnesty reported an intensified press crackdown ahead of the June 2026 election.

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:** Addis Ababa officials temporarily suspended part of the Corridor Development Project in October 2025 and promised improved consultation. Journalist Million Beyene, abducted on 15 April 2026, was released by Ethiopian authorities on 28 April. These are real remediation signals and must narrow the active scope.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` is narrowed to **Corridor Development/other forced-eviction implementations that remain unremediated and lack meaningful consultation/compensation, and identified security/media-regulatory/surveillance projects materially involved in arbitrary journalist/defender detention, enforced disappearance, unlawful surveillance or coercive media closure**. Remediated/suspended project segments leave the active scope unless abuses recur.

**Removal trigger:** permanent rights-compliant redesign of evictions, compensation/consultation, end to incommunicado journalist detention, independent accountability and durable media/civic-space restoration.

Sources:
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/location/africa/east-africa-the-horn-and-great-lakes/ethiopia/report-ethiopia/
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/05/ethiopian-authorities-have-intensified-their-crackdown-on-press-freedom/
