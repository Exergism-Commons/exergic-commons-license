# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 10

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This final scoped-`S` tranche reviews Georgia, India, Singapore and South Sudan. All four were initially classified `R` and were later downgraded to `S` during whole-State adversarial review. This pass therefore tests both directions: whether current evidence requires re-escalation to `R`, or whether remediation/counter-institutions and project cessation justify further narrowing or downgrade. A serious current abuse record is not enough for whole-State attribution where independent institutions still produce material rights-protective effects.

## #63 Georgia — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; DEFINE EXECUTIVE/POLICE, CIVIC-SPACE, MEDIA AND POLITICAL-ENFORCEMENT PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** The Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights' June 2026 memorandum found continuing accountability failures for disproportionate force against protesters and mounting restrictions on peaceful assembly, association and expression. It identified coercive foreign-influence/grant controls, proceedings against civil-society organisations, frozen accounts, restrictions on foreign-funded media and legal actions affecting opposition actors. These are current, institutionally attributable projects rather than historical concerns.

**Counter-evidence / reason not to re-escalate to `R`:** The Public Defender continues to challenge assembly restrictions before the Constitutional Court and to litigate rights questions. The Constitutional Court has accepted and granted rights-protective claims, five police officers were criminally charged over disproportionate force, and legislation requiring visible police identification was proposed. These mechanisms remain materially distinct from the coercive executive/police project.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **executive/police public-order projects materially using unlawful force, arbitrary detention or coercive penalties against protected peaceful assembly; prosecutorial/administrative projects materially targeting opposition or protected civic activity; foreign-influence/grant/registration systems materially suppressing independent civil society; and media/information-control projects materially coercing protected journalism or expression**. The Public Defender, Constitutional Court, genuinely independent prosecution/accountability and unrelated public services remain excluded.

**Removal trigger:** effective accountability for protest violence, repeal or rights-compliant narrowing of coercive civic-space/funding controls, restoration of independent media/political activity and sustained implementation of Constitutional Court/Public Defender safeguards. **Re-escalation to `R`** requires evidence that rights-protective courts/oversight themselves become materially captured or ineffective across the relevant coercive system.

## #77 India — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; NARROW TO CURRENT SECURITY/COUNTERTERRORISM, DETENTION, PUBLIC-ORDER AND DIGITAL-CONTROL PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** UN Special Procedures in March-April 2026 again raised concerns about prolonged UAPA detention, the compatibility of UAPA and AFSPA with international human-rights obligations and the continuing detention of individual defenders. In July 2026, peaceful protesters in New Delhi were reportedly subjected to unnecessary/excessive police force and restrictions including mobile-internet disruption. Current digital-control rules and takedown practices also materially expand executive control over online content.

**Counter-evidence / narrowing:** India's federal constitutional structure, Supreme Court and High Courts retain substantial review powers. Article 32 gives the Supreme Court direct jurisdiction to enforce Fundamental Rights through habeas corpus and other writs. Existing Supreme Court safeguards against punitive demolition remain judicial counter-evidence, and the present review did not identify sufficiently current project-specific evidence to retain `punitive demolition` as an independent active 2026 scope merely from the older pattern.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **identified Union/state counterterrorism/security projects materially producing arbitrary or prolonged detention, disappearance, torture/ill-treatment or repression of protected civic activity; police/public-order operations materially using unlawful force or communications shutdowns against peaceful assembly; and digital censorship/takedown or surveillance projects materially coercing protected expression without meaningful safeguards**. `Punitive demolition` is removed from active scope at this cutoff and becomes a re-expansion trigger if attributable authorities materially evade or violate judicial safeguards. Courts, independent remedies, unrelated state governments/agencies and ordinary public services are excluded absent separate evidence.

**Removal trigger:** rights-compliant reform/non-use of UAPA/AFSPA and coercive digital powers against protected activity, release/remedy for unlawful prolonged detention, effective investigation of protest-policing abuse and durable judicially enforceable due-process safeguards.

## #157 Singapore — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; DEFINE POFMA/INFORMATION-CONTROL, PUBLIC-ORDER AND CAPITAL-PUNISHMENT PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** In March 2026 Singapore brought the first criminal prosecution for communicating alleged false statements under POFMA against government critic Jay Ish'haq Rajoo; in May, rights groups called for charges against Kokila Annamalai over alleged non-compliance with a POFMA order to be dropped. Government ministries continued issuing POFMA Correction Directions in 2026. Capital-punishment operations also remained current: Singapore executed Lingkesvaran Rajendaren for a drug offence on 11 February 2026 and scheduled additional drug-related executions.

**Counter-evidence / reason not to re-escalate to `R`:** POFMA contains a statutory route to appeal ministerial directions to the High Court, and Singapore's Court of Appeal has set out an objective judicial framework capable of setting directions aside. Courts continue to hear constitutional and capital cases. The existence and actual use of judicial review means the coercive projects should not be conflated with the entire State apparatus.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **POFMA/information-control projects materially criminalising or coercively burdening protected political, journalistic or rights expression; public-order/assembly enforcement materially suppressing protected peaceful civic activity; and capital-punishment/execution projects, particularly drug-offence executions, where ECL-covered software would materially facilitate the coercive process**. Courts, POFMA judicial appeals, independent legal defence and unrelated public administration are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** repeal/narrowing or demonstrably non-coercive use of POFMA against protected expression, durable rights-compliant assembly practice, moratorium/abolition or material remediation of the relevant execution system, and preservation of effective judicial challenge.

## #164 South Sudan — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; EXPAND DEFINED SECURITY/MILITARY/DETENTION SCOPE WITHOUT RECAPTURING THE JUDICIARY**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** A 1 April 2026 UN Special Procedures communication addressed South Sudan over serious alleged violations against civilians linked to intensified military operations since late December 2025. UN reporting also describes renewed conflict, arbitrary/prolonged detention, repression and severe civilian harm involving government/state-aligned forces. The evidence strengthens the current security/military component of the scoped finding.

**Counter-evidence / reason not to re-escalate to `R`:** The Judiciary of South Sudan, Ministry of Justice, prosecutors and police investigators continue participating in mobile justice interventions with measurable results. A southern Unity mobile court concluded 68 cases in April 2026, released 29 people who were wrongfully detained or had completed sentences, and heard cases involving members of the security forces. In July 2026 a first judicial mission to Duk reviewed criminal cases and resolved civil disputes. These are substantive rights-protective State functions, not merely announced reforms.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **NSS/intelligence, military, police or other attributable State-aligned security projects materially involved in arbitrary/incommunicado or secret detention, torture/ill-treatment, enforced disappearance, political repression, unlawful/indiscriminate attacks on civilians, reprisals or material obstruction of humanitarian/accountability work**. Rival armed/opposition actors require separate attribution. The Judiciary, mobile courts, prosecutors/investigators acting independently to remedy abuse, legal aid and unrelated civilian functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** closure/reform of secret detention, release/remedy for arbitrary detainees, independent command accountability for civilian harm, effective civilian control and durable expansion of functioning judicial access. **Re-escalation to `R`** requires evidence that independent justice/remedial institutions become materially captured or incapable of producing meaningful contrary outcomes.
