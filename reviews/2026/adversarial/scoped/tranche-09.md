# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 9

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche reviews Thailand, Trinidad and Tobago, Türkiye, Uganda, Uzbekistan, Yemen, Zambia and Zimbabwe. It applies falsification-first review, reversibility and actor-specific attribution. Prior `R → S` outcomes are not treated as presumptively correct; each surviving `S` must remain tied to current coercive projects and survive contrary evidence. Fragmented-conflict actors are not collapsed into a fictional unitary State apparatus.

## #174 Thailand — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO POLITICAL-EXPRESSION / PUBLIC-ORDER AND SPECIFIC CYBER-CONTROL PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** In February 2026 a Bangkok criminal court sentenced four prominent democracy/free-expression activists to 32 months' imprisonment under lèse-majesté-related charges arising from peaceful political speech. Current 2026 review material documents continued use of section 112, sedition, computer-crime and assembly provisions against protected expression and peaceful activism. The 2025 record also documented a coordinated police/military `Cyber Team` targeting civil society and opposition groups.

**Counter-evidence / narrowing:** Courts have granted bail in some cases, prosecutors dropped the Paul Chambers lèse-majesté case in 2025, and soldiers were convicted for torture in a separate case. These are real accountability signals. The prior generic `refoulement apparatus` component is not retained as an active 2026 restriction absent sufficiently fresh recurrence at the cutoff; historical unlawful returns remain a review trigger.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **police, prosecutorial, public-order and cyber/information-control projects materially criminalising or intimidating protected peaceful expression/assembly, including section 112/sedition/computer-crime enforcement when used against protected speech, and coordinated State cyber operations targeting civil society/opposition**. Courts exercising independent remedy, torture-accountability proceedings and unrelated policing/public administration are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** durable non-use/reform of criminal provisions against protected speech, release/remedy in expression-only cases, cessation of coordinated cyber targeting and effective investigation of unlawful protest repression. **Re-expand review** if new attributable unlawful refoulement occurs.

## #178 Trinidad and Tobago — **DOWNGRADE S → U; CURRENT PERSISTENCE OF SUMMARY-RETURN PROJECT INSUFFICIENT**

**Prior scope:** migration, border and asylum enforcement.

**Evidence for the former `S`:** The 2025/26 record describes summary pushbacks/deportations of people seeking asylum and the absence of asylum-specific legislation. A 2026 UPR submission continues to identify immigration detention and the criminalisation of irregular entry as structural protection concerns.

**Material change / counter-evidence:** In 2026 the government introduced a Migrant Registration Framework allowing qualifying undocumented migrants to regularise residence and work through 31 December 2026. UNHCR's current guidance records Special Inquiry procedures, the ability to raise an asylum claim, a right to challenge deportation decisions and possible supervised release. The independent Ombudsman also remains available for public-administration complaints. At the cutoff, the review did not identify sufficiently fresh evidence of a systematic 2026 summary-refoulement project to justify a stable `S` merely from the older pattern and structural risk.

**Adversarial finding:** **DOWNGRADE `S → U — Under Review`.** Current review focuses on **actual deportation/return practice, access to asylum/non-refoulement safeguards, immigration detention conditions and implementation of the 2026 registration framework**. Structural legal vulnerability alone does not equal a current ECL-prohibited project.

**Re-escalation trigger:** new attributable collective or summary deportation of protection-seekers without effective individualized non-refoulement review, systematic arbitrary immigration detention or obstruction of access to UNHCR/legal remedy. **Removal to `N` trigger:** durable individualized protection review, effective appeal, rights-compliant detention practice and no recurrence of unlawful return.

## #180 Türkiye — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; DEFINE COUNTERTERRORISM / POLITICAL-PROSECUTION, CIVIC-SPACE AND PUBLIC-ORDER SCOPE**

**Prior adversarial status:** Whole-State review downgraded Türkiye from `R` to `S` because Constitutional Court and external judicial review produced material rights-protective counterweight.

**Evidence preserving `S`:** UN Special Procedures issued multiple 2026 communications concerning counterterrorism charges, detention and alleged ill-treatment of defenders/lawyers, dissolution and prosecution involving an LGBTI association, judicial harassment and arrest/prosecution of environmental defenders. These show current use of coercive criminal/public-order systems against protected activity.

**Counter-evidence:** The Constitutional Court continues individual-rights review, and the European Court of Human Rights remains an operative external constraint. In May 2026 the ECtHR Grand Chamber found Convention violations in `Yasak v. Türkiye`, including under the principle of legality and the prohibition of inhuman/degrading treatment.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **counterterrorism/security/prosecutorial projects materially criminalising protected defender, lawyer, journalistic or peaceful political activity; police/public-order operations using unlawful force or arbitrary detention against peaceful assembly; coercive association-dissolution/civic-control projects; and specific information-blocking/censorship projects tied to political repression**. Constitutional/remedial courts and unrelated public functions remain excluded.

**Removal trigger:** implementation of Constitutional Court/ECtHR rulings, cessation/remedy of political/counterterrorism prosecutions targeting protected activity, effective police accountability and durable restoration of civic/media freedom.

## #183 Uganda — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; EXPAND DEFINED SCOPE TO CURRENT MILITARY/ELECTION/CIVIC-CONTROL PROJECTS WITHOUT RECAPTURING THE WHOLE STATE**

**Prior adversarial status:** Whole-State review downgraded Uganda from `R` to `S` because the Uganda Human Rights Commission (UHRC) is a constitutional tribunal with powers to investigate, order release/compensation and monitor detention/elections.

**Evidence preserving `S`:** The January 2026 election period and aftermath materially strengthened the coercive record. UN communications concern arbitrary detention of Sarah Bireete and suspension of multiple human-rights organisations around the election. Human Rights Watch documented post-election mass arrests/disappearances and, in July 2026, renewed military seizures of critics, incommunicado detention and pressure on independent media.

**Counter-evidence / reason not to re-escalate to `R`:** UHRC continues to exist as a constitutionally mandated remedial institution, and courts/independent legal functions remain separately capable of contesting abuse. The evidence is severe and expanding, but ECL attribution still should follow the coercive security/election/civic-control apparatus rather than swallow those counter-institutions.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **UPDF/intelligence/police and other attributable security projects materially involved in arbitrary/incommunicado detention, enforced disappearance, torture/ill-treatment, political repression or unlawful force; election-period communications shutdown/surveillance or opposition-suppression projects; and administrative projects arbitrarily suspending rights organisations or independent media as a tool of political control**. UHRC, courts/remedial functions and unrelated services are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** release/remedy for political detainees, independent investigation/prosecution of military/police abuses, compliance with UHRC/court orders, restoration of independent media/civic organisations and demonstrably rights-compliant future electoral security.

## #189 Uzbekistan — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; DEFINE POLITICAL-PROSECUTION, TORTURE, DISCRIMINATORY ENFORCEMENT AND COERCIVE AGRICULTURAL PROJECTS**

**Prior adversarial status:** Whole-State review downgraded Uzbekistan from `R` to `S` because the Ombudsman/NPM, torture prosecutions and other remedial functions demonstrated real rights-protective intervention.

**Evidence preserving `S`:** The current record continues to document politically motivated prosecution, restrictions on expression, torture/ill-treatment, Karakalpakstan accountability failures, discriminatory enforcement against LGBTI people and forced/psychiatric detention. A February 2026 HRW/Uzbek Forum investigation also found that coercive State production mandates and quota-linked penalties continue to violate cotton/wheat farmers' rights despite reforms.

**Counter-evidence:** The Ombudsman/NPM continues detention monitoring and legislative advocacy; police officers have been convicted for torture; and recent laws/decrees purport to strengthen farmer and detainee protections. These developments make a whole-State designation overbroad and must be preserved as exergic functions.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **security/prosecutorial/prison projects materially involved in fabricated or politically motivated prosecution, forced psychiatric detention, torture/ill-treatment or Karakalpakstan repression; law-enforcement projects using discriminatory criminalisation/blackmail against LGBTI people; coercive eviction projects; and agricultural production systems materially enforcing crop quotas/mandates through threats of land loss, violence, extortion or comparable coercion**. Ombudsman/NPM, independent torture prosecution and unrelated reform/remedial projects are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** independent Karakalpakstan accountability, effective torture remediation, cessation of fabricated/political cases and discriminatory enforcement, and verified elimination of coercive agricultural mandates/evictions.

## #193 Yemen — **UPHOLD S; REFRAME AS FRAGMENTED-AUTHORITY MAP WITH ACTOR-SPECIFIC DESIGNATION REQUIREMENT**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** As of June 2026, 73 UN personnel remained arbitrarily detained by Huthi de facto authorities, alongside civil-society and humanitarian workers; one UN colleague had died in detention. The broader current record documents arbitrary detention, enforced disappearance, torture and ill-treatment by Huthi forces, internationally recognised government actors and UAE-backed forces. Conflict escalation in 2026 reinforces that attribution cannot be treated as static.

**Counter-evidence / fragmentation:** A May 2026 agreement between the internationally recognised government and Huthi authorities provided for a major prisoner exchange under international facilitation. More importantly, Yemen has no unitary coercive apparatus: Huthi authorities, government/security bodies, STC/UAE-backed forces and other armed actors exercise different territorial and institutional control.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives as a **fragmented-authority State dossier**, not as a collective Yemeni restriction. Any operative designation must identify the specific **Huthi de facto detention/security apparatus, internationally recognised-government detention/security unit, STC/UAE-backed security structure, or other attributable conflict-party project** materially involved in arbitrary detention, disappearance, torture, reprisal against humanitarian/civic actors or unlawful coercive targeting. The population, humanitarian institutions, independent civilian administration and conduct of rival actors are excluded from one another's attribution.

**Removal trigger:** actor-specific release/remedy, cessation of arbitrary detention/disappearance/torture, independent detention monitoring and durable transfer to rights-compliant civilian legal control. Each actor/project must be reviewed separately.

## #194 Zambia — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE CYBER/STATE-SECURITY, PUBLIC-ORDER AND POLITICAL-DETENTION SCOPE**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** In July 2026 journalist MacPherson Mukuka was detained and charged under the 2025 Cyber Crimes Act over publication of a recording, prompting concerns that the law was being used to intimidate public-interest journalism. Opposition figure Xavier Chungu was also detained after a podcast interview on multiple State-security/sedition charges and denied bail through a `No Bail Certificate`. The broader record documents disruption of opposition meetings and broad surveillance/interception powers under the 2025 cyber legislation.

**Counter-evidence:** Zambia retains competitive elections, courts and active constitutional litigation. The Law Association of Zambia challenged provisions of the Cyber Crimes Act, and government officials publicly deny that the cyber framework authorises indiscriminate surveillance. Those mechanisms prevent a whole-State inference.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **cyber-security/criminal-enforcement and State-security projects materially criminalising protected journalism or peaceful political expression; surveillance/interception deployments lacking meaningful legal safeguards and used for coercive political targeting; and police/public-order projects materially obstructing peaceful opposition assembly or imposing arbitrary political detention**. Courts, legal challenges, election administration absent independent evidence and unrelated public services are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** release/remedy in expression-only cases, rights-compliant narrowing/interpretation of cyber and State-security laws, warrant-based independent surveillance safeguards and sustained non-interference with peaceful opposition/journalism.

## #195 Zimbabwe — **UPHOLD S AFTER PRIOR R → S; DEFINE EXECUTIVE/SECURITY POLITICAL-REPRESSION AND CIVIC-CONTROL SCOPE**

**Prior adversarial status:** Whole-State review downgraded Zimbabwe from `R` to `S` because courts repeatedly acquitted/released political defendants and invalidated repressive legal provisions, demonstrating meaningful counter-institutional effect.

**Evidence preserving `S`:** In 2026 authorities and suspected ruling-party/security-linked actors intensified intimidation of critics opposing proposed presidential-term extension. Human Rights Watch documented attacks, disruption of meetings and police involvement/inaction around the campaign. Separately, a prominent opposition activist was acquitted in June after more than seven months in detention on charges HRW described as baseless, underscoring both coercive prosecution and judicial counterweight.

**Counter-evidence:** Courts continue to acquit political defendants and have struck down unconstitutional provisions; the death penalty was abolished and resentencing proceeded. These outcomes remain incompatible with a whole-State `R` unless the counter-institutions themselves become materially captured.

**Adversarial finding:** retain `S` for **police/security/intelligence and executive-administrative projects materially using arbitrary political detention, surveillance/harassment, violent or coercive suppression of peaceful meetings/protest, criminal-justice pressure on critics, or coercive civil-society/constitutional-term-extension enforcement**. Independent courts, lawyers/remedial functions and unrelated public services remain excluded.

**Removal trigger:** end/remedy of political detention and intimidation, effective investigation of attacks on critics, narrowing/removal of coercive civil-society controls, respect for judicial outcomes and a constitutionally contestable political process free from security intimidation.
