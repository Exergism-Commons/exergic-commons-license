# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 6

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche reviews Lesotho, Libya, Malaysia, Malta, Mexico, Moldova, Mozambique and Nepal. The review applies falsification-first attribution and ECL's reversibility rule: a scoped restriction must remain tied to a current, identifiable coercive project or apparatus; counter-institutions and remediation are treated as evidence; historical abuse is not enough to preserve a restriction after the attributable project or governing context materially changes.

## #95 Lesotho — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE CORRECTIONAL AND SPECIFIC PUBLIC-ORDER / SPEECH-ENFORCEMENT SCOPE**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** The official Commission of Inquiry into Lesotho Correctional Service (LCS) abuse documented assaults and ill-treatment affecting hundreds of detainees after the December 2023 prison break, including a death and permanent disability. More importantly for current persistence, an April 2026 Ombudsman follow-up reported that only about 9% of 78 correctional recommendations had been implemented, while overcrowding and serious health/resource deficiencies persisted. The 2025/26 record also includes sedition/public-order enforcement and protest restrictions affecting protected expression and assembly.

**Counter-evidence:** The Ombudsman is an independent constitutional oversight institution and the Ministry of Law and Justice has acknowledged reform needs and announced rehabilitation/construction measures. Some Commission/Ombudsman recommendations are being implemented. These institutions and remediation projects are excluded from the restriction.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **LCS detention/conditions, abuse and accountability failures materially involving cruel or degrading treatment, and specific police/public-order/sedition enforcement materially suppressing protected peaceful expression or assembly**. The Lesotho State, courts, Ombudsman and unrelated public functions are not Restricted generally.

**Removal trigger:** substantial implementation of Ombudsman/Commission recommendations, credible criminal/disciplinary accountability for correctional abuse, durable reduction of overcrowding and serious detention-health failures, and rights-compliant protest/speech enforcement.

## #97 Libya — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE ATTRIBUTABLE DE FACTO SECURITY / DETENTION / MIGRATION STRUCTURES**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** OHCHR and UNSMIL's 2026 migration report documents continuing widespread and systematic violations against migrants, asylum seekers and refugees, including torture, arbitrary detention and other severe abuse by traffickers, armed groups and **State-affiliated actors involved in migration and border management**. Wider UN reporting continues to describe arbitrary detention and serious abuse across facilities controlled by competing authorities and affiliated security structures.

**Counter-evidence:** Libya continues formal engagement with UN mechanisms and the UPR process, and there are justice, municipal and institutional-reform initiatives that must not be treated as implicated merely because they operate under a fragmented State environment.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **attributable GNU-, LAAF- or other de facto-authority security, detention, migration/border-management units and materially participating State-affiliated actors involved in arbitrary detention, torture, coercive abuse, unlawful transfer/interception or material obstruction of accountability**. Non-attributable private armed-group conduct is not automatically assigned to the State; unrelated civilian/remedial functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** independently verifiable closure or compliant reform of abusive detention/migration facilities, prosecution/remedy for torture and arbitrary detention, safeguards against unsafe transfer/interception and clear civilian/legal accountability over implicated units.

## #103 Malaysia — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO CURRENT SPEECH/PUBLIC-ORDER ENFORCEMENT AND IMMIGRATION DETENTION**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** In April 2026 SUHAKAM criticized police arrests/detention linked to peaceful placard display and separately described custodial remand for non-violent online criticism of the Prime Minister as disproportionate, highlighting continued use of broad speech provisions. In July 2026 SUHAKAM reported the Home Affairs Ministry's disclosure that 465 deaths occurred in immigration detention depots between 2021 and 2025 and called for urgent systemic reform.

**Counter-evidence:** SUHAKAM is an active national counter-institution; detainees in the placard incident were released, Parliament publicly debates SUHAKAM findings and the Government has articulated detention/human-rights reform commitments. These mechanisms materially defeat a whole-State finding.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **specific police, communications/sedition and public-order enforcement materially criminalizing protected non-violent expression or peaceful assembly, and immigration-detention systems producing arbitrary/prolonged detention, serious conditions, preventable custody-death risk or obstruction of independent accountability**. The earlier generic `LGBTI raid` component is removed from the current scope absent sufficiently current project-specific evidence.

**Removal trigger:** durable narrowing/non-use of vague speech offences against protected expression, proportionate assembly policing, effective independent detention oversight, credible investigation/remedy for deaths in custody and measurable improvement in immigration-detention conditions.

## #106 Malta — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE IMMIGRATION-DETENTION, MARITIME RETURN AND RELATED PROSECUTION PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's 2025/26 record describes continued automatic detention of many irregular maritime arrivals/asylum seekers, largely ineffective appeals, detention conditions characterized by the CPT as deeply punitive, allegations of staff violence, reports of delayed/non-assistance at sea and continued Malta-Libya cooperation returning people toward a country not considered safe for disembarkation. The El Hiblu prosecution also remained active in 2026 against people accused after opposing return to Libya.

**Counter-evidence:** Maltese and European courts actively review detention and return practices. Malta's Constitutional Court ordered a retrial in the `Easter Monday push back` case, and Council of Europe supervision provides an external remedial channel. These are significant exergic institutions and are expressly excluded.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **immigration-detention practices amounting to arbitrary or seriously punitive confinement; maritime non-assistance, pushback or Libya-cooperation projects materially facilitating unlawful return to a substantiated serious-abuse risk; and prosecution projects insofar as they materially criminalize protected resistance to an unlawful return**. Courts and independent remedial functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** individualized and reviewable detention replacing automatic practices, effective appeal and detention safeguards, rights-compliant search/rescue and disembarkation policy, non-refoulement safeguards in Libya cooperation and resolution/remedy of prosecutions premised on protected resistance to unsafe return.

## #110 Mexico — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO ATTRIBUTABLE DISAPPEARANCE/TORTURE AND COERCIVE DATA DEPLOYMENTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** In April 2026 the UN Committee on Enforced Disappearances decided to bring Mexico's situation to the UN General Assembly under article 34 after reviewing indications of widespread/systematic enforced disappearance, continuing impunity, alleged public-official involvement or acquiescence and serious search/investigation deficiencies. Current Mexican human-rights bodies also continue issuing grave-violation recommendations attributing enforced disappearance and torture to identified security/military personnel. Mexico is simultaneously expanding national biometric identity infrastructure.

**Counter-evidence:** Mexico maintains federal/state search commissions, specialized prosecutors, the CNDH, courts and victim/search institutions that investigate and sometimes attribute official abuse. Federal fragmentation and the major role of organized-crime actors make whole-State attribution inaccurate.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **identified federal, state or municipal security and military-public-security units/projects materially involved in enforced disappearance, torture, unlawful coercive detention/targeting, official collusion/acquiescence or material obstruction of search/accountability**. Biometric identity/data infrastructure is **not Restricted merely because it uses biometrics**; it enters scope only when a concrete deployment materially enables coercive surveillance, discriminatory targeting or irreversible institutional capture under ECL criteria.

**Removal trigger:** demonstrable reduction of enforced disappearances, effective prosecution/remedy for attributable official involvement, protected and adequately resourced search/investigation functions, demilitarized/rights-compliant public-security practice where relevant and binding safeguards for any coercive biometric/data use.

## #112 Moldova — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO PRETRIAL-DETENTION AND ILL-TREATMENT / REMEDY FAILURES**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Council of Europe supervision in 2026 continued to identify a systemic problem in Moldovan pretrial-detention decisions lacking relevant and sufficient individualized reasoning, alongside unresolved cases concerning ill-treatment/torture used to extract confessions and ineffective investigation/remedy. These are current, institutionally attributable criminal-justice problems.

**Counter-evidence:** Moldova is actively implementing Council of Europe criminal-justice reform, the Ombudsman/National Preventive Mechanism remains active, and a 2026 journalist-safety action plan plus other media reforms provide substantial evidence against retaining the earlier broad media-regulatory component.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **pretrial-detention practices materially lacking individualized necessity/proportionality and identified law-enforcement/detention projects involving ill-treatment/torture or ineffective investigation/remedy**. The broad media-regulatory component is removed from active scope on the present record. Conduct in Transnistria is not attributed to Moldova without independent attribution evidence; courts, Ombudsman/NPM and reform/remedial functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** durable compliance with reasoned pretrial-detention standards, effective independent investigation/prosecution/remedy for torture or ill-treatment, implementation of European Court judgments and sustained operation of independent preventive oversight.

## #117 Mozambique — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE CURRENT POLICE / SECURITY LETHAL-FORCE AND REPRESSION PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** The post-election crackdown produced large-scale unlawful lethal force, arbitrary arrest, disappearances and information suppression attributable to police/security structures. Current persistence is not merely historical: in July 2026 Amnesty documented video evidence of a plain-clothes police officer summarily executing Narciso Castigo Sambo after he had been subdued; police acknowledged the incident and SERNIC opened an investigation. The 2025/26 record also includes detention/intimidation of journalists and opposition-linked actors.

**Counter-evidence:** Investigations by prosecutorial/SERNIC structures and changes in police leadership are material counter-evidence. Those accountability functions must remain outside the scope unless independently shown to obstruct accountability.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **identified police, public-order and security units/operations materially involved in unlawful lethal force or summary execution, arbitrary detention/disappearance, opposition/journalist repression, intimidation or coercive information suppression**. Abuses by insurgent/non-State actors, including in Cabo Delgado, are not imputed to the State absent attribution.

**Removal trigger:** credible prosecution/remedy for unlawful killings and disappearances, transparent use-of-force reform, traceable detention safeguards, durable protection of journalists/opposition activity and independent investigation of security-force abuse.

## #121 Nepal — **DOWNGRADE S → U; PRIOR COERCIVE PROJECT DOES NOT YET SHOW CURRENT CONTINUITY**

**Prior scope:** law-enforcement, protest-policing and coercive development enforcement.

**Evidence for the former `S`:** The September 2025 youth protests involved alleged extrajudicial/unlawful killings, arbitrary detention and excessive force by police/security bodies. Earlier evidence also raised serious concerns about coercive enforcement against Indigenous communities opposing development projects without adequate free, prior and informed consent.

**Material change / counter-evidence:** Nepal subsequently changed government following the 2026 election. The National Human Rights Commission completed its investigation into the `Gen Z` protest violations and transmitted recommendations to the Government in May 2026, while accountability investigations into the former leadership/security response proceeded. At the review cutoff, the record does not establish sufficient fresh recurrence showing that the same protest-repression project remains an active policy of the new administration.

**Adversarial finding:** ECL's reversibility rule requires **DOWNGRADE `S → U — Under Review`**. Current review scope is **implementation of accountability for the September 2025 killings; any renewed unlawful protest-policing, arbitrary-detention or expression-suppression project; and any current coercive development/FPIC enforcement that can be specifically re-established with evidence**.

**Re-escalation trigger:** new attributable unlawful lethal protest policing, systematic arbitrary detention/suppression of peaceful dissent or renewed coercive development enforcement meeting ECL's material/systematic threshold. **Removal to `N` trigger:** sustained non-recurrence plus credible accountability/remediation and rights-compliant policing/FPIC safeguards.
