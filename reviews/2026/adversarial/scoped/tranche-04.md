# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 4

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche reviews Greece, Guatemala, Guinea-Bissau, Haiti, Honduras, Hungary, Iceland and Indonesia. It applies ECL's reversibility rule strictly: ended or materially remediated projects are not kept Restricted by inertia, while a scoped finding may escalate if the coercive apparatus itself has become the governing authority.

## #66 Greece — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO SUBSTANTIATED PUBLIC-ORDER / PROTEST-POLICING OPERATIONS AND ACCOUNTABILITY FAILURES**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's June 2026 protest-policing review documents recurring unnecessary/excessive force against peaceful demonstrators and journalists, arbitrary deprivation of liberty, misuse of stun grenades, batons and chemical irritants, restrictive protest rules and persistent accountability failures. Its review of serious law-enforcement cases found very low rates of prosecution/conviction, including torture allegations.

**Counter-evidence:** Greece has criminal and disciplinary investigations, the EMIDIPA police oversight mechanism and judicial remedies. A police officer was convicted in 2025 for the fatal shooting of 16-year-old Roma boy Kostas Frangoulis. These mechanisms are material counter-evidence even though the broader accountability problem persists.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives only for **identified public-order/protest-policing units and operations materially involving unlawful or disproportionate force, arbitrary identity-check/protest detention, dangerous less-lethal deployments, or projects materially obstructing investigation/accountability for such conduct**. Greek police, courts and public administration are not restricted generically.

**Removal trigger:** sustained compliance with proportionality standards, independent investigations/prosecutions where warranted, effective EMIDIPA capacity and demonstrated reduction in abusive protest policing.

## #68 Guatemala — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO CRIMINALIZATION / ARBITRARY-DETENTION PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Current 2025/26 evidence documents allegedly unfounded prosecutions and arbitrary detention of anti-corruption justice actors, defenders, journalists, students and Indigenous leaders. In May 2026 Amnesty designated Indigenous leaders Luis Pacheco and Héctor Chaclán prisoners of conscience after more than a year in detention arising from peaceful protest activity. Current cases involving lawyer/defender Claudia González and other justice-sector actors reinforce the pattern.

**Counter-evidence:** Guatemala's Constitutional Court remains an independent constitutional remedy institution with amparo and constitutionality powers; in July 2026 it hosted an IACHR delegation and reiterated its fundamental-rights role. The existence of this counter-institution makes restriction of the judiciary generally inaccurate.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **Public Ministry/FECI, prosecutorial, detention and implicated judicial projects materially used for unfounded criminalization or arbitrary detention of anti-corruption justice workers, defenders, journalists, Indigenous peaceful-protest leaders or comparable protected activity**. The Constitutional Court and genuinely independent judicial/remediation functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** release/fair independent review of prisoners-of-conscience cases, end to retaliatory prosecution, protection of Indigenous political participation and effective constitutional/judicial remedies.

## #70 Guinea-Bissau — **ESCALATE S → R; CURRENT MILITARY/TRANSITIONAL GOVERNING APPARATUS**

**Evidence supporting escalation:** The military seized power on 26 November 2025 before completion/publication of the electoral result and detained key political leaders. On 5 March 2026 the African Union Peace and Security Council called on the interim authorities to release political detainees immediately and unconditionally, restore constitutionalism, prevent military interference in politics and subordinate the security sector to civilian authority. Although Domingos Simões Pereira was moved from prison to house arrest and the junta announced elections for 6 December 2026, political detention and military political control remained current in 2026.

**Counter-evidence:** The transitional authorities have announced an inclusive transition, scheduled elections and pledged releases of political prisoners. Some detainees have been released or moved out of prison. These commitments are material and make the finding highly reviewable rather than permanent.

**Adversarial finding:** the first-pass `S` is now too narrow because **the military apparatus implicated in the coercive conduct is itself the de facto governing authority**. Provisional outcome becomes **`R — current Military High Command / de facto transitional governing apparatus`**. It does not attach to the population, civil society or any future civilian constitutional government.

**Mandatory review:** immediate review upon unconditional release of political detainees and again after the scheduled **6 December 2026** election/civilian transfer, or earlier if constitutional government is restored.

## #72 Haiti — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO STATE-LINKED DRONE / SUMMARY-EXECUTION SECURITY PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** 2026 reporting documents extensive apparently unlawful lethal drone strikes and other lethal operations involving State security bodies and private contractors. BINUH attributes the armed-drone programme to a specialized Task Force created by the Prime Minister and operated with contractor support. UN and rights reporting describes civilian/child casualties and probable extrajudicial executions, while no adequate investigation had initially been opened.

**Counter-evidence:** Haiti created two specialized judicial units in 2026 intended to address serious human-rights abuses and financial crimes; police internal-affairs investigations also exist. These are genuine accountability developments and must be excluded from the coercive scope.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **the Prime-Minister-created Security Task Force, PNH/security units materially participating in unlawful drone or summary-execution operations, and private contractors/other Covered Associates materially supporting those projects**. Gang violence and general State collapse are not imputed to the Haitian State.

**Removal trigger:** independent investigation/prosecution, strict targeting/civilian-harm safeguards, termination or compliant redesign of the drone programme and demonstrated judicial accountability.

## #74 Honduras — **DOWNGRADE S → U; FORMER NATIONAL STATE-OF-EXCEPTION PROJECT ENDED**

**Prior scope:** State-of-emergency police, security and detention apparatus.

**Evidence for the former `S`:** During the 2022–January 2026 state of exception, the Honduran human-rights institution CONADEH received hundreds of complaints alleging arbitrary detention, false evidence, torture/ill-treatment, property abuse and sexual violence. Amnesty and other rights reporting documented prolonged emergency-security concerns.

**Material change / counter-evidence:** The new government suspended the nationwide state of exception upon taking office in January 2026 and subsequently stated that its national security policy would operate without a nationwide emergency regime. The specific active project that justified the scoped restriction therefore ceased. Unresolved accountability in the Berta Cáceres case and continuing defender risks remain serious but are not, on the evidence reviewed, a sufficiently precise replacement State project for a stable `S` designation.

**Adversarial finding:** ECL's reversibility principle requires **DOWNGRADE `S → U — Under Review`**. Current review scope is limited to **post-emergency police/security accountability, any newly created localized exceptional-security zones, and defender-protection failures**. Historical emergency abuses remain evidence and may support individual/project accountability, but they do not keep a terminated national project currently Restricted by inertia.

**Re-escalation trigger:** new localized/national exceptional-security projects producing materially comparable arbitrary detention, torture or repression. **Removal to `N` trigger:** sustained non-recurrence plus credible accountability/remediation for legacy abuse.

## #75 Hungary — **NARROW S TO CURRENT ASYLUM / BORDER-PUSHBACK AND EMERGENCY-MIGRATION SYSTEMS**

**Material political/institutional change:** Hungary changed government after the April 2026 election. Under the new government, Budapest Pride proceeded publicly in June 2026 with police authorization/protection rather than the prior ban architecture. The active Pride/assembly component of the earlier scoped finding therefore leaves the current Restricted scope. Broader rule-of-law/constitutional reforms are underway and must be assessed by actual effects rather than partisan labels.

**Evidence preserving a narrower `S`:** As of May 2026 the mass-migration `state of crisis` and border-pushback authority remained in force, and the incoming government indicated it would maintain strict border policy while promising compliance with EU law. Current border-security cooperation with Serbia continued in July 2026. This leaves a live ECL question around collective pushbacks, non-refoulement and meaningful access to individualized protection procedures.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives only for **currently operative asylum/border-pushback and emergency-migration systems that materially deny individualized protection, non-refoulement safeguards or meaningful contestability**. Pride/assembly and prior discriminatory municipal projects are removed from the active Restricted scope unless comparable coercive enforcement recurs.

**Removal trigger:** repeal/non-use of unlawful pushback authority, rights-compliant asylum access, effective judicial remedies and completion of reforms eliminating the ECL-relevant coercive characteristics.

## #76 Iceland — **UPHOLD S; KEEP VERY NARROW PRETRIAL-SOLITARY-CONFINEMENT SCOPE**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's July 2026 UPR submission identifies widespread use of pretrial solitary confinement as a continuing rights concern. Earlier detailed evidence documented disproportionate use, including against children and vulnerable persons, and insufficient safeguards.

**Counter-evidence:** Solitary-confinement decisions are formally subject to judicial authorization and Iceland retains strong courts and oversight institutions. The wider justice/prison system is not shown to constitute an ECL-prohibited project.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives only for **prosecutorial/police requests, judicial orders and detention practices imposing pretrial solitary confinement beyond strict necessity/proportionality, especially involving children or vulnerable persons**. Icelandic courts, prosecutors, police and prison administration are not restricted generally.

**Review trigger:** legislative/procedural reform, independent evidence of substantially reduced use, stronger necessity/proportionality safeguards and follow-up through the next UPR/CPT cycle.

## #78 Indonesia — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE PROTEST-CRIMINALIZATION, MILITARY-LINKED DECEPTION AND SPECIFIC SECURITY PROJECTS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** In 2026 Amnesty documented military-connected coordinated disinformation/intimidation campaigns branding critics as `foreign agents`, including a defender later subjected to an acid attack. UN Special Procedures raised arbitrary arrest/criminalization of activists for peaceful protest, and separate 2026 scrutiny concerned proposed/expanded military counterterrorism roles. Papua and extractive-project security abuses remain separately reviewable where specifically evidenced.

**Counter-evidence:** Indonesia replied to UN communications. In the protest-related prosecution cohort, six of seven activists were released and four were acquitted by July 2026, demonstrating functioning judicial counterweights. A draft or expanded military legal role does not itself become Restricted absent material implementation producing ECL-prohibited conduct.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **specific police/prosecutorial projects criminalizing protected peaceful protest; TNI/military-connected coordinated deception or intimidation materially targeting civil society/opposition; implemented peacetime military/counterterrorism projects producing coercive abuse; and specifically evidenced Papua/extractive security operations**. Courts that acquit/release defendants and independent remediation functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** cessation/accountability for disinformation/intimidation campaigns, durable protection of peaceful protest, rejection/remediation of abusive military expansion and effective investigation of Papua/extractive abuses.
