# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 2

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche applies the falsification-first scoped review to Algeria, Angola, Bahrain, Benin, Bhutan, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Brazil and Cameroon. The central question is not whether serious abuse exists, but whether the existing `S` scope identifies the actual coercive system with enough precision and credits effective counter-institutions or remediation.

## #3 Algeria — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO POLITICAL-REPRESSION / RIGHTS-DEFENDER ENFORCEMENT SYSTEMS**

**Prior scope:** security, judicial and political-repression apparatus.

**Evidence preserving `S`:**

- 2026 UN Special Procedures communications concern the alleged arbitrary detention of journalist and human-rights defender Hassan Bouras; alleged interference, harassment and pressure against human-rights lawyers Mounir Gharbi and Soufiane Oauli; and alleged arbitrary/incommunicado detention and intimidation of members of CFDA/SOS Disparus and relatives of disappeared persons.
- A December 2025 communication, answered in 2026, concerns alleged enforced disappearance, arbitrary detention, ill-treatment, abusive criminal prosecution and unfair trial linked to expression, association, peaceful assembly and public participation.
- Current press-freedom reporting documents multiple journalists prosecuted or detained under terrorism, false-information, insult and national-security theories for journalistic/expression activity.

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- Algeria has replied to several UN communications.
- The President granted an individual pardon to writer Boualem Sansal in November 2025. Clemency is material remediation for an individual case, even though it does not alter the legal/enforcement architecture.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives, but `security, judicial and political-repression apparatus` is too broad. Scope should follow **security/prosecutorial/judicial and administrative projects materially used to criminalize or suppress protected journalism, human-rights defence, disappeared-person advocacy, peaceful assembly or political expression**, including misuse of terrorism/national-security offences. Ordinary courts and unrelated public services are not restricted merely by institutional membership.

**Removal/narrowing trigger:** repeal/narrowing of abusive offences, durable release/remedy for expression/defender cases, independent judicial review and cessation of retaliation against lawyers/journalists/disappearance advocates.

## #5 Angola — **NARROW S TO IDENTIFIED POLICE / RAPID-INTERVENTION AND PROTEST/LAND-ENFORCEMENT OPERATIONS**

**Prior scope:** police and security coercive apparatus.

**Evidence preserving `S`:**

- UN Special Procedures documented allegations arising from the July 2025 protests: indiscriminate and lethal force, injuries, arbitrary detention of peaceful protesters/bystanders/journalists and harassment of defenders.
- Current 2025/26 reporting describes repeated arbitrary arrests and excessive force against peaceful protests and land-rights activity, including conduct by the Rapid Intervention Police and other police structures.

**Counter-evidence with real effect:**

- Courts acquitted several movement leaders in April 2025 for lack of evidence and many protesters were released without charge.
- Angolan police announced an investigation into protest deaths after the July 2025 unrest. The government also replied to the UN communication.

**Adversarial finding:** the evidence supports `S`, but **not a generic restriction of all Angolan police/security functions**. The scope is narrowed to identified Rapid Intervention Police/National Police units, commanders and operations materially connected to substantiated unlawful protest repression, arbitrary political/defender detention, lethal-force incidents or coercive land-rights enforcement, plus any project knowingly enabling those operations.

**Removal/narrowing trigger:** independent investigation results, prosecutions/remedies where warranted, transparent use-of-force reform, durable facilitation of peaceful assembly and demonstrated non-recurrence.

Sources:
- https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TmSearch/RelCom?code=AGO+2%2F2025
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/location/africa/southern-africa/angola/report-angola/
- https://www.hrw.org/world-report/2026/country-chapters/angola

## #13 Bahrain — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE SECURITY / INTELLIGENCE / DETENTION AND POLITICALLY IMPLICATED JUSTICE SYSTEMS**

**Prior scope:** security, detention and judicial repression apparatus.

**Evidence preserving `S`:**

- The UN Committee against Torture's January 2026 concluding observations records consistent reports of torture/ill-treatment by law-enforcement officers, prison guards, military personnel and intelligence staff, including against persons accused of terrorism and real/perceived political opponents, and expresses deep concern about lack of accountability.
- The Committee reports practical failures of fundamental safeguards, concerns about unofficial/incommunicado detention, deaths in custody, prolonged solitary confinement and retaliation against defenders, journalists and political opponents.
- It also raises concerns about practical judicial independence and the independence/effectiveness of existing torture-complaint mechanisms.

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- The Committee expressly welcomed reforms including transfer of prison health services to government hospitals, expanded alternative sentencing, restorative juvenile justice and specialized juvenile institutions.
- Bahrain maintains Ombudsman, Prisoners' and Detainees' Rights Commission, NIHR, prosecution/judicial inspection mechanisms and compensation measures for victims identified by the Bahrain Independent Commission of Inquiry.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives. The proper scope is **security/intelligence/law-enforcement and detention systems materially involved in torture, incommunicado/unofficial detention, political retaliation or coercive interrogation, together with prosecutorial/judicial projects materially enabling those practices in substantiated cases**. Restorative justice, prison-health reform, victim compensation and genuinely independent oversight/remediation functions are excluded.

**Mandatory review:** after Bahrain's requested CAT follow-up due **28 November 2026**, especially on unofficial detention, prison conditions, monitoring and deaths in custody.

## #19 Benin — **NARROW S TO DIGITAL-CODE / MEDIA-REGULATORY / CRIET POLITICAL-EXPRESSION AND RELATED DETENTION PROJECTS**

**Prior scope:** digital censorship, police/protest and counterterrorism detention.

**Evidence preserving `S`:**

- Digital Code offences concerning false news and electronic harassment continued to be used against expression; media outlets were suspended by the High Authority for Audiovisual and Communication (HAAC), and political critic Julien Kandé Kansou was prosecuted after critical/electoral speech.
- UN Special Procedures raised the detention/prosecution of journalist and defender Hugues Comlan Sossoukpè after his alleged abduction/forced transfer from Côte d'Ivoire. As of July 2026 he remained in custody with serious fair-trial concerns.

**Counter-evidence credited:**

- The HAAC suspensions of Le Patriote and Bénin Web TV were later lifted, demonstrating that some sanctions are reversible rather than permanent.
- Ordinary legal procedures remain available, although the reviewed Sossoukpè record raises serious concerns regarding notice, counsel and prolonged pretrial detention before CRIET.

**Adversarial finding:** the generic `police/protest` component is not sufficiently precise on the current record. `S` is narrowed to **specific Digital Code prosecutions, HAAC/media-control actions, CRIET/counterterrorism or detention processes materially used to suppress protected journalism, political expression or rights-defence activity**, plus directly connected enforcement operations.

**Removal/narrowing trigger:** reform of Digital Code speech offences, independent review/remedy in Sossoukpè and comparable cases, durable media-regulatory proportionality and cessation of counterterrorism tools against protected expression.

Sources:
- https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TmSearch/RelCom?code=BEN+1%2F2025
- https://www.amnesty.org/en/location/africa/west-and-central-africa/benin/report-benin/
- https://www.frontlinedefenders.org/en/case/one-year-after-his-arrest-human-rights-defender-hugues-comlan-sosoukpe-remains-custody

## #20 Bhutan — **UPHOLD S; RECORD MATERIAL 2026 REMEDIATION**

**Prior scope:** political-prisoner and detention apparatus.

**Evidence preserving `S`:**

- At least 28 known political prisoners remained detained in June 2026, many serving life sentences originating in pre-democratic prosecutions involving alleged torture, lack of defence counsel and severe prison conditions.
- UN human-rights mechanisms have treated several of the detentions as arbitrary or raised serious concerns about them.

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:**

- On 1 June 2026 Bhutan released Chatur Man Tamang and Hasta Bahadur Rai after more than 18 years in detention pursuant to royal clemency. This is concrete remediation and must materially count in ECL review.
- Bhutan's constitutional/democratic order since 2008 is distinct from the historical system in which many of the convictions arose, making a State-wide or present-day political-system restriction inappropriate.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives and remains narrowly limited to **the unresolved political-prisoner detention system, responsible detention/security functions and continued enforcement of legacy convictions where the underlying detention remains arbitrary or materially connected to torture/unfair-trial findings**. The broader Bhutanese State is excluded.

**Removal trigger:** release or fair independent review of remaining political prisoners, medical/remedial measures for survivors and accountability/guarantees of non-repetition.

Sources:
- https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/06/10/bhutan-2-political-prisoners-freed-but-28-remain
- https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/TmSearch/RelCom?code=BTN+1%2F2024

## #22 Bosnia and Herzegovina — **UPHOLD S; STRICTLY CONFINE TO REPUBLIKA SRPSKA COERCIVE MEDIA/POLITICAL PROJECTS**

**Prior scope:** Republika Srpska political-repression apparatus.

**Evidence preserving `S`:**

- The Office of the High Representative reported that between October 2025 and April 2026 the freedom-of-expression/media environment in Republika Srpska continued to deteriorate: re-criminalized defamation increasingly generated criminal cases against journalists, producing a chilling effect; political pressure, intimidation, SLAPPs and disproportionate inspections continued.
- The Council of Europe Commissioner in February 2026 warned that statements by the highest Republika Srpska political leaders risked fuelling hate and inter-ethnic tension.

**Strong counter-evidence and attribution limits:**

- Bosnia and Herzegovina is institutionally plural. The national Constitutional Court remains a meaningful rights guarantor and in 2026 entered a new EU/CoE programme aimed at strengthening constitutional remedies and enforcement.
- National institutions developed a 2026–2030 framework for protection of journalists/media with participation from police, prosecutors, journalists and civil society; June 2026 work in Banja Luka included police/prosecutorial journalist-safety mechanisms.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives **only for Republika Srpska entity-level projects materially implementing criminal-defamation/media intimidation, retaliatory inspections/SLAPP facilitation or other substantiated coercive political repression**. Bosnia and Herzegovina as a State, its Constitutional Court, Federation institutions and national journalist-protection mechanisms are expressly outside the scope absent separate evidence.

**Removal/narrowing trigger:** repeal/non-use of criminal defamation against protected journalism, effective journalist protection/accountability, cessation of retaliatory inspections/pressure and compliance with constitutional/human-rights judgments.

Sources:
- https://www.ohr.int/69th-report-of-the-high-representative-for-implementation-of-the-peace-agreement-on-bosnia-and-herzegovina-to-the-secretary-general-of-the-un-/

## #24 Brazil — **NARROW S TO SUBSTANTIATED STATE-LEVEL POLICE / PUBLIC-SECURITY OPERATIONS AND UNREMEDIATED INVESTIGATION FAILURES**

**Prior scope:** police and public-security apparatus.

**Evidence preserving `S`:**

- Brazil recorded extremely high police lethality in 2025, including the October 2025 Rio operation in which more than one hundred civilians were killed; rights investigations raise serious concerns about unlawful killings and deficient investigation.
- Structural racial disparity and repeated lethal-force concerns make specific police/public-security systems materially relevant under ECL.

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:**

- Brazil's Supreme Federal Court continues active supervision under ADPF 635. In February 2026 it ordered Rio authorities to provide operation imagery to the Federal Police for forensic analysis and required the State prosecutor to clarify its role in the operation.
- National Council of Prosecutors Resolution 310 establishes standards for prosecutor-led investigation of deaths, sexual violence, torture and enforced disappearance arising from public-security interventions. Although full implementation was postponed, the accountability architecture is substantive rather than merely nominal.

**Adversarial finding:** a restriction of `Brazilian police/public security` generically is overbroad given federalism and active judicial/prosecutorial counter-institutions. `S` is narrowed to **identified State-level police units, commands and operations with substantiated recurring unlawful lethal-force/torture/disappearance patterns, and investigation/forensic projects that materially obstruct accountability**, while STF, independent prosecution/forensics and remedial projects are excluded.

**Removal/narrowing trigger:** independent investigations/prosecutions, implementation of Resolution 310, compliance with ADPF 635 safeguards, sustained reduction in unlawful police lethality and effective racial-disparity remediation.

## #31 Cameroon — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE ELECTION/PROTEST SECURITY, POLITICAL DETENTION AND MILITARY-COURT SYSTEMS**

**Prior scope:** security, protest and political-detention apparatus.

**Evidence preserving `S`:**

- Current 2025/26 evidence describes security forces using excessive force during post-election demonstrations, with at least 48 people reported killed, hundreds arrested and political activists subjected to arbitrary detention.
- Peaceful opposition protesters from the 2020 MRC demonstrations remained imprisoned years later after military-court convictions, and the 2026 Human Rights Committee decision in Marafa v. Cameroon addressed fair-trial, ill-treatment, arbitrary-detention and detention-condition issues.

**Counter-evidence and attribution limits:**

- Dorgelesse Nguessan was released in January 2025 after an appellate reduction of her sentence, demonstrating that judicial relief can occur.
- The criminal trial concerning journalist Martinez Zogo's killing resumed, showing that accountability processes are not entirely absent.
- Significant conflict violence in the Anglophone regions is also attributable to non-State separatist groups and must not be imputed wholesale to the State.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **identified election/protest public-order operations, security units involved in arbitrary political detention or excessive/lethal force, and military-court/detention projects materially used against protected political assembly/expression**. It does not extend to Cameroon's whole State or unrelated judiciary/public services.

**Removal/narrowing trigger:** release/remedy for remaining peaceful political detainees, independent investigation/prosecution of election-period killings, end to military-court prosecution of protected civilian political activity and effective judicial review.
