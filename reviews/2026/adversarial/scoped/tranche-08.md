# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Scoped Findings, Tranche 8

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche reviews Poland, Qatar, Republic of the Congo, Slovakia, Somalia, Sri Lanka, Sudan and Syria. It applies falsification-first review with special attention to fragmentation and institutional change. Current abuse must be tied to a current attributable coercive project; reform, courts, oversight and transitional-justice mechanisms are treated as real counter-evidence; and conduct by rival armed or de facto authorities is not silently attributed to a nominal State apparatus.

## #139 Poland — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO BELARUS-BORDER ASYLUM / PUSHBACK AND ACCESS-EXCLUSION SYSTEMS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Poland's temporary restriction on lodging applications for international protection at the Belarus border remained a live State project at the cutoff. The Council of Ministers adopted another extension on 13 July 2026, after parliamentary consent. Amnesty has assessed the underlying system as effectively codifying unlawful pushbacks, and the border buffer zone remained extended through 31 August 2026.

**Counter-evidence / narrowing:** The prosecution of the Hajnówka humanitarian activists ended with a final acquittal after the prosecutor withdrew the appeal in March 2026. Polish courts and prosecutorial review therefore provide material counter-evidence against treating humanitarian-aid criminalisation or the wider justice system as currently Restricted.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **Border Guard/interior-administration projects materially refusing or bypassing individualized access to international protection, carrying out unlawful summary returns/pushbacks, or using border access-exclusion measures to materially obstruct independent humanitarian, journalistic or rights monitoring without adequate safeguards**. Humanitarian assistance, courts, prosecutorial remediation and unrelated migration/public-order functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** repeal/non-use of the asylum-suspension mechanism, individualized non-refoulement review, cessation of unlawful pushbacks, effective monitoring access and durable compliance with judicial/international protection safeguards.

## #141 Qatar — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO EXPRESSION/SURVEILLANCE ENFORCEMENT AND SPECIFIC COERCIVE MIGRANT-LABOUR FAILURES**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's current Qatar assessment continues to identify tight restrictions on expression and peaceful assembly and continuing migrant-worker vulnerability to exploitation. A June 2026 joint Amnesty/Human Rights Watch statement described intensified phone searches, arrests and restrictions on expression across GCC governments, including a climate of fear affecting migrant workers' communications.

**Counter-evidence:** Qatar has made substantial labour-law reforms. The ILO's February 2026 assessment describes a continuing 2024–2028 technical-cooperation programme built on major improvements in labour mobility, wage protection, social dialogue and labour administration. Those reforms materially defeat any claim that the pre-reform kafala architecture remains intact as a unitary coercive system.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **security/cybercrime/public-order or surveillance projects materially punishing protected expression, peaceful association or private communications, and specifically evidenced labour-administration/enforcement failures that materially enable forced-labour-like coercion, retaliation, blocked job mobility or unrecoverable wage abuse despite formal safeguards**. Qatar's labour system, employers, ministries and migrant-work framework are not Restricted generically; reform, labour-complaint and ILO-cooperation mechanisms are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** durable cessation of speech/communications repression, independent review of phone/search and cybercrime powers, measurable enforcement of labour mobility/wage safeguards and effective worker complaint/remedy without retaliation.

## #142 Republic of the Congo — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE POLITICAL-SECURITY, PUBLIC-ASSEMBLY AND DETENTION SCOPE**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's 2025/26 Congo assessment reports restrictions ahead of the 2026 presidential election, including bans on public demonstrations, continued arbitrary detention of opposition leaders and inhumane detention conditions. The evidence is current and attributable to identified political-security/public-order and detention functions.

**Counter-evidence / attribution limit:** The record does not establish that every court, ministry or public service participates in the prohibited conduct. A scoped project/apparatus finding remains more precise than a whole-State designation.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **security, police, prosecutorial or detention projects materially carrying out arbitrary political detention, suppression of protected peaceful assembly/opposition activity, serious detention ill-treatment or material obstruction of independent remedy/accountability**. Unrelated public administration, ordinary services and genuinely independent judicial/remedial functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** release or fair independent review of political detainees, sustained protection of peaceful assembly/opposition activity, independent detention monitoring and effective investigation/remedy for abuse.

## #158 Slovakia — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO ENACTED CIVIC-CONTROL / DISCRIMINATORY SYSTEMS AND SPECIFIC POLICE-ABUSE FAILURES**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Slovakia's 2025 constitutional amendments entrenched discriminatory rules affecting LGBTI people and other rights, while the NGO law imposed intrusive administrative obligations and increased State oversight. Amnesty's 2025/26 record also documents systemic Roma discrimination and continuing failures in the impartial investigation of credible police ill-treatment allegations.

**Counter-evidence:** The Slovak Ombudsperson challenged the NGO law before the Constitutional Court, demonstrating an active constitutional counter-institution. The existence of constitutional litigation and other review mechanisms makes a broad restriction of the State, legislature or police inappropriate.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **implementation/enforcement of NGO/civic-control rules materially burdening protected association or privacy; legally coercive discriminatory constitutional/administrative measures materially denying equal rights; and specific police/complaint-investigation projects where racially discriminatory violence or torture/ill-treatment is credibly alleged and effective independent investigation is materially obstructed**. The Constitutional Court, Ombudsperson and unrelated public functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** repeal/narrowing or constitutional invalidation of coercive NGO/discriminatory provisions, effective equality remedies, and demonstrably independent investigation/accountability for police ill-treatment.

## #161 Somalia — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE FEDERAL NISA/POLICE EXPRESSION, DETENTION AND COERCIVE EVICTION PROJECTS; KEEP SOMALILAND/ARMED ACTORS SEPARATE**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's 2025/26 Somalia record documents security-force attacks, harassment and arbitrary arrests of journalists and government attempts to censor reporting on insecurity and forced evictions. In April 2026 NISA officers arrested activist Sadia Moalim Ali for peaceful activism; she remained detained under extended investigative orders. The evidence supports a current federal security/public-order project rather than merely historical concern.

**Counter-evidence / fragmentation:** Somalia remains institutionally and territorially fragmented. Somaliland authorities, Al-Shabaab and other armed/de facto actors require separate attribution. Courts, civil-society organisations and journalists contest federal action and must not be collapsed into the implicated apparatus.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **federal NISA, police, information-control and other attributable federal/local security projects materially using arbitrary detention, violence, threats, censorship or prosecution against protected journalism/peaceful activism, and specifically evidenced forced-eviction operations involving unlawful coercion or denial of basic safeguards**. Somaliland, Al-Shabaab and non-attributable clan/armed actors are excluded from this State scope and require separate dossiers/designations where relevant.

**Removal trigger:** release/remedy for peaceful-expression detainees, durable protection of journalists, repeal/non-use of vague censorship directives, lawful eviction safeguards and effective independent oversight of NISA/police detention.

## #166 Sri Lanka — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE CURRENT PTA/COUNTERTERRORISM, DETENTION/TORTURE AND MINORITY-SURVEILLANCE PROJECTS; TREAT REFORM AS COUNTER-EVIDENCE**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** The new administration continued to use the Prevention of Terrorism Act while promising repeal. UN Special Procedures and Amnesty raised concerns about the proposed 2026 replacement law, including risks around arbitrary detention, privacy, defenders, torture and disappearance. Amnesty also issued a July 2026 urgent action after deaths and torture/ill-treatment concerns involving transferred prisoners, and current reporting describes continued surveillance/intimidation of war-affected Tamil communities and defenders.

**Counter-evidence:** The government has publicly committed to repeal the PTA and proposed replacement legislation; the Human Rights Commission of Sri Lanka retains `A`-status and National Preventive Mechanism functions; the Office on Missing Persons remains operational; and the government has announced broader justice reforms. These developments are significant and prevent a broad State-level finding.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **actual use of PTA/counterterrorism powers materially producing arbitrary/prolonged detention, repression of protected journalism/memorialisation/defender activity or coercive surveillance; detention/prison projects involving torture, serious ill-treatment or custody-death accountability failures; and specifically evidenced security/intelligence harassment of war-affected minorities seeking accountability**. A draft law is not Restricted merely because it is proposed; only enacted or implemented coercive provisions enter active scope. HRCSL/NPM, OMP, courts and independent reform/remedial work are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** repeal of the PTA and adoption/implementation of rights-compliant replacement law, cessation of coercive minority/defender surveillance, independent investigation of torture/deaths in custody, effective OMP/accountability outcomes and robust NPM access.

## #167 Sudan — **UPHOLD S; DEFINE SAF / STATE-ALIGNED CONFLICT AND REPRISAL APPARATUS; KEEP RSF SEPARATE**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Amnesty's April 2026 assessment documents SAF air strikes on civilian areas and reprisal attacks by SAF and allied forces against civilians and human-rights defenders perceived as RSF collaborators, alongside arbitrary detention and killings. These are current, attributable conflict projects. Amnesty's July 2026 El Fasher investigation separately documents crimes against humanity by the RSF.

**Counter-evidence / fragmentation:** Sudan's civil war is profoundly fragmented. RSF crimes, including atrocities in Darfur, cannot be silently attributed to the SAF/state-aligned apparatus. Humanitarian, civilian, medical and accountability networks likewise must not be captured by a nominal whole-State restriction.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **SAF and specifically attributable State-aligned military/security units or projects materially carrying out unlawful/indiscriminate attacks on civilians, reprisals, arbitrary detention, torture, killings or material obstruction of humanitarian/accountability activity**. RSF and other independent armed actors require separate attribution/designation; civilian institutions, emergency-response networks and independent humanitarian/remedial functions are excluded.

**Removal trigger:** verified cessation of unlawful attacks/reprisals, effective command accountability, protected humanitarian access, release/remedy for arbitrary detainees and a civilian legal order with effective independent oversight of implicated forces.

## #171 Syria — **UPHOLD S; NARROW TO IMPlicated TRANSITIONAL SECURITY/MILITARY/DETENTION PROJECTS; PRESERVE TRANSITIONAL JUSTICE EXCLUSIONS**

**Evidence preserving `S`:** Government and government-backed forces committed unlawful and extrajudicial killings of Alawite civilians during the 2025 coastal violence and Druze civilians in Suwayda, while arbitrary detention and ill-treatment remained documented under transitional security structures. EUAA's 2026 country material records continuing arbitrary arrests/detention, including in the first quarter of 2026, and serious detention conditions.

**Counter-evidence:** The transitional authorities established a National Commission for Transitional Justice and a National Commission for Missing Persons and began proceedings against former Assad-era officials. These mechanisms are directly responsive to the conduct ECL seeks to avoid and therefore must be protected from overbroad restriction. Fragmented armed control also requires actor-specific attribution.

**Adversarial finding:** `S` survives for **identified transitional-government security, military, detention or government-backed militia projects materially involved in extrajudicial/unlawful killing, arbitrary detention, torture/ill-treatment, sectarian coercive targeting or material obstruction of investigation/accountability**. Transitional-justice/missing-person mechanisms, courts/remedial functions, unrelated civilian administration, SDF/AANES and independent/non-State armed actors are excluded absent separate attribution.

**Removal trigger:** independent prosecution/remedy for coastal/Suwayda abuses, durable protection of Alawite/Druze and other communities, elimination of arbitrary detention/torture, effective civilian command control and demonstrated independence/effectiveness of transitional-justice and missing-person mechanisms.
