## Delta hypothesis D-01 — broad verbs were doing more work than the underlying rights

**Related:** #211; LAR-01, LAR-02, LAR-04, LAR-06.

### 0.2 attack

ECL 0.2 used a sound high-level conditional-grant structure in §3 but then prohibited `use`, `execute`, `deploy`, `train`, hosted functionality and similar conduct without consistently distinguishing:

1. an act that itself exercises an exclusive copyright/software right;
2. an act that implicates such a right only because technical reproduction/adaptation occurs;
3. an act that would need a separate contractual theory; and
4. an act independently permitted by statute.

US 17 U.S.C. §106 enumerates copyright-exclusive rights rather than a generic exclusive right to control every `use`. EU Directive 2009/24/EC Art. 4 and Spain Art. 99 are more directly software-specific and capture loading/running **insofar as** reproduction requiring authorization occurs, but remain subject to statutory limitations/exceptions.

### 0.3 candidate response

- defines `Licensed Rights` as only rights the particular Licensor owns/is authorized to license and for which applicable law requires Licensor authorization;
- makes §§4–6 express conditions on those rights;
- says broad operational verbs describe factual context rather than manufacture new copyright rights;
- says an activity outside Licensed Rights does not become copyright infringement merely because ECL labels it a Prohibited Use; and
- does not assume an independent contract exists outside the Licensed Rights.

### Independent-review questions

- Is `Licensed Rights` sufficiently definite in EU/Spain, US and UK public-license practice?
- Should ECL intentionally create a contractual layer for otherwise non-copyright-controlled uses, or is the current copyright-first model preferable?
- Does the condition/covenant drafting sufficiently connect each core restriction to the permission granted?
- Are any Section 5 restrictions materially ineffective in realistic technical architectures because no Licensed Right is exercised at the relevant time?

**Internal disposition:** candidate mitigation only; external review required.

## Delta hypothesis D-02 — exact Schedule identity could disappear downstream

**Related:** #209; LAR-03, LAR-08.

### 0.2 attack

Sections 2, 8 and 16 made the exact incorporated Schedule legally important, while §4 redistribution only required the exact ECL License version. Mirrors, archives, binaries, vendored copies and containers could therefore preserve `ECL-0.2` while losing the actual Schedule that supplied actor/project designations.

### 0.3 candidate response

- defines exact immutable `Schedule` and `ECL Bundle`;
- rejects mutable `latest`, branches, registry pages or channel pointers as Schedule incorporation by themselves;
- requires distributors exercising Licensed Rights to preserve the exact Schedule or immutable Bundle metadata plus cryptographic identity; and
- creates an **empty-Schedule fallback** where the Licensor's own release materials fail to identify an exact incorporated Schedule objectively, while leaving independent Section 5 conditions intact.

### Independent-review questions

- Is the empty-Schedule fallback the best legal consequence of defective incorporation, or should the stable text use a different failure rule?
- Is a cryptographic hash plus immutable manifest sufficient notice/incorporation in all required distribution modes?
- Should the exact Schedule text always travel with the Software rather than permitting a content-addressed reference?
- Does this model interact correctly with package-manager metadata, SBOMs and offline distribution?

**Internal disposition:** candidate fix for the concrete 0.2 knowability failure; external review required before #209 closes.

## Delta hypothesis D-03 — one Licensor could appear to terminate another's grant

**Related:** #210; LAR-09, LAR-10.

### 0.2 attack

§3 said `each Licensor grants You`, while §10 allowed termination after notice `from a Licensor`. With multiple owners/rightsholders, the text did not state whether A could terminate only A's grant or could somehow terminate B's independently granted rights.

### 0.3 candidate response

Adopts the conservative **per-Licensor / per-grant** model:

- a Licensor terminates only Licensed Rights that Licensor actually granted;
- an agent may act only with authority from the affected Licensor;
- merely being a different Licensor confers no termination authority;
- notice identifies breach and notifying authority;
- downstream independent direct grants are not automatically destroyed by upstream termination; and
- post-termination reinstatement is express rather than silently assumed.

### Independent-review questions

- Is per-Licensor enforcement operationally workable for a large contributor base?
- Would a reviewed contributor agreement granting centralized enforcement authority be materially stronger?
- Is the direct-grant/downstream wording consistent with applicable ownership and standing rules?
- Should reinstatement be automatic/provisional after cure rather than discretionary?

**Internal disposition:** ambiguity removed at drafting level; legal/operational model still requires qualified review before #210 closes.

## Delta hypothesis D-04 — statutory rights needed an explicit savings rule

**Related:** #211; LAR-04.

### Authority observations

Directive 2009/24/EC contains software-specific exceptions/limitations. In particular Art. 5 addresses necessary acts, backup and observation/study/testing; Art. 6 addresses qualifying interoperability decompilation. Spain implements the software regime in Arts. 99–100 RDL 1/1996. US §117 separately permits specified copies/adaptations by owners of program copies.

These regimes are not identical. ECL should not claim that a private license converts every independently lawful statutory act into copyright infringement.

### 0.3 candidate response

Section 11 states that ECL does not restrict acts permitted without Licensor authorization notwithstanding license terms or non-waivable statutory permissions. It also says such an act is not treated as an exercise of ECL-granted rights merely because Software is involved.

It deliberately does **not** resolve whether a waivable statutory default can be changed by a separately formed contract; §3 states ECL does not assume such a contract exists outside Licensed Rights.

### Independent-review questions

- Does this language preserve all intended restrictions while accurately handling EU/Spain mandatory rules?
- Is the `notwithstanding the terms of this License` formulation too broad for exceptions that are contractually waivable in some jurisdictions?
- Should jurisdiction-specific savings wording be more general and less enumerative?

**Internal disposition:** MAJOR remains open pending qualified review.

## Delta hypothesis D-05 — exhaustion required explicit separation from continuing rights

**Related:** #211; LAR-05.

Directive 2009/24/EC Art. 4(2), Spain Art. 99(c), and *UsedSoft* require serious treatment of software-distribution exhaustion. US §109 provides a distinct first-sale framework with software-specific rental limitations.

### 0.3 candidate response

Section 11 says ECL does not recreate an exhausted distribution right over a particular copy while preserving the distinction between exhausted distribution and separate reproduction/adaptation/other rights that may still require permission.

### Independent-review questions

- Does this formulation accurately cover downloaded copies in the EU after *UsedSoft* without overgeneralizing to other jurisdictions/facts?
- How does Schedule-based Restricted Party status apply to possession/use of an exhausted copy when no fresh Licensed Right is exercised?
- Should ECL documentation include explicit worked examples rather than lengthening the operative text?

**Internal disposition:** open MAJOR; candidate narrowing inserted.

## Delta hypothesis D-06 — SaaS needed provider/user decomposition

**Related:** #211; LAR-06.

### 0.2 attack

A provider can hold and operate the ECL copy while a remote customer receives only functionality/output. Treating the remote customer as if it necessarily exercised copyright rights in the Software is unsafe.

### 0.3 candidate response

Section 6 conditions the **provider's** exercise of Licensed Rights when the provider knowingly supplies restricted hosted functionality/capability. A remote consumer receiving only functionality/output is not deemed a copyright licensee solely by consuming the service.

### Independent-review questions

- Which common server/runtime operations actually exercise relevant Licensed Rights in each jurisdiction?
- Does the provider-focused language create a meaningful enforcement path for restricted hosted deployments without pretending the remote customer copied the Software?
- Is any additional contract-based network-use term desirable and legally supportable?

**Internal disposition:** open MAJOR.

## Delta hypothesis D-07 — patent silence should not masquerade as a broad technology grant

**Related:** LAR-11.

ECL 0.2 had no express patent license while describing broad software rights.

### 0.3 candidate response

Section 12 expressly grants **no patent license** and prevents the copyright/software grant from being described as an express patent grant. This is a conservative interim policy choice, not a conclusion that ECL 1.0 should permanently omit patents.

### Independent-review questions

- Is an Apache-style or GPL-style express patent grant materially preferable for adoption and reliance?
- If a patent grant is added, what contributor scope, defensive termination and Restricted Party/Project interaction are appropriate?

**Internal disposition:** deliberate 0.3 limitation; 1.0 policy decision still open.

## Delta hypothesis D-08 — severability should not assume judicial reformation is available

**Related:** LAR-15.

ECL 0.2 directed a held-unenforceable term to be interpreted/limited to the minimum extent necessary. Courts/forums do not uniformly reform private language on demand.

### 0.3 candidate response

Section 16 now conditions narrowing/reformation on applicable law permitting it, otherwise severing only the necessary affected portion.

**Internal disposition:** candidate hardening; jurisdiction review required.

## Delta hypothesis D-09 — independent regulation must stay independent

**Related:** LAR-16.

ECL is not a sanctions/export-control/public-law authorization system.

### 0.3 candidate response

Section 19 states both directions explicitly:

- ECL permission does not legalize conduct prohibited by independent regulation; and
- absence from an ECL Schedule is not regulatory clearance.

**Internal disposition:** candidate clarification; no claim of regulatory review completion.

## Internal delta conclusion

ECL 0.3-DRAFT in PR #212 is a materially stronger **candidate legal mechanism** than 0.2 because it stops relying on several hidden assumptions:

- that every factual `use` is a copyright-exclusive act;
- that a mutable registry can supply an omitted Schedule later;
- that any Licensor can terminate every other Licensor's grant;
- that a remote SaaS user necessarily exercises rights in the server software; and
- that statutory/exhausted rights can simply be relabelled as licensed permissions.

That improvement is not the same thing as legal validation. None of #207, #209, #210 or #211 should be closed solely because this internal delta exists. The next valid closure evidence is qualified independent review of the exact candidate and any subsequent delta.
