## CODEX-0.3-001 — Dynamic-Schedule retroactivity escape

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** The Schedule definition said a later Schedule did not retroactively alter an earlier release **unless that earlier release itself expressly and validly provided otherwise**. That caveat could be read to revive a dynamic/future-Schedule mechanism despite the same draft rejecting mutable `latest`/registry/channel state and despite the exact-Bundle/non-retroactivity model.

**Resolution in candidate:** The caveat was removed. A later Schedule never retroactively alters Licensed Rights or restrictions attached to an earlier release; changing Schedule requires a new exact ECL Bundle for a new Software release.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; still subject to qualified legal review under LAR-08.

## CODEX-0.3-002 — Downstream empty-Schedule laundering

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** The first empty-Schedule fallback applied whenever `the Licensor` supplied release materials without an exact Schedule. Because a downstream modifier can itself be a Licensor for its additions, a downstream release could omit inherited Schedule metadata and argue that the Schedule for the whole derivative release became empty.

**Resolution in candidate:** The fallback is strictly per original Licensor/per grant/per material/per release. It cannot erase, replace, weaken or re-resolve an upstream Bundle; downstream omission/corruption is a breach and cannot expand recipient rights.

**Further hardening (`e3f980a0da`):** CODEX-0.3-017 showed that an abstract empty Schedule still did not provide exact Bundle identity. The fallback now incorporates the canonical immutable `ECL-RP-EMPTY-1` artifact with SHA-256 `e12a6ffc03aa0be24a9f61d0d325bc8c06bb9c870416a72fcac0bfd968025aa2`, and applies only where the exact ECL License artifact/version is itself objectively identifiable. The fallback Bundle is deterministically the exact License artifact/hash plus that fixed Schedule artifact/hash; it cannot guess or substitute later mutable artifacts.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; still subject to qualified review under LAR-03/LAR-08 and #209.

## CODEX-0.3-003 — Mandatory gate references drift after renumbering

The first legal-review specification hard-coded old section numbers, so a qualified reviewer could disposition all LAR identifiers while reviewing the wrong clauses.

**Resolution:** Merged PR #215 changed the reusable gate so LAR surfaces bind to operative subjects/headings and exact candidate locations rather than hard-coded section numbers. #215 also froze review-mechanism inputs per completed review and hardened their identity/integrity validation.

## CODEX-0.3-004 — Bundle hash did not make the Schedule knowable

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Section 4 allowed a distributor to provide only an immutable Bundle manifest/hash instead of the Schedule itself. A hash can identify content without disclosing its Restricted Party/Project entries, and a repository-relative manifest path may cease to resolve after vendoring, archiving, containerization or redistribution. This recreated the notice/knowability defect the Bundle mechanism was intended to eliminate.

**Resolution in candidate (`526760d91f`):**

- redistribution must provide the exact operative Schedule in readable form with the distributed copy;
- immutable Bundle metadata/hashes are a separate verification requirement, not a substitute for Schedule content;
- an immutable content-addressed reference may substitute only if it is demonstrably retrievable independently of mutable branches, channels, registry state, repository-relative paths or later project state; and
- downstream preservation now expressly covers both each applicable upstream Schedule and Bundle.

**Further hardening (`46eae21a03`):** CODEX-0.3-013 showed that preservation duties also had to be apportioned by Licensor grant, material and Bundle. Section 4 now makes each preservation duty and breach several by grant/material/Bundle, so omitting B's Schedule or Bundle cannot create a Section 4 breach of A's otherwise-compliant grant merely because A and B material were distributed together.

**Further hardening (`e3f980a0da`):** the exceptional original-Licensor empty-Schedule path now supplies a real immutable Schedule artifact and component hashes rather than relying on an unknowable abstract empty state.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-03/LAR-08/LAR-10 and #209/#210.

## CODEX-0.3-005 — Termination notice did not identify the affected grant

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** A notice could identify a breach and an agent's general authority without identifying which Licensor, contribution/material, release/Bundle or grant was being terminated. In multi-Licensor derivatives this left `affected Software` objectively indeterminate.

**Initial resolution (`526760d91f`):** A terminating notice was required to identify the affected Licensor(s), material/contribution, release/Bundle where objectively identifiable, Licensed Rights/grant, breach, enforcement authority and cure deadline.

**Superseding resolution (`dcd6e09c88`):** CODEX-0.3-007 showed that `where objectively identifiable` still left a release/Bundle escape. The notice now must identify the affected release and exact Bundle with reasonable specificity. If those cannot yet be established, the notice is insufficient to terminate the grant until they are established. Missing/corrupted metadata cannot broaden termination scope or authorize substitution of a later or mutable Bundle.

**Further hardening (`4c65ddd022`):** CODEX-0.3-012 showed that identifying the affected grant still did not prove that the asserted breach attached to that same grant. Section 10 now requires a breach-to-grant causal mapping for each affected Licensor and Bundle; a breach of B's condition cannot terminate A's otherwise-compliant grant.

**Further hardening (`46eae21a03`):** CODEX-0.3-013 through CODEX-0.3-015 moved the upstream predicates that feed Section 10 onto the same grant/Bundle axis: preservation breaches, circumvention predicates, and Restricted Party/Project status are no longer release-global.

**Further hardening (`e3f980a0da`):** where the canonical empty-Schedule fallback defines the Bundle, Section 10 notice identity expressly includes the exact License and Schedule component hashes rather than depending on an omitted original manifest.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-09/LAR-10 and #210.

## CODEX-0.3-006 — Long-lived hosted deployments could escape the copyright hook

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** A provider can lawfully establish a long-lived or multi-tenant deployment before learning that a later customer is Restricted. If later requests are served without a new reproduction, adaptation, distribution or other Licensor-controlled act, the later restricted-context conduct may exercise no Licensed Right at all. Treating the request or generic server execution as copyright merely by definition would contradict the copyright-first design.

**Primary-law constraint preserved by the fix:** EU Directive 2009/24/EC Art. 4(1)(a) and Spain RDL 1/1996 Art. 99(a) connect loading/running/transmission/storage to the exclusive right insofar as those operations necessitate reproduction, subject to statutory limits. US 17 U.S.C. §106 likewise enumerates exclusive rights rather than a freestanding right to control every software-service use.

**Resolution in candidate:** Section 6 has two deliberately separate hooks:

1. a copyright/software-right condition covering actual Licensed Rights exercised to establish, provision, adapt, deploy, update, scale or maintain restricted hosted capability; and
2. a **Hosted-Service Covenant** for the residual long-lived-service gap, but only where applicable law finds an independently enforceable contract incorporating ECL with sufficient formation, notice, assent, authority and other required legal basis.

The text expressly states that the Hosted-Service Covenant is deliberately contractual rather than a fictional copyright hook.

**Superseding hardening (`dcd6e09c88`):** CODEX-0.3-008 showed that the earlier generic `violation of Section 6` termination trigger and Section 3's broad statement that all of Section 6 was a license condition still allowed covenant-only conduct to terminate the Licensed-Rights grant. Section 3 now expressly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant from copyright/software-right grant conditions, Section 10 excludes it from all grant-termination triggers, and covenant-only breach is limited to remedies available under the independently enforceable contract. The same conduct can trigger ECL grant termination only if it independently breaches a separate Licensed-Rights condition.

**Further hardening (`7edef02496`):** CODEX-0.3-009 showed that the opening and operative grant qualifiers in Section 3 still said the grant was broadly subject to this License, leaving a conflicting route to argue that covenant-only breach placed later Licensed-Rights acts outside the grant. Section 3 now narrows every operative grant qualifier to conditions that expressly govern Licensed Rights and expressly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant from limiting, suspending, conditioning, or terminating the grant merely by itself.

**Further hardening (`fd333a4c84`):** CODEX-0.3-010 and CODEX-0.3-011 showed two remaining indirect paths: Section 4's downstream direct-grant proviso still required compliance with all applicable ECL `terms`, and Section 10 preserved all contract remedies for covenant breach. The downstream proviso now points only to conditions expressly placed on Licensed Rights. Sections 6 and 10 now state that covenant-only remedies must not limit, suspend, condition, rescind, terminate, forfeit, or otherwise alter the existence, scope, continuity, or exercise of Licensed Rights. A separately negotiated independent agreement is not created merely by incorporating ECL and does not become an ECL termination mechanism.

**Further hardening (`46eae21a03`):** Restricted Party/Project status and the hosted-service analysis are now evaluated per exact Bundle rather than through a singular release-global Schedule. This prevents B-only designations from being imported into A's Licensed-Rights conditions while preserving the Hosted-Service Covenant as a separate, Bundle-specific contractual layer.

**Further hardening (`e3f980a0da`):** CODEX-0.3-016 showed that Bundle-specific designation predicates were insufficient if contract formation remained global. The covenant now exists for a particular Licensor/Bundle/material only when that exact Bundle's operative License text actually contains the covenant and an independently sufficient contractual basis exists for that same Licensor/Bundle/material. A-only formation or assent cannot import the covenant into B, and an older Bundle without the covenant never acquires it by co-deployment.

**Status:** the fictional copyright hook and accidental copyright-termination/forfeiture routes are removed in drafting. The Hosted-Service Covenant remains a **MAJOR legal-review surface** under LAR-02/LAR-03/LAR-06/#211 until qualified reviewers determine whether its Bundle-specific formation/remedy model is viable in the required jurisdictions.

## CODEX-0.3-007 — Release/Bundle identity could still be omitted from termination notice

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** The phrase `where objectively identifiable from the relevant release materials` allowed a termination notice to omit the exact release and Bundle precisely when release metadata was missing or corrupted. Because the same material can exist under multiple releases and Schedules, later language limiting termination to an `identified grant` could not objectively bound a dimension the notice was permitted to omit.

**Resolution in candidate (`dcd6e09c88`):** The exception is removed. Release and exact Bundle are mandatory termination-notice fields. If they cannot be established with reasonable specificity, the notice cannot terminate the grant until they are established. Missing or corrupted metadata narrows enforceability rather than broadening termination scope.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-09/LAR-10 and #210.

## CODEX-0.3-008 — Hosted-Service Covenant could still trigger copyright-grant termination

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Although Section 6 characterized the Hosted-Service Covenant as contract-only, Section 10 still terminated the copyright/software-right grant for a knowing `violation of Sections 5 or 6`, and Section 3 described all of Section 6 as a condition on Licensed Rights. A covenant-only breach could therefore still reach grant termination through the generic Section 6 path.

**Resolution in candidate (`dcd6e09c88`):**

- Section 3 now states that only Section 6's Licensed-Rights restrictions are conditions on the ECL grant and expressly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant;
- Section 6 states that covenant breach does not itself terminate a Licensed-Right grant;
- Section 10's knowing-violation trigger expressly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant;
- Section 10's catch-all applies only to other conditions on Licensed Rights; and
- covenant-only breach is limited to independently available contract remedies. If the same conduct also breaches a Licensed-Rights condition, ECL termination may proceed only on that separate license breach under the normal notice/scope/cure rules.

**Status:** resolved in drafting as an internal copyright/contract separation issue, subject to CODEX-0.3-009 through CODEX-0.3-011's further narrowing. The independent contract theory itself remains MAJOR pending qualified review under LAR-02/LAR-03/LAR-06 and #211.

## CODEX-0.3-009 — Blanket Section 3 grant qualifier still captured the Hosted-Service Covenant

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Even after Section 3 later said the Hosted-Service Covenant was not a grant condition, the opening grant remained broadly `subject to the conditions and restrictions of this License`, the operative permission remained `subject to this License`, and the use-purpose paragraph broadly referenced Section 6. Those blanket qualifiers conflicted with the later carve-out and left a plausible argument that covenant-only noncompliance placed subsequent exercises of Licensed Rights outside the grant without invoking Section 10 termination.

**Resolution in candidate (`7edef02496`):** All three grant-level qualifiers were narrowed. The opening grant is subject only to conditions expressly placed on Licensed Rights and explicitly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant. The operative permission is subject only to conditions that expressly govern Licensed Rights, and the purpose paragraph requires an independent Licensed-Rights breach rather than covenant-only noncompliance. Section 3 additionally states that the Hosted-Service Covenant does not by itself limit, suspend, condition, terminate, or remove the copyright/software-right permission.

**Status:** resolved in drafting as an internal consistency issue, subject to the downstream/remedy hardening in CODEX-0.3-010 and CODEX-0.3-011. The separate Hosted-Service Covenant remains MAJOR pending qualified review under LAR-02/LAR-03/LAR-06 and #211.

## CODEX-0.3-010 — Downstream direct-grant proviso still captured the Hosted-Service Covenant

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Section 4 allowed each downstream recipient to exercise a direct grant from each relevant Licensor only `provided the recipient independently complies with those terms`. Because `those terms` could include the independently formed Hosted-Service Covenant, covenant-only noncompliance retained an indirect route to place later exercises outside the downstream direct grant despite the explicit carve-outs in Sections 3, 6, and 10.

**Resolution in candidate (`fd333a4c84`):** The downstream proviso now requires compliance only with conditions that the applicable ECL terms expressly place on Licensed Rights. It expressly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant and says covenant-only noncompliance cannot limit, suspend, condition, terminate, forfeit, or otherwise narrow the recipient's direct Licensed-Rights grant.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-03/LAR-06 and #211.

## CODEX-0.3-011 — Contract remedies could still indirectly terminate or suspend Licensed Rights

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Section 10 limited covenant consequences to remedies available under the independently enforceable incorporating contract. If applicable contract law or the contract itself made rescission, suspension, termination, forfeiture, or comparable relief available for material covenant breach, covenant-only noncompliance could still affect the Licensed-Rights grant indirectly even though ECL's formal termination path excluded the covenant.

**Resolution in candidate (`fd333a4c84`):** Sections 6 and 10 now state that covenant-only remedies must operate without limiting, suspending, conditioning, rescinding, terminating, forfeiting, or otherwise altering the existence, scope, continuity, or exercise of Licensed Rights granted by ECL. The Hosted-Service Covenant itself does not supply or incorporate a grant-affecting contractual remedy. A separately negotiated independent agreement may have its own enforceable terms, but no such term arises merely from incorporating ECL and it does not constitute termination under ECL Section 10. If the same conduct independently breaches a Licensed-Rights condition, only that separate breach can affect the grant under Section 10's notice, scope, and cure rules.

**Status:** resolved in drafting as an internal contract/copyright separation issue. The existence, formation, enforceability, and permissible remedy set of the Hosted-Service Covenant remain MAJOR qualified-review questions under LAR-02/LAR-03/LAR-06 and #211.

## CODEX-0.3-012 — Termination was not causally bound to the Licensor grant whose condition was breached

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** In a combined release with Licensors A and B, Section 10 prevented A from terminating B's grant, but still allowed A to terminate A's own identified grant based on a violation that implicated only Licensed Rights or conditions belonging to B. The notice identified A, the release and Bundle, but the trigger did not require the asserted breach itself to attach to A's grant. That created cross-grant contamination: a breach of B's condition could be used as the factual predicate for terminating A's otherwise-compliant grant.

**Resolution in candidate (`4c65ddd022`):** Section 10 now makes breach-to-grant causality explicit throughout:

- a Licensor's grant may terminate only for a breach that independently violates a condition governing Licensed Rights granted by that same Licensor under the identified release and exact Bundle;
- the termination notice must explain how the facts attach to each affected Licensor's grant and Bundle, separately where multiple Licensors are named;
- the Section 5/6 trigger, circumvention trigger, and catch-all condition trigger each require the violated condition to govern the affected Licensor's own identified grant;
- a breach involving only another Licensor, release, or Bundle cannot terminate the affected Licensor's grant unless the same conduct independently breaches that grant's own condition; and
- the Hosted-Service Covenant same-conduct clause is likewise bound to the specific Licensor grant and Bundle whose Licensed-Rights condition was independently breached.

**Status:** resolved in drafting as an internal multi-Licensor termination-scope issue; qualified review still required under LAR-09/LAR-10 and #210.

## CODEX-0.3-013 — Bundle-preservation breach could contaminate another Licensor's grant

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** In a combined A+B distribution, omitting only B's Schedule or Bundle was a breach of Section 4 as a whole. Because Section 3 made Section 4 a condition on Licensed Rights generally, the new same-grant termination test could still treat that omission as a breach of A's Section 4 condition when the distribution also exercised A's rights.

**Resolution in candidate (`46eae21a03`):** Section 4 now applies its duties separately to each Licensor's grant, affected material, and exact Bundle. A failure to preserve or provide B's Schedule, Bundle, notice, or other required information breaches Section 4 only as to B's affected grant/material/Bundle. It does not by itself breach, condition, narrow, or terminate A's otherwise-compliant grant merely because the materials were distributed together. The upstream-preservation paragraph and downstream direct-grant language carry the same allocation.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-03/LAR-08/LAR-10 and #209/#210.

## CODEX-0.3-014 — Circumvention predicate could import another Bundle's prohibition

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Section 5.7 prohibited an arrangement whose principal purpose was to enable conduct that `another provision of this License` prohibited. If an arrangement exercised only A's rights but enabled conduct prohibited solely by B's condition or Schedule, the broad predicate could manufacture an independent Section 5.7 breach of A's grant and activate the no-cure circumvention termination path.

**Resolution in candidate (`46eae21a03`):** Section 5.7 now requires the enabled prohibition to govern the **same Licensed Rights, same Licensor grant, and same exact ECL Bundle** as the rights exercised through the circumvention arrangement. A prohibition existing only under another Licensor's grant or Bundle cannot satisfy A's circumvention condition. Section 10's no-cure trigger therefore remains downstream of the same grant/Bundle predicate rather than creating one.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-03/LAR-09/LAR-10 and #210.

## CODEX-0.3-015 — Restricted Party/Project status was release-global instead of Bundle-specific

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** The definitions resolved Restricted Party and Restricted Project through the singular Schedule of the `applicable Software release`. A combined release containing A material under Bundle A and B material under Bundle B could therefore import a B-only designation into A's no-grant and Section 6 conditions, bypassing termination entirely for the initial withholding rule and contaminating A's grant for later restrictions.

**Resolution in candidate (`46eae21a03`):** The model is now explicitly grant- and Bundle-relative throughout:

- `Restricted Party`, `Covered Associate`, and `Restricted Project` resolve against the exact Bundle governing the particular Licensor's grant and Licensed Rights at issue;
- the Schedule and Bundle definitions expressly allow a combined Software release to contain multiple Bundles and prevent one Bundle from governing another grant by co-location;
- Sections 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 18 carry the same per-grant/per-Bundle semantics;
- the Section 6 no-grant rule and Restricted Project/provider conditions use only status determined under the exact Bundle governing the affected Licensor's rights; and
- the Hosted-Service Covenant is evaluated separately by Bundle as an independent contractual layer without importing one Bundle's designation into another Licensor's Licensed Rights.

**Status:** resolved in drafting as an internal multi-Licensor/Bundle-scope issue; qualified review still required under LAR-02/LAR-03/LAR-06/LAR-08/LAR-10 and #209/#210/#211.

## CODEX-0.3-016 — Covenant formation could leak across Licensors and Bundles

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** After CODEX-0.3-015, the covenant's designation predicate was evaluated per Bundle, but the formation hook still began from one generic independently enforceable contract incorporating ECL. In a combined deployment, assent sufficient for Licensor A/Bundle A could therefore cause the covenant to be evaluated against B's material and Bundle even when no contractual basis existed for B or B's older operative License text did not contain the Hosted-Service Covenant.

**Resolution in candidate (`e3f980a0da`):** The Hosted-Service Covenant is now explicitly Bundle-specific. For a particular Licensor/Bundle/material it applies only if the operative License text in that exact Bundle actually contains the covenant **and** applicable law recognizes an independently sufficient contractual basis for that same Licensor/Bundle/material. Formation, notice, assent, authority, consideration, acceptance of ECL-granted rights, or another contractual basis associated only with A does not establish B's covenant. A Bundle whose operative License text lacks the covenant never acquires it merely through combined hosting. Sections 3, 6, 10, 11 and 18 carry the same separation.

**Status:** resolved in drafting as an internal formation-scope issue. The underlying contract-formation/enforceability/remedy theory remains MAJOR for qualified review under LAR-02/LAR-03/LAR-06 and #211.

## CODEX-0.3-017 — Empty-Schedule fallback lacked an exact immutable Bundle identity

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Deeming an unidentified Schedule's contents empty did not satisfy ECL's own Bundle identity model. The Bundle definition requires exact immutable License and Schedule components with cryptographic identity, while the fallback supplied neither a canonical empty Schedule artifact nor a deterministic Bundle identity. A recipient or termination notice could therefore still be unable to identify the exact grant state supposedly created by the fallback.

**Resolution in candidate (`0e49b4704b`, `e3f980a0da`):** A canonical immutable Schedule artifact now exists at `schedules/ECL-RP-EMPTY-1.md`, identifier `ECL-RP-EMPTY-1`, SHA-256 `e12a6ffc03aa0be24a9f61d0d325bc8c06bb9c870416a72fcac0bfd968025aa2`. The original-Licensor fallback applies only when the exact ECL License artifact/version is objectively identifiable. The fallback Bundle is deterministically the pair of that exact License artifact/version and SHA-256 plus the canonical empty Schedule identifier and SHA-256. Those component identities constitute equivalent immutable release metadata when the original publisher omitted a manifest. If the License artifact itself cannot be established, the fallback cannot guess or substitute a later/mutable License or Schedule. Section 10 termination notices must identify the two component hashes for such a fallback Bundle.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-03/LAR-08/LAR-09/LAR-10 and #209/#210.

## Result

Across eight adversarial passes Codex identified **seventeen P1 findings**. The process has exposed and patched mutable-Schedule retroactivity, downstream Bundle laundering, brittle legal-gate references, Schedule knowability, termination-scope ambiguity, a long-lived SaaS copyright gap, residual release/Bundle ambiguity in termination, accidental copyright termination for a contract-only covenant, residual grant-level capture of that covenant, downstream direct-grant capture, an indirect contract-remedy route back into the Licensed-Rights grant, cross-grant termination contamination between Licensors, cross-grant Section 4 preservation contamination, cross-Bundle circumvention predicates, release-global Restricted Party/Project status, cross-Bundle covenant formation, and non-deterministic empty-Schedule fallback identity.

All seventeen now have explicit candidate resolutions or, for the hosted-service contract layer, an intentionally exposed legal theory rather than a hidden copyright assumption.

These automated dispositions do not close #207, #209, #210 or #211. The next valid closure evidence is qualified independent review of the exact content-addressed candidate and any subsequent delta.
