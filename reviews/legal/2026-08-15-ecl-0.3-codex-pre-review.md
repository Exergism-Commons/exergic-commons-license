## CODEX-0.3-001 — Dynamic-Schedule retroactivity escape

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** The Schedule definition said a later Schedule did not retroactively alter an earlier release **unless that earlier release itself expressly and validly provided otherwise**. That caveat could be read to revive a dynamic/future-Schedule mechanism despite the same draft rejecting mutable `latest`/registry/channel state and despite the exact-Bundle/non-retroactivity model.

**Resolution in candidate:** The caveat was removed. A later Schedule never retroactively alters Licensed Rights or restrictions attached to an earlier release; changing Schedule requires a new exact ECL Bundle for a new Software release.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; still subject to qualified legal review under LAR-08.

## CODEX-0.3-002 — Downstream empty-Schedule laundering

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** The first empty-Schedule fallback applied whenever `the Licensor` supplied release materials without an exact Schedule. Because a downstream modifier can itself be a Licensor for its additions, a downstream release could omit inherited Schedule metadata and argue that the Schedule for the whole derivative release became empty.

**Resolution in candidate:** The fallback is strictly per original Licensor/per grant/per material/per release. It cannot erase, replace, weaken or re-resolve an upstream Bundle; downstream omission/corruption is a breach and cannot expand recipient rights.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; still subject to qualified review under LAR-03/LAR-08 and #209.

## CODEX-0.3-003 — Mandatory gate references drift after renumbering

**Severity:** P1 / release-process BLOCKER

**Attack:** ECL 0.3 inserted statutory-rights, patent and trademark sections, shifting warranty/liability/severability/exact-Bundle section numbers. The first legal-review specification hard-coded old section numbers, so a qualified reviewer could disposition all LAR identifiers while reviewing the wrong clauses.

**Resolution:** Merged PR #215 changed the reusable gate so LAR surfaces bind to operative subjects/headings and exact candidate locations rather than hard-coded section numbers. #215 also froze review-mechanism inputs per completed review and hardened their identity/integrity validation.

**Status:** resolved in merged review-process infrastructure; still subject to qualified use of that process for the exact stable candidate.

## CODEX-0.3-004 — Bundle hash did not make the Schedule knowable

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Section 4 allowed a distributor to provide only an immutable Bundle manifest/hash instead of the Schedule itself. A hash can identify content without disclosing its Restricted Party/Project entries, and a repository-relative manifest path may cease to resolve after vendoring, archiving, containerization or redistribution. This recreated the notice/knowability defect the Bundle mechanism was intended to eliminate.

**Resolution in candidate (`526760d91f`):**

- redistribution must provide the exact operative Schedule in readable form with the distributed copy;
- immutable Bundle metadata/hashes are a separate verification requirement, not a substitute for Schedule content;
- an immutable content-addressed reference may substitute only if it is demonstrably retrievable independently of mutable branches, channels, registry state, repository-relative paths or later project state; and
- downstream preservation now expressly covers both each applicable upstream Schedule and Bundle.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-03/LAR-08 and #209.

## CODEX-0.3-005 — Termination notice did not identify the affected grant

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** A notice could identify a breach and an agent's general authority without identifying which Licensor, contribution/material, release/Bundle or grant was being terminated. In multi-Licensor derivatives this left `affected Software` objectively indeterminate.

**Initial resolution (`526760d91f`):** A terminating notice was required to identify the affected Licensor(s), material/contribution, release/Bundle where objectively identifiable, Licensed Rights/grant, breach, enforcement authority and cure deadline.

**Superseding resolution (`dcd6e09c88`):** CODEX-0.3-007 showed that `where objectively identifiable` still left a release/Bundle escape. The notice now must identify the affected release and exact Bundle with reasonable specificity. If those cannot yet be established, the notice is insufficient to terminate the grant until they are established. Missing/corrupted metadata cannot broaden termination scope or authorize substitution of a later or mutable Bundle.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-09/LAR-10 and #210.

## CODEX-0.3-006 — Long-lived hosted deployments could escape the copyright hook

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** A provider can lawfully establish a long-lived or multi-tenant deployment before learning that a later customer is Restricted. If later requests are served without a new reproduction, adaptation, distribution or other Licensor-controlled act, the later restricted-context conduct may exercise no Licensed Right at all. Treating the request or generic server execution as copyright merely by definition would contradict the copyright-first design.

**Primary-law constraint preserved by the fix:** EU Directive 2009/24/EC Art. 4(1)(a) and Spain RDL 1/1996 Art. 99(a) connect loading/running/transmission/storage to the exclusive right insofar as those operations necessitate reproduction, subject to statutory limits. US 17 U.S.C. §106 likewise enumerates exclusive rights rather than a freestanding right to control every software-service use.

**Resolution in candidate:** Section 6 has two deliberately separate hooks:

1. a copyright/software-right condition covering actual Licensed Rights exercised to establish, provision, adapt, deploy, update, scale or maintain restricted hosted capability; and
2. a **Hosted-Service Covenant** for the residual long-lived-service gap, but only where applicable law finds an independently enforceable contract incorporating ECL with sufficient formation, notice, assent, authority and other required legal basis.

The text expressly states that the Hosted-Service Covenant is deliberately contractual rather than a fictional copyright hook.

**Superseding hardening (`dcd6e09c88`):** CODEX-0.3-008 showed that the earlier generic `violation of Section 6` termination trigger and Section 3's broad statement that all of Section 6 was a license condition still allowed covenant-only conduct to terminate the Licensed-Rights grant. Section 3 now expressly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant from copyright/software-right grant conditions, Section 10 excludes it from all grant-termination triggers, and covenant-only breach is limited to remedies available under the independently enforceable contract. The same conduct can trigger ECL grant termination only if it independently breaches a separate Licensed-Rights condition.

**Further hardening (`7edef02496`):** CODEX-0.3-009 showed that the opening and operative grant qualifiers in Section 3 still said the grant was broadly subject to this License, leaving a conflicting route to argue that covenant-only breach placed later Licensed-Rights acts outside the grant. Section 3 now narrows every operative grant qualifier to conditions that expressly govern Licensed Rights and expressly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant from limiting, suspending, conditioning, or terminating the grant merely by itself.

**Status:** the fictional copyright hook and accidental copyright-termination/forfeiture routes are removed. The Hosted-Service Covenant remains a **MAJOR legal-review surface** under LAR-02/LAR-03/LAR-06/#211 until qualified reviewers determine whether its formation/remedy model is viable in the required jurisdictions.

## CODEX-0.3-007 — Release/Bundle identity could still be omitted from termination notice

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** The phrase `where objectively identifiable from the relevant release materials` allowed a termination notice to omit the exact release and Bundle precisely when release metadata was missing or corrupted. Because the same material can exist under multiple releases and Schedules, later language limiting termination to an `identified grant` could not objectively bound a dimension the notice was permitted to omit.

**Resolution in candidate (`dcd6e09c88`):** The exception is removed. Release and exact Bundle are mandatory termination-notice fields. If they cannot be established with reasonable specificity, the notice cannot terminate the grant until they are established. Missing or corrupted metadata narrows enforceability rather than broadening termination scope.

**Status:** resolved in drafting; qualified review still required under LAR-09/LAR-10 and #210.

## CODEX-0.3-008 — Hosted-Service Covenant could still trigger copyright-grant termination

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Although Section 6 characterized the Hosted-Service Covenant as contract-only, Section 10 still terminated the copyright/software-right grant for a knowing `violation of Sections 5 or 6`, and Section 3 described all of Section 6 as a condition on Licensed Rights. A covenant-only breach could therefore still reach grant termination through the generic Section 6 path.

**Resolution in candidate (`dcd6e09c88`):**

- Section 3 now states that only Section 6's Licensed-Rights restrictions are conditions on the ECL grant and expressly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant;
- Section 6 states that covenant breach does not itself terminate a Licensed-Right grant;
- Section 10's knowing-violation trigger expressly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant;
- Section 10's catch-all applies only to other conditions on Licensed Rights; and
- covenant-only breach is limited to independently available contract remedies. If the same conduct also breaches a Licensed-Rights condition, ECL termination may proceed only on that separate license breach under the normal notice/scope/cure rules.

**Status:** resolved in drafting as an internal copyright/contract separation issue, subject to CODEX-0.3-009's further narrowing of Section 3. The independent contract theory itself remains MAJOR pending qualified review under LAR-02/LAR-03/LAR-06 and #211.

## CODEX-0.3-009 — Blanket Section 3 grant qualifier still captured the Hosted-Service Covenant

**Severity:** P1 / candidate BLOCKER

**Attack:** Even after Section 3 later said the Hosted-Service Covenant was not a grant condition, the opening grant remained broadly `subject to the conditions and restrictions of this License`, the operative permission remained `subject to this License`, and the use-purpose paragraph broadly referenced Section 6. Those blanket qualifiers conflicted with the later carve-out and left a plausible argument that covenant-only noncompliance placed subsequent exercises of Licensed Rights outside the grant without invoking Section 10 termination.

**Resolution in candidate (`7edef02496`):** All three grant-level qualifiers were narrowed. The opening grant is subject only to conditions expressly placed on Licensed Rights and explicitly excludes the Hosted-Service Covenant. The operative permission is subject only to conditions that expressly govern Licensed Rights, and the purpose paragraph requires an independent Licensed-Rights breach rather than covenant-only noncompliance. Section 3 additionally states that the Hosted-Service Covenant does not by itself limit, suspend, condition, terminate, or remove the copyright/software-right permission.

**Status:** resolved in drafting as an internal consistency issue. The separate Hosted-Service Covenant remains MAJOR pending qualified review under LAR-02/LAR-03/LAR-06 and #211.
