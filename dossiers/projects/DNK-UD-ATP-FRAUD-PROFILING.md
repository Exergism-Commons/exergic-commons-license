# Udbetaling Danmark / ATP automated fraud-risk profiling systems

> **Governance record only.** This dossier has no licensing effect by itself. It projectizes the already-reviewed Denmark system-specific scope; it does not make ATP, Udbetaling Danmark or Danish welfare administration generally restricted.

## 1. Current determination

**S — Scoped project-family candidate.** The 2026 Denmark adversarial review preserved a finding only for specified Udbetaling Danmark / ATP automated fraud/risk-profiling models and materially connected workflows where discriminatory, opaque or disproportionate surveillance effects remain unremediated.

This dossier does not convert the entire welfare-administration technology stack into one Restricted Project.

## 2. Exact project identity and boundary

The tracked object is the **reviewed family of Udbetaling Danmark / ATP automated fraud-control and risk-profiling models** used to flag welfare recipients for investigation, together with materially connected data-processing workflows documented by the cited technical review.

Membership is intentionally narrower than `all ATP systems` or `all Danish welfare algorithms`. A future Schedule would need to enumerate exact models/versions/workflows or supply an objectively knowable inclusion rule; this dossier does not pretend the repository currently contains a complete per-model inventory.

## 3. ECL criteria engaged

The existing State review tests the project family for disproportionate/repressive data surveillance, discriminatory profiling, opacity/non-contestability and reduction of meaningful agency in welfare administration.

## 4. Material participants and roles

The canonical Denmark review identifies Udbetaling Danmark / ATP as the institutions operating the reviewed fraud/risk-profiling architecture. This dossier does not create semantic `controls`, `operates` or supplier-participation Claims; those relationships require separate curation if needed.

## 5. Evidence supporting the determination

Amnesty's technical investigation, as summarized in the canonical Denmark dossier and scoped adversarial review, documents dozens of data-driven fraud-control algorithms used to flag welfare recipients, including models using foreign affiliation/citizenship and atypical household patterns, with mass-data processing, opacity and discrimination risks.

The reviewed record also states that the authorities declined requests for collaborative audit/full code and data access, and that the adversarial pass did not locate authoritative evidence that the implicated architecture had been terminated or independently cleared by the 2026-08-11 cutoff.

## 6. Counter-evidence / alternative explanations

Danish authorities dispute parts of Amnesty's legal/technical characterization, including whether the systems constitute prohibited social scoring under the EU AI Act. Denmark also has ordinary judicial/administrative review and EU data-protection/AI-law safeguards.

Those counter-institutions are why this remains a narrowly scoped project-family record rather than an inference about Denmark or ATP generally.

## 7. Attribution and exclusions

Excluded absent separate evidence: unrelated ATP/Udbetaling Danmark systems, ordinary Danish welfare administration, employees by identity, vendors, beneficiaries and any model independently retired/redesigned and validated as remediated.

No governance outcome propagates through a semantic relation to any actor.

## 8. Adversarial determination

**Preserve the already-reviewed system-specific `S` boundary; reject organization-wide or State-wide inheritance.** The project dossier is an entry point for the reviewed system family, not a shortcut around exact model/version knowability required for a future Schedule.

## 9. Review / removal triggers

Independently auditable retirement/redesign of implicated models, validated non-discrimination/proportionality assessment, adequate notice/contestability and effective remediation for affected persons.
