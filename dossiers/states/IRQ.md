# Iraq

> **Governance record only.** This dossier does not itself create licensing restrictions. Only a versioned Schedule expressly incorporated by a software release can have operative effect.

## 1. Current determination

**S — Restricted with a newly frozen narrow subset.** Current 2026 evidence supports one objectively knowable project boundary around the Al-Karkh handling of the cohort transferred from northeast-Syrian detention facilities. The restriction is limited to materially participating abusive custodial/interrogation capacities; Iraq and its detention/counterterrorism systems are not restricted generically.

## 2. ECL criteria engaged

- torture and ill-treatment;
- coercive interrogation / coerced confession;
- disappearance-like concealment or denial of basic custody safeguards;
- destruction of meaningful contestability through abusive detention practices.

## 3. Evidence supporting the determination

The Iraqi Supreme Judicial Council reported in 2026 that **5,704 people from 61 nationalities** had been transferred from Syrian detention facilities and that the Karkh First Investigation Court was conducting the investigations. It also documented a designated investigation site and reported that more than 500 people had been interrogated by mid-February, with 157 juveniles separated for juvenile procedures.

Human Rights Watch warned in February 2026 that the transferred cohort faced risks of enforced disappearance, torture or ill-treatment and unfair proceedings. Separately, lawyers for French transferees reported specific allegations of coercive interrogation and torture, including strangulation, suspension by handcuffs and threats of sexual violence. Later 2026 human-rights reporting continued to question the basis and safeguards surrounding transferred detainees, supporting treatment of the project as current rather than historical.

## 4. Counter-evidence and exergic institutions

The same official record contains material safeguards and remediation evidence. Juveniles were separated for specialized handling, and in April 2026 Iraq transferred a Finnish juvenile and a United States national onward after investigators found no evidence of ISIS membership. Iraq continues treaty-body engagement and maintains courts, prosecutors and administrative mechanisms capable of review.

Those facts defeat any whole-prison, whole-court, whole-security-sector or whole-State restriction.

## 5. Frozen attribution and exclusions

The renderable subset is recorded in `../../registry/schedule-state-s-freezes/batch-22b-irq-freeze.yml`.

The frozen project is limited to the Al-Karkh Central Prison / designated Karkh investigation-site functions handling the 2025-26 transferred cohort **only where a person or function Materially Participates in torture/ill-treatment, coerced-confession extraction, concealment or material denial of basic safeguards**.

Expressly excluded are Iraq, the Ministry of Justice, Supreme Judicial Council, Counter-Terrorism Service, Al-Karkh Central Prison and Iraqi corrections/security functions generally; lawful individualized custody/investigation/prosecution; juvenile protection and rehabilitation; medical care; defence counsel; repatriation or release following genuine review; monitoring/remediation; and personnel without Material Participation in the frozen abusive capacities.

## 6. Adversarial determination

**UPHOLD / NARROW `S`.** The transferred-detainee project supplies a finite institutional/time/cohort boundary that the earlier generic disappearance record lacked. The freeze records the abusive project capacity and contemporary allegations; it does not adjudicate individual criminal responsibility or treat alleged ISIS affiliation, detention or prosecution as restricted per se.

## 7. Review / removal triggers

- credible independent investigation materially rejecting the torture/coercive-interrogation attribution;
- demonstrated safeguards/remediation eliminating the frozen abusive capacities;
- closure, transfer or material redesign of the transferred-detainee project; or
- authoritative findings materially changing institutional attribution.
