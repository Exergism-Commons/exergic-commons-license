# Jordan

> **Governance record only.** This dossier does not itself create licensing restrictions. Only a versioned Schedule expressly incorporated by a software release has operative effect.

## 1. Current determination

**S — Restricted with defined scope.** Current evidence supports a precise restriction of expression-punishment, administrative-detention, cybercrime and media-blocking projects, not Jordan's State apparatus generally.

## 2. ECL criteria engaged

- political repression of protected expression and peaceful assembly;
- arbitrary detention without meaningful contestability;
- coercive information control / media blocking;
- use of vague cybercrime or security provisions to suppress protected civic activity.

## 3. Evidence supporting the determination

Amnesty International's 2025/26 record documents arbitrary restrictions on expression and assembly, thousands of people in governor-ordered administrative detention without charge or access to an ordinary judicial body to challenge legality, and blocking of 12 media websites by the Media Commission. Ayman Sanduka remained imprisoned in 2026 after a five-year conviction tied to a critical Facebook post; Amnesty reported in May 2026 that his cassation appeal had been rejected.

These facts demonstrate material deployment rather than a restriction inferred from statutory text alone.

## 4. Counter-evidence and exergic institutions

Jordan retains ordinary courts, parliament and the National Centre for Human Rights (NCHR). In 2026 the NCHR publicly reaffirmed constitutional protection of privacy, opinion, expression and press freedom and the role of a free press in accountability. Political representation also persists outside the banned Muslim Brotherhood organization. These institutions and functions weigh strongly against whole-State attribution.

## 5. Attribution and exclusions

Covered scope follows State Security Court/prosecutorial/security projects materially punishing protected expression or peaceful assembly; governor-administered detention lacking meaningful judicial review; cybercrime enforcement used against protected speech; and Media Commission blocking based on vague political-expression grounds.

Excluded: the NCHR, parliament, ordinary courts providing independent remedy, unrelated administration and Jordanian people/nationality.

## 6. Adversarial determination

**UPHOLD / NARROW S.** The active detention/expression record supports restriction, but functioning counter-institutions and political/legal contestability require precise project-level attribution.

## 7. Review / removal triggers

- release/remedy in expression-only detention cases;
- effective ordinary judicial review of administrative detention;
- narrowing or rights-compliant application of vague cybercrime/public-order offences;
- durable cessation or independent judicial review of politically motivated media blocking.
