# Russia

> **Governance record only.** This dossier does not itself create licensing restrictions.

## 1. Current determination

**R — State apparatus Restricted**, including domestic coercive-security systems and military/occupation systems connected to Ukraine.

## 2. ECL criteria engaged

Coercive targeting/unlawful violence, occupation-related domination, arbitrary detention, torture and enforced disappearance, repression of dissent, surveillance and coerced legal/political assimilation.

## 3. Evidence supporting the determination

OHCHR reported in March 2026 that Russian authorities systematically imposed discriminatory and coercive measures in occupied Ukrainian territory, including forced transfer/deportation, citizenship coercion, conscription, detention, torture and ill-treatment. UN Special Procedures continued to raise arbitrary-detention and reprisal cases involving defenders inside Russia. The relevant conduct spans military, security, judicial, detention and administrative institutions.

## 4. Counter-evidence and exergic institutions

Russia maintains a Constitutional Court and formal legal-remedy structures; 2026 legal changes included expansion of some free legal-aid eligibility and regulation of restraint use in detention. These formal mechanisms are material evidence but the review did not establish sufficiently effective restraint of the conduct underlying the designation.

## 5. Attribution and exclusions

The finding applies to the State apparatus and materially connected projects, not Russian people, nationality, language, culture, or independent private activity.

## 6. Adversarial determination

**UPHOLD R.** Formal legal mechanisms did not rebut the breadth, severity and persistence of domestic and occupation-related coercion.

## 7. Review / removal triggers

Cessation of occupation-related unlawful conduct, verified withdrawal/remediation, release and remedy for political detainees, effective prosecution of torture/disappearance, restoration of independent civic/political space, and evidence that courts can materially constrain security/military systems.
