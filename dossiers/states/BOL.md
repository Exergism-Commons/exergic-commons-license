# Bolivia

> **Governance record only.** No licensing effect by itself.

## 1. Current determination

**U — Under Review.** The 2026 conflict record remains serious, but the evidence currently available does not support retaining a stable ECL `S` merely from arrests, injuries, emergency powers or military/police deployment. A qualifying ECL restriction requires a specifically attributable project involving unlawful force, arbitrary detention or comparable coercive suppression.

## 2. ECL criteria potentially engaged

- unlawful coercive targeting or public-order force against protected peaceful activity;
- arbitrary detention;
- systematic coercive suppression of protected protest.

The existence of blockade-clearing operations or a state of exception is not independently sufficient.

## 3. Evidence requiring continued review

During the May–June 2026 social conflict, the Defensoría del Pueblo documented large numbers of arrests and injuries and monitored police/military operations, including a 6 June operation in San Julián where civilians and police were injured. The IACHR expressed concern on 2 June about the human-rights impacts of the escalating conflict and called for protection of legitimate protest.

Those facts justify continued review, but they do not by themselves establish that the relevant State project crossed the ECL prohibited-use threshold.

## 4. Material counter-evidence

Bolivia declared a state of exception through Supreme Decree No. 5636 on 20 June 2026. The Defensoría conducted sustained monitoring during its implementation. Subsequent monitoring reported that most 20–21 June blockade-clearance operations occurred without use of force, and by 19 July the Defensoría stated that it had not identified human-rights violations or arbitrary detentions arising from application of the state of exception.

This later evidence directly weakens the inference used to escalate the dossier from `U` to `S`.

## 5. Attribution and exclusions

No State institution, police service, military body or emergency measure is currently Restricted through this dossier. Courts, the Defensoría, independent monitoring and ordinary public-order activity are outside any future scope unless separately evidenced.

Any future `S` finding must identify a specific operation, unit, detention project or coercive measure and establish the qualifying conduct rather than infer it from emergency deployment alone.

## 6. Adversarial determination

**DOWNGRADE `S → U`.** Reversibility requires crediting later independent monitoring when it materially undermines the factual premise of an earlier escalation. The May–June conflict remains reviewable, but a stable project-level restriction is not presently established with sufficient precision.

## 7. Review / escalation triggers

Escalate toward `S` only on authoritative evidence of a current attributable operation involving unlawful force, arbitrary detention, torture, enforced disappearance, political repression or another ECL 0.2 criterion. Move toward `N` on sustained non-recurrence, completion of review/remedy for disputed incidents and continued rights-compliant public-order practice.
