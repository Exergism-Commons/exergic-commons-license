# Moldova

> Governance record only; no licensing effect by itself. Only a versioned Schedule expressly incorporated by a software release can have operative effect.

## 1. Current determination

**S — Restricted with defined scope.** Current scope is limited to persistent pretrial-detention and detention/ill-treatment/remedy failures in attributable criminal-justice projects. A separately frozen subset now covers the current informal-prisoner-hierarchy control problem in three CPT-inspected penitentiaries; it does not restrict Moldova's penitentiary or justice system generally.

## 2. ECL criteria engaged

- arbitrary or insufficiently contestable deprivation of liberty;
- torture/ill-treatment, degrading treatment and coercive targeting;
- forced labour or coercion enabled through materially tolerated custodial power structures;
- ineffective investigation and remedy that materially preserves coercive conduct.

## 3. Evidence supporting the determination

Council of Europe work in June 2026 records that repeated European Court judgments identified a pattern of violations concerning pretrial detention and that the lack of relevant and sufficient reasons in decisions ordering or extending detention remains a **systemic problem**. It also identifies unresolved enhanced-supervision cases concerning ill-treatment/torture to extract confessions and ineffective investigation/remedy.

A second, independently current detention basis is now frozen. During its June 2025 ad hoc visit, the CPT visited Penitentiary no. 6 in Soroca, no. 15 in Cricova and no. 2 in Lipcani. It reported that long-standing recommendations on informal prisoner hierarchies remained largely unimplemented in all three establishments, with intimidation and violence, serious concern for prisoners in the lowest caste, and de facto relinquishment of authority/control to informal prison leaders among the identified systemic drivers.

On 5 March 2026, the European Court in **Petrov v. the Republic of Moldova (no. 38066/18)** found violations arising from degrading treatment, discrimination and forced labour linked to the informal prisoner hierarchy and the authorities' failure to protect the applicant. On 20 July 2026, the Council of Europe's Department for the Execution of Judgments described the informal-hierarchy problem as systemic and underlined the need for a comprehensive national strategy, supporting continued current-status concern rather than treating the 2025 CPT findings as already remediated.

## 4. Counter-evidence and exergic institutions

Moldova is actively participating in Council of Europe criminal-justice and penitentiary reform. The CPT received no allegations of physical ill-treatment by staff in the three establishments visited in 2025, noted improvements in food and injury reporting, and recorded the authorities' SAFE Roadmap to Europeanisation of Moldovan Prisons. The Ombudsman/National Preventive Mechanism remains an active oversight channel. A 2026 journalist-safety action plan and other media reforms provide substantial counter-evidence against retaining the first-pass broad media-regulatory scope.

These measures matter: the Schedule freeze therefore follows only materially participating custody/administration functions in the specifically documented hierarchy-tolerance/control project, and expressly expires as to facility-specific practices independently shown durably remediated.

## 5. Attribution and exclusions

Scope follows **pretrial-detention practices materially lacking individualized necessity/proportionality**, **identified law-enforcement/detention projects involving ill-treatment/torture or ineffective investigation/remedy**, and the specifically frozen **informal-prisoner-hierarchy control project at Penitentiary no. 2 Lipcani, no. 6 Soroca and no. 15 Cricova** where custody/administration functions materially tolerate, rely on or relinquish control to informal prisoner leaders in a way that enables the documented coercive conditions.

The broad media-regulatory component is removed from current scope. Conduct in Transnistria is not attributed to Moldova absent independent attribution evidence. Courts performing genuine remediation, the Ombudsman/NPM, health care, rehabilitation, legal defence, inspection and reform/accountability projects are excluded. No generic Moldovan prison, police, prosecutor or judicial class follows from this dossier.

## 6. Adversarial determination

**UPHOLD / NARROW S.** The detention/ill-treatment basis remains current. The Petrov judgment and the 2025 CPT findings, still treated as a systemic execution problem in July 2026, justify one tightly bounded penitentiary subset. Evidence of active reform and media-rights safeguards defeats broader State-level, justice-sector or penitentiary-system attribution.

## 7. Review / removal triggers

Review on durable compliance with individualized pretrial-detention reasoning; effective investigation/prosecution/remedy for torture or ill-treatment; implementation of European Court judgments; sustained operation of independent preventive oversight; and facility-specific evidence that the informal prisoner hierarchy no longer exercises coercive control with material custodial tolerance or reliance.
