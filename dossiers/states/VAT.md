# Holy See

> Governance record only; no licensing effect by itself.

## 1. Current determination

**N — No current State-level or defined-project basis.** Clerical-abuse accountability and transparency failures are grave, but the current record does not establish a materially deployed Holy See State project satisfying ECL §5.

## 2. ECL criteria tested

The review tested repressive surveillance, unlawful software-enabled targeting, automated discriminatory domination, deceptive population manipulation and coercive technological/informational capture.

## 3. Evidence requiring scrutiny

The 2026 record continues to raise serious questions about transparency, victim participation and accountability in canonical abuse proceedings. Those institutional failures remain reviewable but are not automatically equivalent to an ECL prohibited-use project.

## 4. Counter-evidence and exergic institutions

The Holy See continues abuse proceedings, safeguarding reforms and survivor engagement, including 2026 meetings and public commitments to reparative action. Their adequacy remains contested, but they constitute contrary evidence against treating concealment as a unitary current prohibited project.

## 5. Attribution and exclusions

This dossier concerns the Holy See as a State/governing apparatus, not the Catholic Church globally, Catholics, clergy generally or independent local accountability work.

## 6. Adversarial determination

**UPHOLD `N`.** Current evidence does not establish the project-specific ECL nexus required for restriction.

## 7. Review triggers

Reopen on evidence of technology-enabled systematic concealment, retaliatory surveillance/targeting of survivors or investigators, or coercive information-control systems primarily designed to prevent meaningful accountability.
