# Cyprus

> Governance record only; no licensing effect by itself.

## 1. Current determination

**U — Under Review.** Prior pushback allegations remain serious, but the current Schedule review did not identify a sufficiently fresh 2026 operation/project to preserve `S` solely through historical continuity.

## 2. Potential ECL criteria

A named maritime return could engage ECL 0.2 where it materially implements summary return, unlawful force or denial of meaningful individualized protection. A named public-order project could separately qualify if protected assembly is coercively suppressed.

## 3. Evidence requiring continued review

UN records document serious allegations of sea/Green-Line pushbacks, force, arbitrary detention and lack of procedural guarantees. The best frozen communications currently available concern 2021 and 2024 operations.

## 4. Counter-evidence and attribution limits

Judicial and inquest mechanisms remain operative, and earlier civil-society remedies demonstrate contestability. The current record does not justify converting all maritime/border enforcement into a continuing Restricted Project without a fresh attributable operation.

## 5. Attribution and exclusions

No Cyprus border, maritime, police or migration institution is currently Schedule-ready from this dossier. Courts, independent remedies, humanitarian activity and ordinary lawful border/migration administration are excluded.

## 6. Adversarial determination

**DOWNGRADE `S → U`.** Re-escalation requires a current attributable operation or a materially continuous unremediated project established with evidence specific enough for Schedule knowability.

## 7. Review triggers

Escalate on authoritative evidence of new summary returns, force, arbitrary detention or denial of individualized protection. Move toward `N` on sustained non-recurrence, effective accountability and rights-compliant border practice.
