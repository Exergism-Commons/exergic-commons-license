# Colombia

> **Governance record only.** No licensing effect by itself.

## 1. Current determination

**U — Monitor / unresolved for current restriction.** Military-versus-ordinary jurisdiction conflicts remain a material accountability risk, but current-status review did not establish a sufficiently precise, still-unremediated 2026 obstruction project to preserve `S`.

## 2. Evidence retained in the governance record

Amnesty International's 2025 investigation of 398 jurisdictional conflicts remains significant evidence that Military Criminal Justice claims can delay or divert ordinary investigations into alleged abuses by police or armed-forces personnel. That dataset, however, covers conflicts decided between February 2021 and October 2024 and cannot by itself establish which exact interventions remain active in August 2026.

Official 2026 Constitutional Court decisions confirm that jurisdictional disputes continue to arise. In Auto 262/2026 (`CJU-7386`), concerning the December 2024 death of Juan Sebastián Collantes Betancourth during Army operation order 039 "Damocles", a military criminal court claimed jurisdiction and the Constitutional Court ultimately assigned the case to the ordinary jurisdiction.

Auto 337/2026 (`CJU-7374`) concerns the April 2025 death of an eight-year-old girl after a police incident. A Military Criminal Justice prosecutor sought military jurisdiction; the Constitutional Court found that the procedural conditions for a jurisdictional conflict had not yet been completed and ordered the military criminal judge to state expressly whether it claimed or rejected competence.

## 3. Counter-evidence and current-status limits

The 2026 Court record also shows active constitutional correction rather than a uniformly successful obstruction project. Auto 767/2026 records a military prosecutor declining military jurisdiction before the Constitutional Court assigned the matter to ordinary jurisdiction.

For `CJU-7374`, no sufficiently authoritative later public disposition was found establishing that the military claim remained active and obstructive after the Court's procedural order. ECL does not infer continued obstruction merely from the absence of a later public update.

## 4. Attribution and exclusions

This record does **not** restrict Colombia, the Armed Forces, National Police, Military Criminal Justice, military prosecutors, military judges or ordinary prosecutors/courts generically. Constitutional Court supervision, ordinary-jurisdiction investigation, OPCAT implementation and genuine accountability/remedial work are excluded.

Any future `S` finding must follow a fresh named case or finite intervention whose current material obstruction of an ordinary human-rights investigation is independently established.

## 5. Adversarial determination

**DOWNGRADE `S` → `U`.** The structural governance concern remains well supported, but the present exact-case evidence is corrected, supervised or unresolved in current public status. Freezing a broader Military Criminal Justice class would over-include lawful judicial functions.

## 6. Review / re-escalation triggers

- a fresh named military-jurisdiction intervention that currently diverts or obstructs an ordinary investigation into alleged serious abuse;
- reliable case-level mapping showing a finite subset of the 398-conflict dataset remains materially unremediated;
- retaliation, evidence suppression or other attributable obstruction tied to an objectively knowable unit/case; or
- authoritative findings materially changing the status of a reviewed 2026 conflict.
