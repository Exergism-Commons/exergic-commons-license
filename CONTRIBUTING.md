# Contributing to the Exergic Commons License

ECL is an experimental license project. Contributions are welcome, especially legal criticism, adversarial examples, ambiguity reports, governance proposals, and improvements that make the license more precise and enforceable without abandoning its exergist purpose.

## What to contribute

Useful contributions include:

- identifying ambiguous definitions;
- proposing narrower or more objective legal tests;
- analyzing enforceability across jurisdictions;
- comparing ECL with existing ethical or source-available licenses;
- proposing evidence standards for Restricted Party designations;
- testing the Restricted Project and Covered Associate rules against realistic consortium, contractor, university, government, and commercial scenarios;
- identifying accidental overreach or loopholes;
- improving non-retroactivity and versioning rules; and
- translating stable explanatory documents after the English text is settled.

## Before ECL 1.0

The 0.x series is explicitly unstable. Language may change substantially between drafts.

Do not treat a draft as a professionally reviewed production license unless your own qualified legal counsel has reviewed the exact text for your intended use and jurisdiction.

## Proposed workflow

For substantive changes:

1. Open an issue describing the problem before changing multiple sections at once.
2. Give a concrete example showing why the existing wording succeeds or fails.
3. Distinguish philosophical disagreement from legal or operational ambiguity.
4. Keep changes to the operative `LICENSE` separate from changes to explanatory philosophy where practical.
5. A substantive license change must produce a new version snapshot in `versions/` before being declared stable.

## Restricted Party proposals

A proposal to add or remove a Restricted Party should follow `EXERGIC-GOVERNANCE.md` and should contain evidence, counter-evidence or uncertainty, proposed scope, and the specific exergic criteria relied upon.

Do not use designation proposals for harassment, guilt by association, nationality-based exclusion, or unsupported accusations.

## Contribution rights

By intentionally submitting text, code, examples, or documentation to this repository for inclusion, you represent that you have the right to submit it and grant the repository maintainers and downstream recipients a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive, irrevocable copyright license to reproduce, modify, publish, distribute, sublicense, and incorporate your contribution into current or future versions of ECL and related documentation.

This contribution term is itself a draft and should be reviewed before the project accepts high-value third-party legal drafting or reaches ECL 1.0.

## Style

Prefer:

- defined terms over rhetorical language;
- behavior over ideology;
- material participation over remote association;
- objective triggers where possible;
- explicit knowledge standards;
- versioned schedules;
- non-retroactivity; and
- examples that expose edge cases.

Avoid language such as "evil," "enemy," or similarly undefined moral labels in operative license text. Exergic principles should be converted into legal criteria capable of being applied to facts.

## Code of discussion

Criticism of the license's philosophy is welcome. Personal harassment is not.

A useful challenge is one that helps answer questions such as:

- Can a reasonable user know whether they are licensed?
- Can a restricted organization evade the rule through a contractor?
- Can an innocent third party become unknowingly restricted?
- Can maintainers abuse the designation process?
- Does the wording distinguish incidental dual use from intentional prohibited use?
- Can a designation be removed fairly?

The project should improve by trying to break its own rules before others have to rely on them.
