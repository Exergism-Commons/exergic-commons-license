# Global State Screening under the Exergic Commons License (2026)

> **Status: analytical screening only — no licensing effect.** This document does not add or remove a Restricted Party. Legal effect arises only from the exact `RESTRICTED-PARTIES.md` Schedule incorporated by a software release.

## 1. Scope

This screening covers the **193 UN Member States plus the State of Palestine and the Holy See** (195 state entities). It asks whether the *state apparatus as an institutional actor* appears to warrant ECL restriction or deeper review. It does not classify populations, nationalities, religions, ethnic groups, or private actors by association.

The screening has two conceptual axes:

- **Internal exergy:** meaningful political agency, contestability, expression, association, due process, freedom from arbitrary detention/coercion, surveillance, and ability to exit or challenge state decisions.
- **External exergy:** occupation, unlawful targeting, torture/rendition, transnational repression, systematic coercion abroad, forced assimilation, and state projects that materially destroy the agency of non-citizens.

Freedom House *Freedom in the World 2026* is used only as a **comparative screening signal** for internal rights and civil liberties; it covers events in calendar year 2025. V-Dem v16 (March 2026) is a secondary comparative source for subsequent dossiers. Neither index is itself an ECL legal trigger. Current-2026 events and authoritative primary findings can override a favorable comparative score, as with the United States and Israel in the current draft schedule.

Sources:
- https://freedomhouse.org/country/scores
- https://freedomhouse.org/reports/freedom-world/freedom-world-research-methodology
- https://www.v-dem.net/data/the-v-dem-dataset/

## 2. Screening categories

- **R — Restricted candidate (state-level override):** current ECL evidence already supports assessing the whole state apparatus as the institutional Restricted Party.
- **R\* — Presumptive Restricted candidate:** extremely severe internal agency deficit; requires an individual evidence record before stable licensing effect.
- **S — Scope-restricted:** specific national organs/projects already cross ECL thresholds, but current evidence does not yet justify restricting the whole state.
- **H — High-priority review:** strong Not Free signal; state-level ECL attribution must be established with actor-specific evidence.
- **U — Under review:** material concerns warrant review, but comparative indicators alone are insufficient.
- **N — No current state-level basis:** no present basis in this global screen for restricting the state as a whole. This is not an endorsement; individual projects/agencies remain assessable.
- **P — Split/fragmented:** a unitary state-apparatus classification is not appropriate without separately evaluating competing authorities.

## 4. Interpretation rule

This matrix is deliberately asymmetric between **screening** and **restriction**. A low comparative freedom score can trigger review but cannot, by itself, make a State a Restricted Party. Conversely, a high internal-freedom score does not immunize a State whose external military, intelligence, detention, occupation, or coercive projects independently satisfy ECL criteria.

Before moving any `R*`, `H`, or `U` State into the operative Schedule, the repository should open a state-specific evidence record identifying: exact state organs; attributable conduct; authoritative sources; counter-evidence; whether conduct is systematic or exceptional; external as well as internal effects; scope of controlled entities; and a review/removal condition.

## 5. Recognition-sensitive cases

This 195-state baseline follows the UN membership/observer framework. Separately governed or recognition-contested entities such as Taiwan and Kosovo should receive their own supplemental screening rather than being silently folded into another State's row.
