# ECL Adversarial Review 2026 — Tranche 3

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**

This tranche continues the falsification-first review of whole-State `R` findings. It gives special weight to counter-institutions that produce verifiable legal effects: constitutional invalidation, independent monitoring, criminal investigation, case dismissal, adjudication, or public parliamentary scrutiny. Merely nominal human-rights bodies or unenforced reform promises are not sufficient.

## Results

### #87 Kazakhstan — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:**

- Kazakhstan's new Constitution entered into force on 1 July 2026 and constitutionalizes judicial control of detention, protection against arbitrary detention, defence rights from the moment liberty is restricted, state compensation for unlawful official action, and digital/privacy guarantees.
- The Constitutional Court is not merely formal. In June 2026 it declared part of the Criminal Procedure Code unconstitutional because it allowed detention beyond constitutionally permissible limits; it also invalidated or narrowed other rules affecting labour rights, enforcement of family judgments and municipal restrictions.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- UN Special Procedures in March 2026 raised the detention/prosecution of the former head of independent outlet Orda and the disbarment of her lawyer, allegedly linked to journalism and legal representation.
- A separate March 2026 communication concerned judicial harassment of a religious advocate and denial of registration to an independent religious community.

**Adversarial finding:** the current record supports serious restrictions attributable to political-security, media, religious-registration and prosecutorial systems, but a whole-State `R` no longer accurately describes an apparatus that now includes a Constitutional Court exercising demonstrable rights-protective review against legislation and executive/local rules. **DOWNGRADE to `S`.**

**Proposed scope:** political-security and politically motivated prosecution/detention; media repression; retaliatory professional sanctions; discriminatory religious-registration/enforcement; specific surveillance/censorship projects where evidenced.

### #91 Kyrgyzstan — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Counter-evidence with institutional effect:**

- A constitutional law signed in September 2025 strengthened the Ombudsman/Akyikatchy as a constitutional human-rights institution, expressly aiming at independence and Paris Principles compliance.
- The Ombudsman's powers were extended for states of emergency and martial law.
- Its 2025 reporting publicly identifies state bodies and officials that violated rights; the institution performs law-enforcement monitoring, judicial-proceeding monitoring and inspections. In 2026 it continued prison monitoring and publicly documented violations.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- UN Special Procedures continue to document alleged arbitrary detention and prosecution of peaceful human-rights advocates, arrests and alleged torture of members of a religious minority, and a broader crackdown against independent media.
- A May 2026 UN legal communication also raised concerns about proposed family/health-law amendments affecting privacy, association and equality.

**Adversarial finding:** these abuses remain material, but the existence of a constitutionally strengthened oversight institution that actively names violating state bodies and conducts detention/law-enforcement monitoring makes whole-State attribution too broad. **DOWNGRADE to `S`.**

**Proposed scope:** security/law-enforcement and prosecutorial systems involved in politically motivated detention or torture; media repression; discriminatory religious enforcement; restrictive NGO/civic-control projects.

### #63 Georgia — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:**

- Georgia's Public Defender is actively challenging assembly restrictions before the Constitutional Court and filing amicus submissions in protest-related cases.
- In April 2026 the Constitutional Court granted a Public Defender constitutional suit concerning compulsory psychiatric treatment, showing that the challenge mechanism can produce binding rights-protective outcomes.
- The Council of Europe Commissioner reported in June 2026 that five police officers had been criminally charged over disproportionate force against protesters/journalists, and welcomed a legislative proposal requiring visible police identification numbers.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- The same Council of Europe review found substantial delays in accountability despite 360 ill-treatment reports, mounting legal restrictions on assembly, association, media and civil-society funding, and legal actions against opposition actors.
- The Public Defender itself continues to challenge new restrictions as incompatible with constitutional freedom of expression and assembly.

**Adversarial finding:** a whole-State `R` is overbroad because independent/rights-protective institutional channels are demonstrably functioning, even if under severe pressure. **DOWNGRADE to `S`.**

**Proposed scope:** executive/police protest repression; politically motivated prosecution/detention; coercive foreign-influence/grant controls; specific media/civic-space repression projects. The Public Defender, Constitutional Court and genuinely independent accountability functions must not be treated as part of the restricted coercive project merely by legal incorporation into the State.

### #131 Pakistan — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Counter-evidence / attribution correction:**

- Pakistan is a federal State with a multi-party National Assembly whose Standing Committee on Human Rights remained active in 2026 and publicly criticized failures of government departments, summoning responsible federal bodies and demanding concrete measures. Its membership includes government, opposition and regional parties.
- This does not prove that parliamentary oversight is sufficient, but it is meaningful evidence that the entire federal legal/governmental apparatus cannot safely be collapsed into the military/intelligence/security systems responsible for the strongest ECL allegations.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- UN Special Procedures describe a continuing pattern in Balochistan involving alleged arbitrary arrest, torture/ill-treatment, enforced disappearance, extrajudicial killing, excessive force against peaceful protesters, censorship, counterterrorism misuse and preventive detention.
- Late-2025 communications, with government replies continuing into 2026, also concern reprisals, detention and disappearances connected to the Reko Diq project and new preventive-detention powers.

**Adversarial finding:** the evidence is severe but is much more precisely attributable to military/intelligence, counterterrorism, Balochistan security, preventive-detention, censorship and related coercive systems than to every Pakistani governmental institution. **DOWNGRADE to `S`.**

**Proposed scope:** armed forces/intelligence and security bodies involved in disappearance/torture/extrajudicial conduct; Balochistan counterterrorism and preventive-detention systems; censorship/reprisal projects; military-court or coercive surveillance projects where separately evidenced.

### #164 South Sudan — **DOWNGRADE R → S**

**Counter-evidence with operative effect:**

- The South Sudan Judiciary, Ministry of Justice, state prosecutors and police investigators are participating in mobile justice interventions that produce actual case review, judgments and dispute resolution. In March 2026 UNMISS-supported state-led mobile courts concluded criminal cases, including SGBV cases; in July a state judicial mission reviewed criminal cases and settled civil disputes in remote Duk County.
- A Leer mobile court dismissed a case involving a woman who alleged torture in detention, providing a concrete example of a judicial institution acting against abusive detention rather than merely endorsing it.
- National Prison Service personnel are participating in human-rights training and prison reform initiatives.

**Evidence preserving a scoped restriction:**

- Serious arbitrary detention, political repression, conflict-related civilian harm and security-sector abuse remain attributable to state/security institutions, and formal justice capacity remains weak and geographically uneven.
- The ongoing conflict environment means much violence is also fragmented among non-State and competing armed actors, making whole-State attribution particularly risky.

**Adversarial finding:** the first adjudication collapsed a fragmented and partially reforming State too broadly into its abusive security components. **DOWNGRADE to `S`.**

**Proposed scope:** intelligence/security and detention organs engaged in arbitrary political detention, torture or secret detention; state-aligned conflict operations where unlawful targeting/violence is substantiated; coercive projects that materially suppress political agency. Judiciary/mobile-court/remediation functions are excluded absent independent evidence against them.
