# ECL Adversarial Review 2026

> **Status: PROVISIONAL GOVERNANCE RECORD — NO LICENSING EFFECT BY ITSELF.**

Review cutoff: **2026-08-11**  
Purpose: attempt to falsify, narrow, or remove provisional `R` / `S` findings in `STATE-ADJUDICATION-2026.md` by actively seeking contrary evidence, functioning counter-institutions, remediation, accountability, institutional reform, cessation, and attribution errors.

## 1. Adversarial-review rule

A prior ECL finding is not presumed correct. For each reviewed State, the reviewer must ask:

1. Is the prohibited conduct actually attributable to the designated apparatus?
2. Is it systematic/persistent rather than isolated or rogue conduct?
3. Do courts, auditors, ombuds institutions, prosecutors, legislatures or other counter-institutions materially constrain or remedy the conduct?
4. Has the State investigated, prosecuted, compensated, reformed or ceased the conduct?
5. Would a narrower agency/project designation be materially more accurate?
6. Would the restriction itself suppress an exergic accountability/remediation project?

Possible adversarial outcomes:

- **UPHOLD** — original finding survives.
- **NARROW** — category survives but scope must be reduced.
- **DOWNGRADE** — evidence no longer supports the original tier.
- **REMOVE** — current evidence does not support restriction.

## 2. Architectural finding: accountability/remediation exception required

The first adversarial pass reveals a structural problem in whole-State designations. A State can contain coercive systems and also contain institutions whose function is to investigate, restrain, expose, adjudicate or remedy those systems. Examples found during review include independent or semi-independent courts, auditors, inspectors general, ombuds institutions and criminal investigators.

A blanket State-level restriction can therefore produce an anti-exergic result if it prevents ECL-covered software from being used **exclusively to investigate, challenge, disclose, prosecute, audit, compensate for, or remediate ECL-prohibited conduct**.

Before ECL 1.0, the operative license should consider a tightly drafted **Accountability and Remediation Exception**. Such an exception should not permit ordinary governmental use and should not apply where the software materially benefits the prohibited operation itself.

Indicative rule:

> Notwithstanding a State-level Restricted Party designation, use by an otherwise covered public institution may remain permitted where the Software is used exclusively and materially for independent judicial review, public audit, ombuds oversight, criminal investigation of official abuse, legal defence, human-rights remediation, civilian-harm investigation, whistleblower protection, or equivalent accountability activity, and does not materially support a Restricted Project.

This is a governance recommendation only; it does not modify `LICENSE` yet.

## 3. First high-impact adversarial tranche

### #187 United States — **UPHOLD R, BUT NARROW OPERATIVE EFFECT THROUGH ACCOUNTABILITY EXCEPTION**

**Original:** State apparatus Restricted — United States federal governmental apparatus.

**Contrary evidence / counter-institutions:**

- The U.S. Government Accountability Office continues to investigate ICE detention failures and issue public recommendations. In 2026 DHS/DOD accepted several GAO recommendations concerning Camp East Montana, including contract reform, pre-occupancy controls and lessons-learned review.
- PCLOB's April 2026 Section 702 report states that reforms since 2023 are producing positive privacy and civil-liberties effects, even while Section 702 remains operative.
- DOJ OIG and federal prosecutors continue bringing cases against federal officers for alleged civil-rights violations; in March 2026 a BOP officer was indicted on eight deprivation-of-rights counts.
- DOD has a Civilian Harm Mitigation and Response framework and has implemented at least some external oversight recommendations, although significant deficiencies and open recommendations remain.

**Adversarial finding:** These counter-institutions are material and must be credited. They do **not**, however, rebut the existing cross-institutional record supporting the federal institutional finding: coercive immigration/detention systems, intelligence surveillance, military targeting/civilian-harm failures, Guantánamo detention, historic torture/rendition, and related systems span multiple central organs and persist across time. The `R` finding therefore survives provisionally.

**Required correction before stable Schedule:** the operative effect should not bar genuinely independent judicial/audit/OIG/rights-remediation projects merely because their institution is legally part of the federal State.

### #82 Israel — **UPHOLD R, WITH SAME ACCOUNTABILITY EXCEPTION ISSUE**

**Original:** State apparatus Restricted — governmental/legal apparatus.

**Contrary evidence / counter-institutions:**

- Israel retains judicial review and a State Comptroller/Ombudsman system. In June 2026 the High Court adjudicated petitions concerning State Comptroller war-related audits, allowing several audit reports to proceed while halting others under the judgment.
- The Knesset has advanced a proposed commission of inquiry concerning the events of 7 October and the subsequent war, evidencing at least a formal internal accountability pathway.

**Evidence against removal:**

- OHCHR's January 2026 thematic report describes systemic discriminatory administration in the occupied West Bank and warns of violations of international obligations concerning racial segregation/apartheid.
- UN Special Procedures continued to document detention/torture concerns and in May 2026 challenged the newly adopted death-penalty legislation on right-to-life, discrimination, torture and fair-trial grounds.
- The underlying ICJ 2024 findings on unlawful continued presence in the occupied territory, self-determination and systemic discrimination remain unremedied at the review cutoff.

**Adversarial finding:** Internal review bodies are real counter-evidence but do not displace the cross-institutional and continuing basis of the State-level finding. `R` survives provisionally. As with the U.S., an accountability/remediation exception is necessary to avoid prohibiting software used by genuinely independent review or rights-remediation functions.

### #36 China — **UPHOLD R**

**Original:** State apparatus Restricted — coercive-security, judicial, surveillance and assimilation apparatus.

**Contrary evidence / claimed reform:**

- China released a National Human Rights Action Plan 2026–2030 promising stronger civil/political-rights safeguards and legal protection of human rights.
- A revised Prison Law adopted in 2026 strengthens formal procuratorial supervision and inmate-rights provisions and takes effect in November 2026.
- The Supreme People's Court reported 294 acquittals, 1,235 withdrawn prosecutions, 6,865 state-compensation cases and measures concerning defence counsel rights in its 2026 work reporting.

**Evidence against removal:**

- January 2026 UN Special Procedures material continues to document prolonged national-security detention and allegedly retaliatory solitary confinement of Hong Kong democracy/human-rights defenders.
- The OHCHR Xinjiang assessment and unresolved coercive-assimilation/surveillance findings remain material to ECL attribution.
- The formal reform documents do not establish cessation or effective remediation of the specific systems underlying the ECL finding.

**Adversarial finding:** The reform evidence is meaningful and should be retained for future review, but currently does not rebut the systematic cross-institutional basis of `R`. Review again after implementation evidence exists for the 2026–2030 plan and revised Prison Law.

### #144 Russia — **UPHOLD R**

**Original:** State apparatus Restricted — domestic repression and military/occupation systems.

**Contrary evidence:**

- Russia continues to operate a Constitutional Court issuing rulings, and 2026 federal changes include expansion of free legal-aid eligibility and regulation of restraint use in detention.
- Domestic formal legal-remedy structures therefore exist and cannot be treated as literally nonexistent.

**Evidence against removal:**

- OHCHR reported in March 2026 that Russia systematically imposed discriminatory and coercive measures in occupied Ukrainian territory, including forced transfer/deportation, citizenship coercion, conscription, detention, torture and ill-treatment.
- 2026 UN Special Procedures continued to raise arbitrary-detention/reprisal cases involving defenders inside Russia, while earlier/current UN material documents torture, enforced disappearance and unlawful detention of civilians in occupied Ukraine.

**Adversarial finding:** The formal-remedy evidence does not show effective institutional restraint sufficient to rebut the breadth, severity and persistence of the conduct. `R` survives.

### #186 United Kingdom — **UPHOLD S**

**Original:** Restricted with defined scope — counterterrorism protest enforcement, police surveillance and asylum/migration apparatus.

**Contrary evidence:**

- The Palestine Action proscription was subjected to full judicial review. The Divisional Court initially quashed the decision in February 2026 on ECHR/policy grounds.
- Other 2026 Administrative Court cases continue to grant interim relief against Home Office decisions, demonstrating meaningful judicial contestability.

**Important correction:** The Court of Appeal on 15 June 2026 reversed the Divisional Court and held the Palestine Action proscription lawful. The original adjudication must not imply that the February quashing remained the final position.

**Adversarial finding:** Judicial contestability is strong counter-evidence against whole-State restriction and supports the existing `S` classification. It does not require removal of scoped review of specific surveillance, counterterrorism-enforcement or migration systems. `S` survives.

### #116 Morocco — **UPHOLD S**

**Original:** Restricted with defined scope — police, intelligence, prosecutorial, judicial and digital-surveillance apparatus against peaceful dissent, including Western Sahara.

**Contrary evidence / reforms:**

- Morocco is pursuing criminal-procedure reforms framed around fair-trial guarantees and rights protections.
- The Justice Ministry and National Human Rights Council expanded victim-protection cooperation in 2026, including anti-trafficking coordination.

**Evidence against removal:**

**Adversarial finding:** General justice reform and pardons are meaningful counter-evidence but do not specifically remediate the conduct underlying the Western Sahara/dissent designation. The whole-State finding remains unwarranted; the current scoped `S` survives.

### #184 Ukraine — **NARROW S**

**Original:** Restricted with defined scope — territorial recruitment, military-conscription, collaboration-prosecution and selected security apparatus.

**Contrary evidence / accountability:**

- Ukraine's State Bureau of Investigation is actively opening criminal cases and serving notices of suspicion against TCC/recruitment officials and police officers for violence, unlawful confinement, torture and registry manipulation.
- In June/July 2026 the SBI publicly reported investigations of deaths after TCC custody and criminal proceedings concerning coercive treatment.
- The Ukrainian Ombudsman/National Preventive Mechanism continues facility visits, formal submissions and referrals leading to criminal proceedings.

**Adversarial finding:** This counter-evidence is strong enough that the original broad phrase `territorial recruitment, military-conscription` is overinclusive. Current evidence supports `S` only for **identified abusive TCC units, personnel and coercive mobilization/detention practices where material violations are substantiated**, plus separately evidenced security/collaboration-prosecution projects. Ordinary lawful recruitment/conscription administration should not be treated as Restricted merely by category.

**Outcome:** `S` survives but must be **narrowed materially**.
